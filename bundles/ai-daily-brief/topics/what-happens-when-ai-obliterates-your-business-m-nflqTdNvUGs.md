# Overview

Whittemore uses the viral Tailwind CSS story to examine what happens when AI destroys a business model without destroying demand for the product. Tailwind's open-source CSS framework is more popular than ever — heavily used by AI coding tools at 75 million downloads a month — yet CEO Adam Wathan revealed on GitHub that 75% of the engineering team had just been laid off: documentation traffic was down 40% versus 2023 because AI answers questions directly, and since the docs were the only funnel to the paid Plus tier, revenue fell close to 80% even as usage grew. Whittemore pairs this with Stack Overflow's effective death — monthly queries down to 6,866, roughly its 2008 first-month level, from a 2020 peak of 300,000 — as "death by LLM."[^what-happens-when-ai-obliterates-your-business-m-nflqTdNvUGs]

His framing device: watching what developers are struggling with is "as close to living in the future as you can get," because he predicts other knowledge-work sectors will be "vibified" in 2026 the way development was in 2025 — meaning these business-model shocks are a preview of what is coming for information businesses generally.[^what-happens-when-ai-obliterates-your-business-m-nflqTdNvUGs]

# Key points

- Tailwind CEO Adam Wathan, replying to a GitHub request for AI-friendly text-only docs, disclosed that "75% of the people on our engineering team lost their jobs here yesterday because of the brutal impact AI has had on our business."[^what-happens-when-ai-obliterates-your-business-m-nflqTdNvUGs]
- The catch-22: AI adoption drove more Tailwind usage but eliminated doc visits (down 40% from 2023) that marketed the commercial products — revenue down ~80% while the framework was bigger than ever.[^what-happens-when-ai-obliterates-your-business-m-nflqTdNvUGs]
- Commentators drew broad conclusions: Santiago warned of "a massive graveyard of abandonware"; Nick Papa George called Tailwind "a canary" since AI can work with grittier open-source versions of anything; Valentine argued OSS maintainers are "extra screwed" because AI scrapes project sites so users never see commercial offerings.[^what-happens-when-ai-obliterates-your-business-m-nflqTdNvUGs]
- After Wathan's podcast recorded on a morning walk hit nearly 2 million views, sponsors flooded in: Google AI Studio (via Logan Kilpatrick), Lovable (CEO Antoine Osika: "Every app built on Lovable uses Tailwind"), Supabase, Vercel (Guillermo Rauch: "Tailwind is foundational web infrastructure"), Gumroad, Cursor, and more.[^what-happens-when-ai-obliterates-your-business-m-nflqTdNvUGs]
- Stack Overflow registered 6,866 queries last month — near its 2008 launch level, down from 300,000/month at the 2020 peak and 150-200,000 sustained for nearly a decade; Elon Musk had called its plight "death by LLM" back in July 2023.[^what-happens-when-ai-obliterates-your-business-m-nflqTdNvUGs]
- A second-order worry: if the culture of building open repositories of human expertise dies, there will be no source of high-quality, well-structured data to train future AI on.[^what-happens-when-ai-obliterates-your-business-m-nflqTdNvUGs]
- Skeptics like Daniel Jeffries countered that "AI didn't kill Tailwind, AI exposed a fragile business model" — hidden product pages, one-time purchases, donation reliance, no recurring revenue — noting MongoDB (Atlas), Elastic (hosting), and GitLab (enterprise tiers) solved open-source monetization.[^what-happens-when-ai-obliterates-your-business-m-nflqTdNvUGs]
- Akash Gupta's prescription: build what AI can't replace — services, consulting, enterprise contracts, deep integrations requiring human judgment — warning that "if your value is answering questions that AI can now answer, your moat just vanished."[^what-happens-when-ai-obliterates-your-business-m-nflqTdNvUGs]
- Proposed fixes for the public-goods problem: Balaji Srinivasan suggested a big AI company acquire or strategically invest in Tailwind; Nat Elias proposed Claude Code let users pay 1% extra to auto-fund the open-source projects they use, proportional to token spend.[^what-happens-when-ai-obliterates-your-business-m-nflqTdNvUGs]

# Takeaways

Whittemore concludes that Tailwind now functions almost as a public good — the corporate rescue wave supports that reading — and the open question is whether a new conception of funding digital infrastructure emerges, whether via patrons, token-spend contributions, or something else. More broadly, he holds that what is hitting developer-adjacent information businesses now will have "similar seismic effects" across other sectors in the months and years to come, so watching developers today is the best available preview of everyone else's future.[^what-happens-when-ai-obliterates-your-business-m-nflqTdNvUGs]
