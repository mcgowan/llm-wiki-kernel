# Overview

With Anthropic officially filing confidential IPO paperwork with the SEC and OpenAI's filing reportedly imminent, Nathaniel Whittemore explores the question of how to cut the public into the spoils of AI. He dismisses the financial press's framing of an IPO "race" between the two labs — arguing that just as every token they produce gets bought, every share they offer will be bought, and the market won't pick a winner based on filing order. The IPO moment collides with a rising policy discourse: Bernie Sanders' New York Times op-ed proposing an "AI Sovereign Wealth Fund Act" funded by a one-time 50% tax paid in stock of OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, and other foundation labs, with government voting shares, 50% board control, and Alaska-oil-fund-style citizen dividends.[^with-ai-ipos-on-the-way-should-the-public-own-ai-L2ZJ5HITV48]

Whittemore notes the idea of AI-derived public wealth isn't fringe — OpenAI's April industrial-policy white paper called for a public wealth fund giving every citizen a stake, and Anthropic floated sovereign-wealth positions last October — but he prefers the trajectory of Ezra Klein's framing: distributing AI itself as a public good, defining the public problems AI should solve. Headlines cover Nvidia's RTX Spark prosumer chip (a potential "M1 moment for Windows"), Vera Rubin entering full production, Meta's AI pendant plans and its AI-support account-hijacking exploit, Bain's warning on missing AI ROI, and Walmart ending unlimited tokens for its Code Puppy agent.[^with-ai-ipos-on-the-way-should-the-public-own-ai-L2ZJ5HITV48]

- PitchBook's contrarian read: Anthropic "volunteered to absorb all the disclosure risk first," giving OpenAI a free option to watch investors react to audited AI financials; Dan Ives called the filing "an opening of the floodgate for the IPO market."[^with-ai-ipos-on-the-way-should-the-public-own-ai-L2ZJ5HITV48]
- Google plans to raise $80B in equity — its first new stock issuance in over two decades — to help fund $190B in 2026 spend, marking the hyperscaler funding progression from cash to debt to equity dilution; Berkshire Hathaway took a $10B allocation, making Google a top-five holding (~$32B) in new CEO Greg Abel's largest bet.[^with-ai-ipos-on-the-way-should-the-public-own-ai-L2ZJ5HITV48]
- The S&P 500 rose 16% in two months (fifth-strongest such run since the 1950s), driven by a US semiconductor index up 69% in the quarter's first two months — reviving bubble questions countered by structural supply-shortage arguments.[^with-ai-ipos-on-the-way-should-the-public-own-ai-L2ZJ5HITV48]
- Sanders' proposal: a one-time 50% tax paid in stock, creating a sovereign wealth fund with citizen dividends and later funding healthcare, education, and housing, on the argument that AI is "built on the collective knowledge of humanity" and creative work was "stolen"; Whittemore believes 50% is Sanders' genuine number, not a negotiating position, and that public discontent means "all bets are off on what the public will tolerate."[^with-ai-ipos-on-the-way-should-the-public-own-ai-L2ZJ5HITV48]
- Labs themselves have floated versions: OpenAI's white paper proposed a public wealth fund for every citizen; Anthropic suggested sovereign wealth funds could spread AI-derived wealth if the sector captures outsize gains.[^with-ai-ipos-on-the-way-should-the-public-own-ai-L2ZJ5HITV48]
- Ezra Klein's alternative: distribute AI itself as a public good — "we need to define the public goods that AI can serve and create the conditions under which AI can be useful."[^with-ai-ipos-on-the-way-should-the-public-own-ai-L2ZJ5HITV48]
- Nvidia's RTX Spark, unveiled at GTC Taipei: a prosumer standalone CPU with 20 cores, 6,000+ integrated GPU cores, up to 128GB unified memory, one petaflop of AI compute, shipping in Windows PCs from Asus, Dell, HP, Lenovo, and Microsoft by fall — aimed at Apple's M-series for local inference; Vera Rubin entered full production with OpenAI and Anthropic taking first units, its Vera CPU purpose-built for agentic AI.[^with-ai-ipos-on-the-way-should-the-public-own-ai-L2ZJ5HITV48]

- Bain & Company found almost 40% of large companies report AI cost savings below 10% against 11–20% targets, and 44% were funding their next AI investments from assumed savings — "a circular bet with a structural leak"; top blockers were data access/integration (41%), compliance, priorities, and skills gaps.[^with-ai-ipos-on-the-way-should-the-public-own-ai-L2ZJ5HITV48]

# Takeaways

Whittemore concludes we are in uncharted territory and the discussion "is going to do nothing but get weirder before it lands." He isn't keen on the specifics of any proposal so far, but likes the trajectory of Klein's questions — what positive benefits do we actually want from AI, and how do we achieve them — and observes that even Sanders' "extremely socialistic" plan is provoking interesting counter-proposals that preserve broad public financial upside in AI without putting the government in charge of running the world's most important companies. He sees the simultaneity of IPO mania and public-ownership discourse as no accident: the financial stakes of AI have become the policy stakes.[^with-ai-ipos-on-the-way-should-the-public-own-ai-L2ZJ5HITV48]
