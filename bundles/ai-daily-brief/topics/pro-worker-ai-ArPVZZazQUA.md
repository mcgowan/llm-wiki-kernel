# Overview

Against a backdrop of AI-credited layoffs — Atlassian cutting roughly 10% of its workforce (1,600 jobs) with CEO Mike Cannon-Brookes explicitly citing AI, plus unconfirmed Oracle rumors — Whittemore asks whether "pro-worker AI" is a contradiction in terms or an untapped opportunity. He first airs Buccocapital's contrarian macro take that "AI-driven layoffs" are largely air cover for reset valuations and COVID-era overstaffing, then covers Anthropic's "Labor Market Impacts of AI" research, which introduces "observed exposure" and finds huge theoretical AI coverage of management, business, and finance work — the capabilities overhang in empirical form — though no detectable unemployment effect yet.[^pro-worker-ai-ArPVZZazQUA]

The bulk of the episode surveys emerging high-level answers: former Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo's New York Times op-ed proposing a public-private "grand bargain" on workforce transition; a Washington Post editorial citing an ECB study of 5,000 Eurozone firms finding AI-intensive companies are about 4% more likely to hire; and the MIT paper "Building Pro-Worker Artificial Intelligence" by Daron Acemoglu, David Autor, and Simon Johnson, whose five-category taxonomy argues AI's collaborative potential is real but currently underutilized because market incentives favor automation.[^pro-worker-ai-ArPVZZazQUA]

# Key points

- Buccocapital's argument: Atlassian and HubSpot have near-zero free cash flow, stocks down 60-70% from highs, and bloated COVID-era cost structures — "layoffs are coming, they will be credited to AI, and that will be cover for the real problem."[^pro-worker-ai-ArPVZZazQUA]
- Anthropic's observed-exposure measure weighs automating (versus augmenting) usage more heavily; some categories show 90%+ theoretical exposure, and the canary in the coal mine — hiring of young workers into exposed jobs — appears to be slowing.[^pro-worker-ai-ArPVZZazQUA]
- Raimondo proposes a modern transition system: employers provide real-time AI-powered insights on hiring and skill needs; education shifts to short, affordable, stackable job-linked credentials (a mid-career accountant needs a four-month credential plus temporary wage insurance, not another master's); modernized apprenticeships; and tax incentives rewarding retention, penalizing layoffs, and encouraging reinvestment of AI savings into job creation.[^pro-worker-ai-ArPVZZazQUA]
- Whittemore links reinvestment incentives to his "efficiency AI versus opportunity AI" heuristic: incentives to reinvest savings push employers past doing-the-same-with-less toward expansion — and expanding organizations win in the long run.[^pro-worker-ai-ArPVZZazQUA]
- The ECB study of 5,000 Eurozone firms found AI-embracing businesses are ~4% more likely to add staff — "AI-intensive firms tend on average to hire rather than fire"; meanwhile 63% of Americans told YouGov AI will decrease jobs versus 7% expecting an increase, more pessimistic than China (~40% worried).[^pro-worker-ai-ArPVZZazQUA]
- The MIT taxonomy: labor-augmenting, capital-augmenting, automation, new-task-creating, and expertise-leveling technologies — only new-task-creating is unambiguously pro-worker; expertise-leveling is ambiguous because incumbents' expertise gets devalued while new entrants benefit.[^pro-worker-ai-ArPVZZazQUA]
- Field examples of pro-worker AI: an electrician's assistant matching photos and diagnostics to a prior-problem database halved maintenance-report time, with the worker collaboratively in the loop.[^pro-worker-ai-ArPVZZazQUA]
- Why pro-worker AI is underbuilt: misaligned firm incentives (automation to reduce dependence on unionized labor, rent extraction), the "AGI bet" (why enhance workers who'll be replaced?), developer incentives following firm demand, time-horizon problems, and worker resistance.[^pro-worker-ai-ArPVZZazQUA]
- The paper's historical rebuttal to automation doom: labor's share of income rose during the first eight decades of the 20th century, and rich, heavily automated countries have higher labor shares than poor ones — new task creation counterbalances automation.[^pro-worker-ai-ArPVZZazQUA]

# Takeaways

Whittemore predicts the new dominant role: take any knowledge-worker job, "make it have a baby with a software engineer" — agent builders and agent orchestrators in every flavor of the knowledge-worker rainbow, with AI engineer variants potentially becoming the dominant job category. He criticizes traditional media incentives toward relentless pessimism (the Times titling Raimondo's proactive piece "America cannot withstand the economic shock that's coming"), and argues that with a little work, more positive, evidence-backed thinking about the AI labor future can be found in many — sometimes unexpected — places.[^pro-worker-ai-ArPVZZazQUA]
