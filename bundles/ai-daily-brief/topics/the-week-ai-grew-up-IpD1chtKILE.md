# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore introduces an experimental weekly format that identifies the meta-theme connecting a week's stories rather than recapping them, and argues this week's theme was AI growing up — moving out of a startup-flavored era into one where AI is treated as critical global economic infrastructure. He traces the maturation across four dimensions: token demand outstripping supply and forcing business model change, AI showing up decisively on Big Tech's bottom line, government asserting control over model deployment, and rapid innovation in the harnesses through which people actually use models.[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]

He explicitly declines to fold in Jasmine Sun's New York Times essay on Silicon Valley bracing for a permanent underclass, arguing that the skills needed to build technology differ from those needed to understand how it meets the world, and that many AI builders extrapolate their own transformed experience onto everyone else.[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]

# Key points

- Demand indicators: GPU rental prices up 40% over six months per Oguz Erken, the top two labs generating nearly $60 billion in aggregate annual revenue, Dylan Patel arguing even tier-two and tier-three labs will be sold out of tokens, Andy Jassy describing Trainium demand exceeding supply, and OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar calling it "a vertical wall of demand" with compute as the bottleneck.[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]
- GitHub moved Copilot to usage-based billing, with chief product officer Mario Rodriguez saying the premium request model is no longer sustainable when a quick chat costs the same as a multi-hour autonomous session; Satya Nadella said any per-user Microsoft business — productivity, coding, security — will become per-user-plus-usage.[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]
- Whittemore notes even hardware is sold out: Mac minis are unavailable from Apple for at least several months, discussed by Tim Cook on the earnings call.[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]
- Earnings: AWS up 28% year-over-year (best since 2021), Azure up 40%, and Google Cloud up 63%, driving the second-largest one-day market cap jump in history and putting Google in reach of Nvidia for most valuable company — one of Whittemore's 2026 predictions.[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]
- Bloomberg reported Anthropic in talks to raise at more than $900 billion, above OpenAI's $825 billion March valuation, with TechCrunch reporting investors expected a $50 billion raise and some Anthropic secondary shares trading as high as a trillion-dollar valuation.[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]
- Microsoft and OpenAI restructured their deal: Microsoft keeps free non-revenue-share model access for another five years and the AGI clause is removed, while OpenAI gains freedom to sell through AWS and Google Cloud.[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]
- The White House explored unwinding Anthropic's supply chain risk designation and deploying Mythos in agencies, but by week's end officials opposed broader Mythos rollout on national security grounds, worried Anthropic lacked compute to serve both government and the roughly 70 preview companies; Anthropic disputes the compute constraint.[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]
- Dean Ball characterized the government restricting a model's release as an informal, improvised licensing regime, writing that "the training wheels have come off on AI policy."[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]
- Product-side maturation: the new Cursor SDK for embedding agents, plus a Codex update for non-developer work that asks users their role (finance, product, marketing, operations, sales, data science, design, student) and tailors task suggestions — a one-interface-for-everyone bet contrasting with Anthropic's split between Claude Code and Claude Co-work.[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]
- The week's quirky story: OpenAI published "Where the Goblins Came From" after its Codex system prompt was found instructing the model never to mention goblins, gremlins, raccoons, trolls, ogres, or pigeons. OpenAI traced the habit to personality reinforcement learning, where Codex scored creature references highly for a "nerdy personality" starting in GPT-5.1 and spiking in 5.4, then spilling into other RL training.[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]

# Takeaways

Whittemore argues that in a world where token demand vastly exceeds supply, flat seat-based pricing that subsidizes heavy users cannot survive — and while he acknowledges this reduces room for the experimentation he considers vital, he says ultimately we want business models that do not drive companies into the ground. He expects Google to capitalize as the subsidy era ends, since it has the best mature set of cheaper models for enterprises reluctant to adopt Chinese open-weight alternatives. On the private-market surge he says the logic is not the right revenue multiple but a belief that roughly half a dozen companies are writing the future. On the Codex-versus-Co-work interface bet he sides with Codex, saying his experience is that people are not waiting for simplified tools but instead using AI as tutor and build partner to do technical things they never could before. He compares the current harness moment to the shift from hobbyist PCs to the Apple II Plus era. His build suggestions: revisit Codex (pointing to Riley Brown's 28-minute overview) and explore Cursor as a flexible harness.[^the-week-ai-grew-up-IpD1chtKILE]
