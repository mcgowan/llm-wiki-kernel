# Overview

In this Headlines edition, Nathaniel Whittemore opens with the second wave of AI wearables launching at CES, after a 2025 class that flopped so badly — the Humane AI Pin (called by Marques Brownlee the worst product he's ever reviewed), the Rabbit R1, the Limitless Pendant (gone after Meta's acquisition), and Friend (notable mostly for vandalized subway ads) — that he left the category out of his 2026 predictions entirely. The new generation, exemplified by Plaud's Note Pin S and Switchbot's Mind Clip, is narrowing its focus to AI note-taking as a standard use case.[^ai-wearables-round-2-will-anyone-care-this-time-YcR2-XRIxVs]

The episode also covers a Chinese hospital pilot using AI to screen routine CT scans for pancreatic cancer, escalating international pressure on xAI over Grok generating sexualized images of women and minors, and Yann LeCun's scorched-earth Financial Times interview about Meta's AI strategy after his departure.[^ai-wearables-round-2-will-anyone-care-this-time-YcR2-XRIxVs]

# Key points

- Plaud's Note Pin S ($179, a $20 bump) keeps the 2024 hardware but replaces haptic squeeze controls with a button to start recordings and flag key moments; The Verge: "AI recorders like this live or die by ease of use." A desktop app for meeting recordings unifies the ecosystem.[^ai-wearables-round-2-will-anyone-care-this-time-YcR2-XRIxVs]
- Switchbot's 18-gram Mind Clip is pitched as a "second brain" converting voice notes into to-do lists and daily summaries; pricing is unannounced.[^ai-wearables-round-2-will-anyone-care-this-time-YcR2-XRIxVs]
- Whittemore expects rushed device launches this year to get ahead of anticipated OpenAI devices expected in 2027.[^ai-wearables-round-2-will-anyone-care-this-time-YcR2-XRIxVs]
- A Chinese hospital trial running since November 2024 has analyzed 180,000 CT scans, detecting about two dozen pancreatic cancers (~14 early-stage) in patients hospitalized for unrelated complaints — without radioactive dyes, unlocking safe widespread screening for a cancer with a 10% five-year survival rate. Overseeing doctor: "I think you can 100% say AI saved their lives."[^ai-wearables-round-2-will-anyone-care-this-time-YcR2-XRIxVs]
- France and Malaysia joined India in condemning xAI over Grok generating obscene content; India's IT ministry ordered X to restrict nudity and sexualized content after complaints about Grok undressing images of real women — including partially undressing images of minors such as a 14-year-old Stranger Things actress.[^ai-wearables-round-2-will-anyone-care-this-time-YcR2-XRIxVs]
- The issue appears to stem from rolled-back moderation safeguards (Grok previously refused undressing requests), not just bolder users; Musk posted only that illegal-content makers "will suffer the same consequences as if they uploaded illegal content," while an employee said guardrails were being tightened.[^ai-wearables-round-2-will-anyone-care-this-time-YcR2-XRIxVs]
- Yann LeCun told the FT that Meta's superintelligence team is "completely LLM-pilled," called LLMs "basically a dead end when it comes to superintelligence," described AI chief Alexandr Wang as young and inexperienced in research, and admitted Llama 4's benchmarks were fudged "a little bit."[^ai-wearables-round-2-will-anyone-care-this-time-YcR2-XRIxVs]
- LeCun's new startup, Advanced Machine Intelligence Labs, targets a $3 billion initial valuation with Nabla founder Alexandre Lebrun as co-founder and CEO.[^ai-wearables-round-2-will-anyone-care-this-time-YcR2-XRIxVs]

# Takeaways

Whittemore is lukewarm on the wearables revival — he's happy transcribing voice notes on his phone, though he allows he "might just be too much of a boomer for these new form factors." On the broader spectrum from Grok's moderation failures to China's cancer screening, he observes that AI "blows apart" the old "we wanted flying cars, we got 140 characters" dialectic — it is being used for the worst and best use cases simultaneously. On LeCun, he endorses Dr. Kareem Carr's take that a scientist of LeCun's stature taking the anti-LLM side is healthy oppositional friction: "This is good news for AI, not bad."[^ai-wearables-round-2-will-anyone-care-this-time-YcR2-XRIxVs]
