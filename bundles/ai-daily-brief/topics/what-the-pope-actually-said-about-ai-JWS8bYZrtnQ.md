# Overview

The main episode analyzes Magnifica Humanitatis, the first encyclical of Pope Leo XIV — the first American pope, elected May 2025, who named himself after Leo XIII (of the 1891 industrial-labor encyclical Rerum Novarum) and made AI the central social question of his papacy. Subtitled "on safeguarding the human person in the time of artificial intelligence," the document, Whittemore argues, is neither doomer nor cheerleader: it positions society "at the beginning of the beginning," offering principles for discernment rather than blanket rejection, with its core plea being to keep real living humans — not AIs, not anonymous market forces — as the barometer of success with the technology. He surveys the polarized reactions, from Timnit Gebru attacking Anthropic's presence at the ceremony to Dean Ball's complaint that the encyclical's "axiomatic denial of AI cognition" is "a punt of the highest order."[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]

The headlines cover Anthropic's Project Glasswing update on the limited Mythos rollout, a secret $9 billion White House-approved budget for intelligence-agency inference compute, DeepSeek making its 75% discount permanent ahead of a record $10B round, and xAI's Grok V9 medium training run.[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]

# Key points

- Project Glasswing (Mythos with ~50 partners) has identified more than 10,000 high/critical-severity software vulnerabilities with under a 10% false-positive rate; Mozilla fixed 271 vulnerabilities (10x its Opus 4.6 rate), Palo Alto Networks shipped 5x the patches, and one finance firm stopped a $1.5M fraudulent wire transfer.[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]
- The bottleneck has shifted from finding bugs to patching them — maintainers asked Anthropic to slow disclosures; Box CEO Aaron Levie predicted "a security engineer boom. Jevons paradox all over again." Governments in Europe, the UK, Canada, Australia, and Asia are lobbying for Mythos access; Anthropic says no company has safeguards strong enough for a public Mythos-class release.[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]
- The White House approved a secret $9B budget for the CIA and NSA to build an inference cluster of Nvidia Blackwell chips, per the NYT; Chief of Staff Susie Wiles approved NSA use of Anthropic despite the Pentagon labeling it a supply-chain risk — intelligence agencies, barred from domestic collection anyway, accept Anthropic's bans on autonomous weapons and domestic surveillance that the Pentagon rejected.[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]
- DeepSeek made its V4 discount permanent — roughly Opus 4.5-equivalent performance at $0.44/$0.87 per million input/output tokens — and is raising $10B at a $45B valuation (a Chinese venture record) with the state AI fund, Tencent, and JD.com participating; founder Liang Wenfeng says the focus stays on open-source research toward AGI.[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]
- xAI (now SpaceX AI) finished training Grok V9 medium (1.5T parameters, with Cursor data added), a major upgrade over the 0.5T V8 small, 2-3 weeks from release; Grok Build access expanded to SuperGrok and X Premium tiers to positive early reviews.[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]
- Encyclicals interpret existing Catholic teaching for new circumstances; a pope's first is read as signaling the pontificate's themes — Leo XIV explicitly framed AI as "another industrial revolution" posing challenges to "human dignity, justice, and labor."[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]
- The most-quoted line — "the pursuit of greater profits cannot justify choices that systematically sacrifice jobs, because the human person is an end, not a means" — drew fire from entrepreneurs like Blake Scholl and cherry-picked endorsement from figures like Senator Chris Murphy and Karen Hao.[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]
- Paragraph 99 denies AI equivalence with human intelligence: AIs "do not undergo experiences, do not possess a body, do not feel joy or pain... their way of learning is a form of statistical adaptation... which does not imply inner growth." Whittemore notes this is uncontroversial for most people, including daily AI users.[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]
- Paragraph 118 argues limits — illness, old age, vulnerability — are realities through which humanity matures, not defects to fix: "humanity flourishes not despite limitations, but often through them" — which Whittemore reads as the foundation of a fundamentally contra-transhumanist argument.[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]
- Catholic commentators had the best read, per Whittemore: "The Pope is an AI realist. He knows its growth is inevitable. He just wants to ensure it's always in service of the human person" (Christopher Hale); paragraph 178 warns that control of peoples' health data confers "structural leverage over the future" — a warning about tomorrow, not an indictment of today.[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's central reading: the encyclical's underpinning argument is that human value cannot be reduced to an intelligence benchmark — even when AI becomes "smarter" than us, it remains categorically different — and the document is a flag-planting in preparation for the period when that debate heats up. Echoing Miles Brundage, his main advice is to actually read it rather than consume takes, and echoing Boyan Tanguay, he holds that the most important thing about Magnifica Humanitatis is that it exists: one of the world's largest institutions treating AI with urgency and centrality, a model he hopes governments and professional bodies follow. He also notes the discourse around it was markedly more elevated than the usual AI "mudfest."[^what-the-pope-actually-said-about-ai-JWS8bYZrtnQ]
