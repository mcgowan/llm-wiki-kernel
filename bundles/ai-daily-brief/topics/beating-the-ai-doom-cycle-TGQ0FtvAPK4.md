# Overview

In this extended single-topic episode, Nathaniel Whittemore introduces his concept of the "AI doom cycle" — an emotional and cognitive analogue to Gartner's technology hype cycle, describing people's relationship with AI rather than the technology itself. His five stages: skepticism and disbelief; "AI psychosis" (peak conviction that AI changes everything, used non-pejoratively); doom desperation (extrapolating current workforce shifts into joblessness); real-world recalibration (checking narratives against what's actually happening); and "enlightened excitement" — a portmanteau of anxiety and excitement that he presents as the healthiest place from which to engage AI's big questions.[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]

He maps the weekend's stories onto the cycle: Citadel CEO Ken Griffin's conversion from "it's all garbage" skeptic to declaring "for the first time, AI is real" — and going home depressed about high-skill job automation; Deedy Das's viral post on San Francisco's frenetic wealth divide and malaise; graduates booing Eric Schmidt and other commencement speakers over AI; Meta's 8,000-person layoffs; and the labs' shift to usage-based token pricing as compute scarcity forces an ROI mindset. Whittemore argues the recalibration phase — where AI turns out to be expensive, capital-intensive, and hard to integrate — opens space for nuanced discourse and real policy debate.[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]

# Key points

- The doom cycle's five stages: skepticism/disbelief, AI psychosis, doom desperation, real-world recalibration, enlightened excitement ("excitement" being GPT-4's suggested portmanteau of anxiety and excitement).[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]
- Skepticism is waning; NLW attributes what remains to non-updated priors or AI skepticism as a business model, recalling how DeepSeek's free R1 reasoning model in early 2025 converted many by giving them their first reasoning-model experience.[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]
- Ken Griffin, who at Davos in January called AI hype-driven and its stock reports "all garbage," told Stanford Business School that work done by finance master's and PhDs over weeks is now done by AI agents in hours, with Citadel seeing 15-25% productivity gains — and that the automation of "extraordinarily high-skilled jobs" left him "fairly depressed."[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]
- Doom desperation is amplified by AI executives themselves: Fortune resurfaced Mustafa Suleyman's "18 months for all white-collar work" claim, and the WSJ resurfaced Dario Amodei's predictions of 10% overall and 50% entry-level white-collar unemployment — with critics noting such narratives help Anthropic "raise $30 billion at $900 billion post."[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]
- Deedy Das's ~11-million-view post described SF's worst-ever outcome divide: ~10,000 people at Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, Nvidia, and Meta with $20M+ retirement wealth, everyone else in malaise, middle managers paralyzed, and even the rich purposeless; Bucco Capital's response: "commit to never living your life this way."[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]
- Eric Schmidt was booed at the University of Arizona commencement; days earlier a non-tech speaker was booed at UCF for calling AI "the next industrial revolution." Alex Kantrowitz: "Maybe telling college students AI was taking their job wasn't the best strategy." Trevor Garcia: graduates are "anti-being told to be excited about something that feels like it was built for everyone except them."[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]
- Recalibration cuts both ways: Meta laid off ~10% of staff (~8,000 people) with morale at new lows, partly over screen-tracking software reportedly used for AI training — "American employees being used to train the AI models that will replace them."[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]
- The token-maxing era is colliding with structural compute shortage: Anthropic moved enterprise Claude Code customers from $200 flat-rate to $20/seat plus usage billing, GitHub went token-based, and Copilot users shared estimates like $451 current vs $11,432 usage-based — revealing massive subsidies. Axios: "AI can cost more than human workers now," which slows theoretical displacement.[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]
- Enlightened excitement enables nuance: post-ZIRP hiring context for software jobs, a generational data-center construction boom creating near-term blue-collar jobs, Alex Emos's "What Will Be Scarce" essay on the rising relational sector (picked up by Ezra Klein), and labs launching consulting efforts — Paul Bloom joking that Anthropic is "weeks away from AGI, which is why they are... certifying 30,000 PwC professionals on Claude."[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]
- Messaging is shifting: Jensen Huang's Carnegie Mellon commencement (called "the most anxiety-relieving speech in the AI era") stressed that doom is not destiny and re-industrialization creates jobs; Sam Altman pivoted in May to "tools to augment and elevate people, not entities to replace them."[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]
- Better policy conversations become possible: Matthew Yglesias's (possibly tongue-in-cheek) idea of requiring 20% of data-center GPUs as affordable compute for low-income agents, and Mark Cuban's proposal for a sub-50-cent-per-million federal token tax to drive optimization, cut energy use, and raise ~$10B/year.[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]

# Takeaways

Whittemore concludes that the vast majority of the world still lives in the first hump of the doom cycle — somewhere between skepticism, AI psychosis, and doom desperation — and that the faster more people move into real-world recalibration and enlightened excitement, the better off everyone will be. His core argument: if AI inexorably takes all jobs, the only policy discussion is "UBI or die"; but if disruption is specific, discrete, and slower than the doom prophecies claim — as the real world's compute costs, institutional inertia, and integration challenges suggest — then there is room for genuinely useful debate about how to shape outcomes rather than assuming they're already written.[^beating-the-ai-doom-cycle-TGQ0FtvAPK4]
