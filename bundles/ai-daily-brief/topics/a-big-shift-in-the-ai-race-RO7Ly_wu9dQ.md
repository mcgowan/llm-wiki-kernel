# Overview

Five days into the US government's forced shutdown of Anthropic's Mythos and Fable 5, Whittemore reports negotiations stalled: Anthropic sent a technical delegation to Washington (Chief Compute Officer Tom Brown, external affairs head Tara Heck, red-teaming head Logan Graham, and security researcher Nicholas Carlini — profiled by the Wall Street Journal as the hacker whose Mythos-powered discovery of bugs in Ghost and Linux convinced him "these current models are better vulnerability researchers than I am"), but none of the decision's architects attended. The government appears willing to restore Fable if the jailbreak is patched — which experts like Helen Toner say may be impossible — while Mythos is the deeper problem, after Anthropic expanded Project Glasswing by ~50 firms including a South Korean telecom suspected of Chinese-government ties and took days to tell Washington who the recipients were.[^a-big-shift-in-the-ai-race-RO7Ly_wu9dQ]

The main episode charts a realignment in the AI race driven by the economics of mature agentic AI: SpaceX's IPO closed up 49% at $201.80, valuing it at $2.6 trillion — fifth largest company in the world, ahead of Amazon — making Musk the world's first trillionaire; SpaceX completed its $60 billion acquisition of Cursor (a $4 billion run rate, growing 7x year-over-year); and Ed Zitron leaked OpenAI's audited financials, which on inspection reveal a profitable core inference business beneath headline losses.[^a-big-shift-in-the-ai-race-RO7Ly_wu9dQ]

# Key points

- The Commerce Department letter (published by Bloomberg) required removing access for "all foreign nationals, wherever located," under threat of criminal and civil penalties; when Dario Amodei told Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick "this means we can't have the model out," Lutnick replied "That's the point."[^a-big-shift-in-the-ai-race-RO7Ly_wu9dQ]
- Administration sources are scathing — "They came to every fork in the road and took the wrong fork"; "They screwed us" — while Whittemore faults Anthropic's slow responsiveness (days to hand over the Glasswing list, an hour and 15 minutes to return a mission-critical government call) and argues the charitable interpretation is that Anthropic hadn't realized managing the US government relationship "is now as important a job as anything they build in the lab."[^a-big-shift-in-the-ai-race-RO7Ly_wu9dQ]
- Backlash is multi-disciplinary: an open letter with 100+ signatures says removing Mythos from cyber defense makes everyone more vulnerable; CFR's Agathe Demarais wrote Washington "has just done more to boost the appeal of Chinese AI models than Beijing ever could have hoped"; Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid warned "you can't rely on something that could be switched off"; and the directive bans foreign-national researchers from working on the most advanced models, sending the US research community into a tailspin. Institute for Law and AI's Charlie Bullock calls the ad hoc licensing regime legally vulnerable and in desperate need of replacement by legislation.[^a-big-shift-in-the-ai-race-RO7Ly_wu9dQ]
- SpaceX's turnaround: after the xAI merger looked like a bailout, monetizing Colossus 1 and 2 via compute deals with Anthropic and then Google made neocloud revenue its number-one source almost overnight, giving the IPO narrative coherence; Musk's 46% stake made him roughly three times richer than the next wealthiest person, and Flexport's Ryan Petersen noted Musk made more in one day's pop than Warren Buffett in his entire career. Skeptics note Amazon has ~40x SpaceX's revenue and most stock is still locked.[^a-big-shift-in-the-ai-race-RO7Ly_wu9dQ]
- Bill Ackman on the acquisition mechanics: "One of the things that makes SpaceX so valuable is how valuable it is" — a high valuation makes accretive acquisitions like Cursor cheap in dilution terms; the FT called buying Cursor SpaceX's potential "Instagram moment."[^a-big-shift-in-the-ai-race-RO7Ly_wu9dQ]
- Cursor teased a trained-from-scratch model at its Compile event: same size as Opus/GPT-5.5, no more Kimi base, 10-20x more compute than Composer, "generally intelligent, not just coding"; Chamath Palihapitiya counters that Cursor's real value is the control plane — governance, auditability, and business continuity across models — calling it "the first, but not the last, big exit at the application layer."[^a-big-shift-in-the-ai-race-RO7Ly_wu9dQ]
- The DOJ intervened in the NAACP's Clean Air Act suit over Colossus 2's unpermitted gas turbines, arguing Grok is vital to national security — including targeting decisions for recent Iran strikes; Amodei has confirmed Claude is used for missile targeting. One commentator noted the same week's Fable ban and turbine defense are "actually the same story": AI being placed under national-security control.[^a-big-shift-in-the-ai-race-RO7Ly_wu9dQ]
- OpenAI's leaked audited financials: 2024 — $3.7B revenue, $12.4B costs, ~$5B net loss; 2025 — ~$13B revenue, $38.5B net loss. But $30B of the 2025 loss was a one-time non-cash accounting charge from the public-benefit-company conversion; stripped of that and stock comp, the loss was ~$8B — and inference is profitable ($13B revenue on $7.5B direct costs in 2025). Q1 burn was $3.7B (excluding $8.6B training costs) against $73B cash on hand, up from $40B in December.[^a-big-shift-in-the-ai-race-RO7Ly_wu9dQ]
- Both Anthropic and OpenAI have filed confidentially for IPOs; Whittemore sketches a scenario where SpaceX lockup expirations, government friction, and its cash pile keep OpenAI private longer.[^a-big-shift-in-the-ai-race-RO7Ly_wu9dQ]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's conclusion is that just as the industry seemed to settle into the agentic phase, the AI race has revealed itself as "an incredibly dynamic and fast-moving environment" undergoing political and economic realignment at once. His watch list: resolution of Anthropic's Fable 5 standoff; whether OpenAI can ship a more advanced model without a similar government collision; SpaceX's stock trajectory in the coming months; what Cursor's next model reveals about SpaceX's strategy (frontier-at-any-cost vs efficiency); and Google, "conspicuously quiet this year," which can't stay that way for long. On the Anthropic affair, his blunt verdict: the government is telling on itself about its lack of technical capacity, but a company now integral to the economy and national security doesn't get to treat managing Washington as optional.[^a-big-shift-in-the-ai-race-RO7Ly_wu9dQ]
