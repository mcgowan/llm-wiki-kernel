# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore uses Meta's and Microsoft's earnings as the first real 2026 read on where markets stand in the AI-bubble debate — a debate he stresses is separate from whether AI is meaningful technology, though not disconnected, since a financing pullback would slow how fast better AI reaches market. The pattern, per Bloomberg's Caroline Hyde: both companies beat on earnings and revenue, both raised capex, both talked up AI — but "Meta wins, Microsoft loses." Meta's stock jumped 8% after guiding data-center spending up to $135B (nearly double last year, implying debt funding) because investors could see AI converting into results — 24-25% revenue growth from AI-improved recommendations and ads, plus tripled Ray-Ban sales. Microsoft fell about 5% despite 38% Azure growth and a $625B cloud backlog, punished for a one-point cloud-growth slowdown that read as excessive caution during a boom.[^are-markets-still-worried-about-an-ai-bubble-LGNs5g1j8KE]

Rounding out the picture, South Korean memory giants Samsung and SK Hynix both roughly doubled profits on the AI-driven memory shortage, with Citigroup expecting DRAM costs to rise 120% this year. Whittemore's verdict: bubble fears persist, but markets are still in full gold-rush mode — rewarding companies for their place in the AI narrative, not just their spending.[^are-markets-still-worried-about-an-ai-bubble-LGNs5g1j8KE]

# Key points

- Meta grew revenue 24% year-over-year, raised 2026 data-center capex guidance to as much as $135B (~20% above median analyst forecast, almost double last year's spend), and disclosed a 40% expense rise from AI-team salary commitments; with ~$60B quarterly revenue, the plan implies debt reliance — critics noted Meta issued $30B in debt and would otherwise hold under $10B in cash.[^are-markets-still-worried-about-an-ai-bubble-LGNs5g1j8KE]
- Zuckerberg framed AI glasses as inevitable — "it's hard to imagine a world in several years where most glasses that people wear aren't AI glasses" — with Ray-Ban sales tripling over the past year, "some of the fastest-growing consumer electronics in history."[^are-markets-still-worried-about-an-ai-bubble-LGNs5g1j8KE]
- Zuckerberg called 2025 a rebuilding year for Meta's AI program, promised new models and products "over the coming months," emphasized commerce (agentic shopping tools) and personally contextual agents, but sidestepped an analyst's request for a 3/5/10-year ROI plan, admitting his answers "may be somewhat unfulfilling."[^are-markets-still-worried-about-an-ai-bubble-LGNs5g1j8KE]
- Microsoft's Azure grew 38% — down one percentage point from the prior quarter — and its cloud backlog more than doubled to $625B, 45% of it from a new $250B OpenAI commitment; Jefferies' Brent Thill flagged the concentration: can OpenAI achieve the financial goals to pay Oracle, Microsoft, and other providers?[^are-markets-still-worried-about-an-ai-bubble-LGNs5g1j8KE]
- Microsoft capex hit $37.5B for the quarter, up 66% year-over-year, but guidance wasn't raised; operating margin of 45.1% slightly missed the 45.5% consensus. Whittemore reads the selloff not as bubble fear but as punishment for under-building — over a trillion dollars in backlog while choosing to miss sales.[^are-markets-still-worried-about-an-ai-bubble-LGNs5g1j8KE]
- Nadella played the long game — "we are only at the beginning phases of AI diffusion" — and framed agents as "the new apps," with Microsoft providing model catalogs, orchestration harnesses, context engineering, and AI safety/observability.[^are-markets-still-worried-about-an-ai-bubble-LGNs5g1j8KE]
- Bloomberg's Dave Lee ("Microsoft has lost its AI sparkle") argued Microsoft's early-mover OpenAI advantage has run its course amid "AI polyamory" — OpenAI turning to Oracle, Google, and Amazon while Microsoft invests in Anthropic — with the 2026 narrative favoring Google's Gemini on consumer and Anthropic on coding.[^are-markets-still-worried-about-an-ai-bubble-LGNs5g1j8KE]
- Samsung profits doubled year-over-year to $13.7B (up 61% quarter-over-quarter; memory-division profits up fivefold), and SK Hynix doubled operating profit to $13.5B; both are keeping capex restrained rather than risk overbuilding, and Citigroup expects DRAM costs up 120% and NAND up 90% this year.[^are-markets-still-worried-about-an-ai-bubble-LGNs5g1j8KE]
- CLSA's Sanjeev Rana: "The companies are spending real money on real stuff. We're in uncharted territory in terms of valuations, share prices, the demand cycle — everything is unprecedented."[^are-markets-still-worried-about-an-ai-bubble-LGNs5g1j8KE]

# Takeaways

Whittemore concludes that this first round of 2026 tech earnings shows "pretty much a full-on gold rush" still underway in AI. Bubble fears are present, and markets increasingly demand that AI spending translate into visible growth — cloud revenue for Microsoft, ad conversion for Meta — but the divergent reactions prove it isn't about capex alone: Zuckerberg raised spending far more aggressively than Microsoft and was rewarded, because Meta paired the spend with demonstrated AI conversion and undisputed leadership in AI wearables. The market, in his read, is rewarding firms both for participating in the boom and for where they sit in the AI narrative — and to the extent genuine bubble fears exist, they center on OpenAI's ability to make good on its massive commitments.[^are-markets-still-worried-about-an-ai-bubble-LGNs5g1j8KE]
