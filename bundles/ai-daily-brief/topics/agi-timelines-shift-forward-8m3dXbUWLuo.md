# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore reports from the sidelines of the 2026 World Economic Forum in Davos, where Anthropic's Dario Amodei and Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis gave a run of interviews signaling that AGI timelines are shifting forward. Hassabis puts AGI on roughly a 5-year timeline, suggesting the "last mile" is harder than throwing more compute at recursive self-improvement, while Amodei is closer to 2 years — and, Whittemore observes, seems to believe it is even nearer, with the 2-year figure functioning as a hedge "to not sound insane."[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]

The timeline compression frames the episode's most attention-grabbing moment: Amodei's attack on the Trump administration's approval of Nvidia selling advanced chips to China, which he called "a bit like selling nuclear weapons to North Korea." The two CEOs also debated whether the AGI race could be paused — Hassabis said he would support a pause if everyone did, invoking his long-held dream of a "CERN for AI," while Amodei argued geopolitical competition makes any enforceable slowdown impossible unless chip proliferation is stopped first.[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]

# Key points

- Hassabis puts AGI on about a 5-year timeline; Amodei is closer to 2 years, and Whittemore reads him as thinking it is even sooner than that.[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]
- Amodei called the administration's approval of advanced Nvidia chip sales to China "a major mistake" with national security implications: "It's a bit like selling nuclear weapons to North Korea... it's basically the only area where we are meaningfully ahead."[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]
- Hassabis reiterated that China is about 6 months behind the West and "very good at catching up to where the frontier is," but has yet to show it can innovate beyond the frontier.[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]
- Asked if he would pause in a perfect world where all companies and countries paused, Hassabis said yes, again proposing an international CERN-equivalent for AI — but noted it is useless without whole-world agreement on minimum standards.[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]
- Amodei said he would prefer Hassabis's timeline and would slow down if the race were only "between me and Demis," but geopolitical adversaries building the same technology make an enforceable agreement impossible — hence his push to restrict chip sales.[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]
- Pause advocates seized on the exchange; Michael Trazzi cited it as vindication four months after a hunger strike, saying the concession requires international coordination.[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]
- Amodei updated his Davos-2025 software-engineering prediction: AI models will be able to do "most, maybe all" of what software engineers do end-to-end within 6 to 12 months — the point where the recursive AI-builds-better-AI loop begins.[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]
- Node.js creator Ryan Dahl went viral saying "the era of humans writing code is over," with engineers' remaining work no longer being writing syntax directly.[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]
- Amodei warned of a "very unusual combination of very fast GDP growth and high unemployment," requiring a government role in a macroeconomically large displacement; Hassabis is more optimistic but says adaptation must be intentional.[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]
- Andrew Curran's read: with no coordination possible, Amodei "has to blitz" — Anthropic will lean into recursive self-improvement and "go all out from here to the finish line."[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]

# Takeaways

Whittemore argues that while he takes AI disruption concerns seriously, "made-for-social-media" positions like a 6-month pause or a data-center moratorium are counterproductive — there is neither the political capital nor the will for an enforceable cross-border pause in a moment when the US Commerce Secretary is telling the same Davos forum that globalization has failed. The one thread connecting accelerationists and safetyists — belief that AI's change is immense — could power unexpected coalitions on public awareness and policy, but not if energy goes to soundbite policies. His bottom line: unlike last year's theoretical Davos AI talk, this year's predictions carry a new confidence grounded in evidence, and as Diego Od put it, "2026 will be a weird year. Brace yourself for the next generation of models."[^agi-timelines-shift-forward-8m3dXbUWLuo]
