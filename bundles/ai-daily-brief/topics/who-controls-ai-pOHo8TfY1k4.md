# Overview

In a special weekend episode, Whittemore recounts how the skirmish between Anthropic and the Pentagon became "all-out war." Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth had given Dario Amodei an ultimatum: remove terms-of-use limits by Friday or be blacklisted from the military supply chain. Anthropic's red lines — no domestic mass surveillance of Americans and no fully autonomous weapons — had, per Amodei, never been included in its Department of War contracts and never blocked adoption. Amodei publicly refused ("These threats do not change our position. We cannot in good conscience accede"), and on Friday afternoon President Trump ordered every federal agency to cease using Anthropic's technology, with Hegseth designating Anthropic a supply chain risk — a label previously reserved for adversaries — barring military contractors from any commercial activity with the company.[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]

Hours later, Sam Altman announced OpenAI had reached its own agreement to deploy models on the DOW's classified network, saying the department accepted OpenAI's principles against mass surveillance and for human responsibility over force. Whittemore maps the sprawling debate — from Palmer Luckey's democratic-control argument to Dean Ball calling the contractor ban "attempted corporate murder" — and frames the whole affair as the moment AI ethics stopped being theoretical and became geopolitical.[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]

# Key points

- Amodei's Thursday statement stressed Anthropic is not pacifist — it has been more hawkish on China tech access than peers — but held that surveillance and autonomous weapons "can undermine rather than defend democratic values" and exceed what today's technology can safely do.[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
- Amodei revealed the DOW threatened removal from its systems, a supply-chain-risk designation, and invoking the Defense Production Act — threats he called "inherently contradictory" (security risk vs. essential to national security).[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
- Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell set a 5:01 p.m. Friday deadline demanding use for "all lawful purposes"; Under Secretary Emil Michael called Amodei "a liar" with "a god complex"; over 200 Google and OpenAI staff signed a petition (notdivided.org) backing Anthropic's red lines.[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
- Trump's 3:47 p.m. Truth Social post called Anthropic a "radical left woke company," directed every federal agency to immediately cease using its technology with a 6-month phase-out, and threatened "major civil and criminal consequences."[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
- Hegseth's designation would bar any contractor, supplier, or partner doing business with the US military from commercial activity with Anthropic — lawyers noted 10 USC 3252 requires a risk assessment, written determination, and congressional notice, making "effective immediately" almost certainly untrue.[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
- Taken literally, the ban would sever Anthropic from AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure — all military contractors; Dean Ball: "This is simply attempted corporate murder... I could not possibly recommend starting an AI company in the United States."[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
- Anthropic responded that it had received no direct communication beyond social media, promised a court challenge, and assured commercial customers that a supply-chain-risk designation legally only covers Claude's use in DOW contract work — though NLW notes Operation Choke Point history shows implication alone makes companies ditch vendors.[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
- Altman's OpenAI-DOW deal: deployment on classified networks, OpenAI builds its own safety stack, the government won't force refused tasks, forward-deployed engineers, cloud-only deployment, and a request that the DOW offer identical terms to all AI companies.[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
- Critics like Peter Wildeford and Mark Valerian accused Altman of abandoning "moral clarity" within half a day to torture a competitor; NLW cautions that nobody commenting has the behind-the-scenes context.[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
- Palmer Luckey's defense of the government position: seemingly innocuous vendor terms are "moral minefields" that hand unelected corporations veto power over military operations — "Should our military be regulated by our elected leaders or corporate executives?"[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
- Libertarian Erik Voorhees called Anthropic's refusal "eminently based"; conservatives like Adam Holter argued the government could walk away from the $200M contract but that punishing a company for refusing service in peacetime is unprecedented.[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
- Claude hit number two in the App Store amid the fight; commentators flagged a scenario where OpenAI's DOW association triggers visceral consumer switching, with Greg Brockman's Trump donations already circulating in progressive circles.[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
- Whittemore's six-camp taxonomy of the discourse ranges from "Anthropic is right, there should be AI red lines" to "punish the infidels lest the other uppity AI CEOs get ideas."[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]

# Takeaways

Whittemore lands on Kirsten Faulkner's framing: "The Anthropic Pentagon standoff is not a tech story. It's the moment AI ethics stopped being theoretical and became geopolitical" — whoever decides the ethics of AI will be deciding the ethics of society. He also credits Mike Solana's trilemma: a free society doesn't want to force private companies, doesn't want companies running the military, yet races an authoritarian state that controls its labs — with no satisfying answer. His positive note: the mess is a "big blinking reminder" of how important these conversations are, and since AI is not yet a hardened partisan issue, his plea is to ignore anyone trying to turn it into another culture-war fight.[^who-controls-ai-pOHo8TfY1k4]
