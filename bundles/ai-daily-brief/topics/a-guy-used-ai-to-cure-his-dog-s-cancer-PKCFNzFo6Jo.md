# Overview

Nominally about the viral story of an Australian who "used AI to cure his dog's cancer," this episode is really about the frenetic state of AI discourse. Whittemore's frame: we are in AI's "second moment." The first was the ChatGPT moment of late 2022/early 2023; the second is the Claude Code / Opus 4.5 / Codex 5.2 agents moment — and just as Time ran an "End of Humanity" cover in May 2023, the dust cloud around this second moment is proportionally bigger. Bernie Sanders is posting nine-minute X-risk videos, ServiceNow's Bill McDermott casually predicts 30%+ new-graduate unemployment, polls show worsening sentiment — while non-coders quietly manage teams of a dozen agents. "The divergence between mainstream perception and actual capability has never been higher."[^a-guy-used-ai-to-cure-his-dog-s-cancer-PKCFNzFo6Jo]

Two weekend stories illustrate the everything-at-11 dynamic: Andrej Karpathy's two-hour vibe-coded BLS jobs-exposure tree map, sensationalized into "a diagnosis of how replaceable you are" until he took it down in frustration; and the hyper-viral story (nearly 13 million views) of Paul Cunningham, whose dog Rosie received the first personalized mRNA cancer vaccine designed for a dog — a story that was simultaneously genuinely remarkable and heavily oversimplified in both directions.[^a-guy-used-ai-to-cure-his-dog-s-cancer-PKCFNzFo6Jo]

# Key points

- Six differences between AI's first and second moments: (1) a huge capability jump — the AutoGPT/BabyAGI seeds of 2023 have become real agents; (2) billions of weekly users versus ChatGPT's then-record 100 million in 5 weeks; (3) far higher economic stakes — Anthropic went from "not a blip" to a $19 billion run rate "taking down industries every time it announces a new feature"; (4) AI as corporate fall guy for unwinding post-COVID over-hiring (Chamath: AI as "plausible deniability" for RIFs); (5) general political volatility, including the Iran war; (6) three and a half years of the AI industry "doing a completely awful job of explaining itself" (Packy McCormick: "AI companies have clearly botched telling the story").[^a-guy-used-ai-to-cure-his-dog-s-cancer-PKCFNzFo6Jo]
- The Karpathy episode: he scraped 342 BLS occupations, had an LLM score digital AI exposure 0-10 (average 5.3; software devs 8-9, roofers 0-1, medical transcriptionists 10), with explicit caveats that a high score "does not predict the job will disappear" and ignores demand elasticity, latent demand, regulatory barriers, and social preferences; viral posts converted it into "57 million Americans at high risk" claims until Karpathy — calling it "a Saturday morning 2-hour vibe-coded project... wildly misinterpreted" — took the page down.[^a-guy-used-ai-to-cure-his-dog-s-cancer-PKCFNzFo6Jo]
- Economists pushed back on the exposure-equals-displacement reading: Chicago Booth's Alex Imas — "exposure... can literally mean the opposite"; Anthropic's Peter McCrory on observed-exposure measures and bottleneck tasks increasing demand for complementary human skills; Toronto's Kevin Bryan betting $1,000 that most "susceptible" jobs see increased labor share by 2030.[^a-guy-used-ai-to-cure-his-dog-s-cancer-PKCFNzFo6Jo]
- The Rosie story: Cunningham's rescue dog had chemo- and surgery-resistant cancer; ChatGPT suggested sequencing her tumor DNA (~$3,000) and using AlphaFold to find immunotherapy targets; when no drug maker would help, Pall Thordarson of the RNA Institute at the University of New South Wales built a bespoke mRNA vaccine in under two months — the first personalized cancer vaccine designed for a dog. Some tumors shrank, but calling it a "cure" goes too far.[^a-guy-used-ai-to-cure-his-dog-s-cancer-PKCFNzFo6Jo]
- Thordarson's own nuances: it was about buying time, not curing; real costs are hard to estimate because many donated time and resources; veterinary regulation differs greatly from human health. His forward-looking point: Rosie shows cancer-vaccine design can be democratized, with genomic analysis and RNA production becoming service provision — raising questions about regulatory overhaul and equitable access.[^a-guy-used-ai-to-cure-his-dog-s-cancer-PKCFNzFo6Jo]
- Whittemore notes the story is arguably about the Nobel Prize-winning AlphaFold more than ChatGPT, and that the treatment's own designer had to take to X to walk back the framing — yet finds it "refreshing to have people excited about the positive disruptive potential of AI."[^a-guy-used-ai-to-cure-his-dog-s-cancer-PKCFNzFo6Jo]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's conclusion is that the doom sensationalism around Karpathy's chart and the hype sensationalism around Rosie's vaccine are "two sides of the same coin": symptoms of the transition into AI's second moment, when everything in the discourse runs at an 11 and will stay weird "until we all get used to the new paradigm that we're living in." His promise to listeners: hanging around the show will at least make them "feel slightly less like you're taking crazy pills."[^a-guy-used-ai-to-cure-his-dog-s-cancer-PKCFNzFo6Jo]
