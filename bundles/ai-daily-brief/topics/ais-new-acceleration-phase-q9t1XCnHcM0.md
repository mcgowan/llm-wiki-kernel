# Overview

In this Friday weekly recap, Nathaniel Whittemore argues the week's individual stories add up to something more than their sum: a felt acceleration across model development, business models, pricing paradigms, consumer services, compute, and policy. The anchor story is Anthropic projecting its first-ever profitable quarter — the first for any AI lab — which, despite caveats (projections not realized revenue, revenue-recognition questions, discounted SpaceX compute), resets expectations after a bubble narrative claiming labs could never serve agentic tokens profitably. OpenAI generated about a billion dollars more than Anthropic in Q1, boosted by "token hungry Codex," and Nvidia beat every analyst expectation at a five-trillion-plus market cap.[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]

Alongside the money, Whittemore tracks the end of the "subsidy era" of flat-rate AI pricing, SpaceX's emergence as a compute provider at scale, Google I/O's agentic-search announcements, an internal OpenAI model disproving a 1946 Erdős conjecture, and Andrej Karpathy joining Anthropic to work on recursive self-improvement.[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]

# Key points

- Anthropic expects the first profitable quarter of any AI lab; caveats include unfinished quarter, counting top-line revenue before partner distributions, and discounted SpaceX compute — but Whittemore calls these "relative quibbles" against the reset in expectations.[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]
- Patrick Moorhead on Nvidia: with Jensen's $1 trillion forward demand pipeline "this is an eight-to-nine-trillion-dollar company. People are afraid to move even on exceptional earnings."[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]
- The subsidy era is ending: Google's Ultra plan "price cut" from $250 to $200/month actually shifted token-hungry use cases to usage-based billing; Microsoft canceled its Claude Code licenses partly on cost; Hedgeye: "Token-based pricing is forcing every enterprise customer to confront the actual cost of running these models at scale." Anthropic added a /usage command showing which skills, agents, MCPs, or plugins hog tokens.[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]
- Cursor's Composer 2.5 performed comparably to Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5 (per Artificial Analysis) at 10–60x lower cost — efficiency as one answer to the token shortage.[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]
- Musk declared SpaceX is "offering AI compute as a service at significant scale"; Anthropic's chief compute officer Tom Brown announced expansion onto Colossus 2 — positioning SpaceX as an alternative neo-cloud ahead of its IPO, with orbital data centers as the unique long-term pitch.[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]
- Google's Gemini app hit 900 million MAUs, effectively closing the gap with ChatGPT; monthly tokens processed jumped 700% from 480 trillion to 3.2 quadrillion year over year.[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]
- Google is bringing agents into Search — persistent "information agents" working 24/7 (e.g., apartment-hunting alerts) — which Whittemore calls potentially the most significant I/O announcement, converting search from one-time to persistent information gathering; Docs Live adds voice-first document creation.[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]
- An internal OpenAI model — a general-purpose LLM with no math-specific training, per Noam Brown — disproved Erdős's 1946 unit-distance conjecture; Fields medalist Tim Gowers called it "the first really clear example of AI solving... a really well-known unsolved math problem." Ethan Mollick estimated the solve used less water than three almonds.[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]
- Andrej Karpathy joined Anthropic to build a team using Claude to accelerate pre-training research itself (recursive self-improvement), saying the next few years at the LLM frontier will be "especially formative."[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]
- Policy whiplash: Newsom signed an exploratory executive order on AI labor disruption (dashboards, early-warning signs, severance/UI reform) — which Whittemore reads as a preview of 2028 presidential AI language; Trump's long-awaited AI executive order (90-day pre-release government model review, Treasury-led security hardening) was scuttled hours before signing after David Sacks personally intervened, with Trump saying "I don't want to do anything that gets in the way of that lead" over China.[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's summary: the acceleration was less in any single story than in the feeling their implications create — a routine forthcoming model solving 80-year-old math problems, skyrocketing revenues, a first profitable lab quarter, billionaire researchers returning to the field, and intensifying political jockeying. He welcomes the accelerating counter-narrative on data centers (water-use comparisons to golf courses and almonds; blue-collar job stories) because community debates should rest on "real facts and evidence" rather than generalized fear. He closes approvingly with Demis Hassabis's I/O framing — "we were standing in the foothills of the singularity" — adding: "It's a great message and it's our job to make sure it's true."[^ais-new-acceleration-phase-q9t1XCnHcM0]
