# Overview

An extended headlines section leads with the Wall Street Journal report that Stripe is in talks to acquire OpenRouter for around $10 billion — a huge markup on its $1.3B valuation just two months earlier — which Whittemore reads as a sign that in the shift from "token maxing" to token scarcity, the metering, billing, and routing layer for inference is becoming a prize. Related stories: Cursor launched its own router claiming frontier-level performance at 60% lower cost, Anthropic and OpenAI both shipped voice upgrades, Amazon shut down its AGI spin-off lab amid Nova-team layoffs, Microsoft published "hill-climbing" results showing tiny MAI models beating larger rivals inside product harnesses (with Satya Nadella framing a route-to-cheap-models strategy), SpaceX AI is expanding terrestrial data centers in Texas, and Washington debated an AI Kill Switch Bill while Commerce Secretary Lutnick tried to calm the Kimi K3 panic.[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]

The main episode reads Anthropic head of economics Peter McCrory's essay "Why hasn't AI increased unemployment?" — his personal synthesis of 18 months of Anthropic economic research. McCrory's core claim: the US labor market is stable and near maximum employment, AI has caused no material increase in unemployment to date, and AI so far bears the hallmarks of a skill-biased, labor-augmenting technology where complementary human expertise amplifies what AI can do.[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]

# Key points

- Stripe-OpenRouter: reportedly close to a ~$10B deal; commentators framed it as Stripe buying "the metering and billing layer for inference"; competitors include Cursor Router, Meta's internal router, Ramp, and Vercel.[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]
- Anthropic extended voice mode to Opus and Sonnet (not just Haiku), added connector support (Gmail, Slack, Notion) and took foreign-language support out of beta; OpenAI brought voice to desktop via its real-time model GPT Live.[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]
- Microsoft's MAI Code 1 Flash achieved a 10% higher code-accept rate than GPT-5.4 Mini and Haiku 4.5 in VS Code with 10% lower token usage; Excel-harness training pushed SWE-bench Verified from 72% to 86%, and MAI Image 2.5 replaced GPT Image 2 in PowerPoint at an 84% cost reduction per Mustafa Suleyman.[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]
- A joint CAISI/UK AISI report found Kimi K3 far behind US frontier models on cybersecurity (32.2% vs 76.2% average on ExploitBench); David Sacks: "the Kimi panic needs to stop... Let our horses run"; Jensen Huang argued many open models are safer than a US duopoly with "one single point of attack."[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]
- McCrory's baseline: June unemployment at 4.2% (consistent with full employment), job openings-to-unemployed ratio just over one, prime-age employment near multi-decade highs — and AI is big enough to matter, with 20% of firms using AI (40% in the information sector) and quality-adjusted AI output growing over 2,000% per year in 2024 and 2025.[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]
- Labor productivity grew 2% per year from 2022 to 2026 versus 1.6% in the four pre-pandemic years — early evidence of AI in aggregate statistics.[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]
- On displacement, McCrory is "unconvinced": no worsening unemployment in Claude-automation-exposed roles; suggestive weakness in young-worker hiring (consistent with Stanford's "Canaries in the Coal Mine") is confounded by "the largest non-recessionary labor market slowdown on record" and a low-hire/low-fire market that hits entrants hardest.[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]
- Why augmentation: capabilities remain "stubbornly jagged" requiring expert oversight; no O*NET occupation has all tasks systematically handled by Claude; sophisticated user inputs correlate with complex Claude outputs; and experienced users treat Claude as a thought partner.[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]
- Claude Code data shows "persistent returns to human expertise": people with more domain expertise succeed more often and recover better from errors — straightforward coding ability may be worth less, but complementary skills like delegation and evaluation are worth more.[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]
- On recursive self-improvement, McCrory cites Aghion, Jones, and Jones: growth "may be constrained not by what we do well, but rather by what is essential and yet hard to improve" — weak links that can keep labor's income share elevated; his prediction: models get much better, but unemployment won't be noticeably higher in a year "at least not because of AI."[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]
- Responses: Stanford's Andy Hall said the piece explains why the unemployment-driven political backlash hasn't arrived; Trace Cohen countered the real impact may show first in hiring — fewer junior roles, smaller teams, slower backfilling.[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]

# Takeaways

Whittemore calls the evidence "extremely encouraging" while stressing epistemic humility about scenarios where AI does hit jobs hard. His key addition is that narrative itself is causal: if the story is that AI should halve headcount — and investors expect it — executives will do exactly that; if the story is augmentation, lateral expansion, and new products, leaders will use AI that way instead. He flags average team size as the metric to watch, expecting smaller, more nimble teams with bigger responsibilities and gradual redeployment rather than mass displacement, and welcomes Anthropic finally telling a positive story.[^why-ai-hasn-t-increased-unemployment-according-t-niPCJO0Cdss]
