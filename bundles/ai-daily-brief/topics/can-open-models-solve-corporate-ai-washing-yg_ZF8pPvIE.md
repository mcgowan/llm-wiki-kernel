# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore ties Alibaba's Qwen 3.8 Max release to a broader shift in enterprise AI thinking. The 2.4-trillion-parameter model — which Latent Space says would have been the world's top open model but for Kimi K3 — marks the first time Alibaba will open-source the weights of a Max-class Qwen model, reversing last year's move to closed, and lands at $2/$6 per million input/output tokens: about a third of Kimi K3's price and a fifth of Opus's. Self-reported benchmarks claim scores between Fable 5 and GPT-5.5 Soul on terminal bench and state-of-the-art agentic computer use, though early independent testing is far more mixed. Whittemore's framing device is his return from KPMG's Tech and Innovation Symposium, where the conversation had leapt from counting AI use cases in 2025 to governance, cost provisioning, and open-weights-model policy — meaning mainstream enterprise IT buyers, not just developers, now pay attention to releases like this.[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]

He pairs the release with former Lululemon CIO Julie Averill's New York Times op-ed on "AI wishing" (believing AI is a magic wand that skips hard work) and "AI washing" (claiming more AI progress than exists — most insidiously the AI layoff), arguing the enterprise conventional wisdom is finally becoming directionally correct. In the headlines: Palantir's 93% revenue growth and Alex Karp's broadside against the "token industrial complex," OpenAI's retort in the Apple trade-secrets lawsuit, DeepMind framing capex as a bet on recursive self-improvement, and Claude helping researchers expose a decades-old DNA-database vulnerability.[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]

# Key points

- Palantir posted $1.94B quarterly revenue (up 93% YoY), commercial sales up 149%, and $1B net income growing at a 225% annual pace; Karp framed the results as demand for AI sovereignty — customers wanting "maximal control over their operations, data, and decisions" — and attacked labs "structurally designed to capture" customers' business intelligence, saying of Dario Amodei and "the effective altruism crew": "We have people trying to drug addict us to a future they believe they control."[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]
- In the Apple-OpenAI trade-secrets suit, OpenAI's letter called the lawsuit "careless, aggressive, and oddly personal," revealing Apple's outside lawyers had emailed the wrong person "after confusing two Asian last names" — undermining Apple's claim that OpenAI ignored its February outreach.[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]
- Google DeepMind's chief strategy officer acknowledged AI revenues "don't sustain the capital expenditures" and warned of an "AI air pocket," but reframed capex as a down payment on recursive self-improvement — "the biggest scientific bet civilization has ever made."[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]
- Researchers used Claude-written code to demonstrate (in ~45 minutes) that 1995-era DNA evidence database software lacks tamper-evident protections — files "referred to as the gold standard of forensic science that lack the same level of tamper-evident markings that we require for a paper bag" — prompting a digital-signatures update; Whittemore's takeaway: AI is a massive upgrade for cyber defenders, not just attackers.[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]
- Qwen 3.8 Max: 2.4T parameters, claims of 10+ days of autonomous self-evolving development, native multimodal intelligence as a "continuous feedback loop," and self-reported near-frontier benchmarks; full weights release promised within a week — the first open-weighted Qwen Max-class model.[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]
- The launch ad — a laptop working while humans fish, play tennis, rock climb, and read — drew as much discourse as the model; Jason Calacanis praised China's "world-positive AI marketing" while Alex Svanevik noted the contrast with Western doom framing.[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]
- Early independent results are skeptical: Artificial Analysis briefly published a score of 53 (four points behind Kimi K3, one behind Grok 4.5) then pulled it without explanation; Ethan Mollick found it solid but "not Kimi K3 level"; one tester called it "unusable" — slow, unstable, last in every head-to-head; Pavel Huryn's bug bench had it fixing 19/105 bugs at ~$31 cost versus GPT-5.6 Luna's 33 bugs for $1.80.[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]
- Whittemore stresses pricing reality: Chinese models are no longer "pennies on the dollar" (Kimi K3 is only ~40% cheaper than Opus), making Qwen's steeper discount notable.[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]
- Averill's op-ed: in May, US employers announced 97,000 job cuts with 40% blamed on AI, yet about a third of hiring managers who cut a role for AI had already rehired for the same or similar one — companies "eliminated positions before redesigning the work." Her key line: "This kind of work doesn't happen in a quarter, and believing that it can is the trap."[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]
- The cost-management ecosystem is maturing: Thinking Machines Lab's Tinker for fine-tuning, Microsoft's frontier tuning service on MAI models, router companies (with reports Stripe may buy OpenRouter for $10B), and the mantra that "AI cost optimization is now a discipline, not a hack."[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]

# Takeaways

Whittemore argues organizations treating AI strictly as an efficiency technology may eke out short-term headline wins but "are ultimately going to be absolutely pummeled" by companies that treat it as a redesign moment and opportunity technology — AI done well requires organizational, role, and process redesign, and shortcuts fail. His optimistic thesis: for the first time since ChatGPT launched, enterprise conventional wisdom is getting directionally correct — leaders are asking sophisticated questions about open-weights policies, fine-tuning, routing, and cost discipline rather than chasing PR value — and as the board-plaudit incentive for AI washing dries up, the real work of redesigning around AI's capabilities can begin.[^can-open-models-solve-corporate-ai-washing-yg_ZF8pPvIE]
