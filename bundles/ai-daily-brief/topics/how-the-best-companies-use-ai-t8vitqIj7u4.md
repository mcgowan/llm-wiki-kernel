# Overview

In this "long read/big think" episode, Nathaniel Whittemore synthesizes what distinguishes companies that are best at using AI from everyone else, drawing on a PwC study, McKinsey's AI transformation manifesto, George Zarkadakis's a16z essay on institutional versus individual AI, and — most concretely — Ramp's internal AI workspace "Glass." The consistent theme: leading companies treat AI as a growth and business-model-reinvention technology, not merely an efficiency tool, and they build systems — coordination layers, context infrastructure, and harnesses — rather than just dropping tools on employees.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]

Whittemore holds up Ramp's Glass as "harness engineering at an organizational level": a fully configured AI workspace every employee gets on day one, with SSO-connected integrations, a marketplace of 350+ shared skills, persistent memory, and scheduled automations. He argues its design principles — don't limit anyone's upside, make one person's breakthrough everyone's baseline, and treat the product itself as the enablement — represent a crucial mindset reset: organizations should stop presuming some employees will only ever be basic chat users, because AI itself now serves as the tutor that lets anyone reach power-user capability.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]

# Key points

- A PwC study found three-quarters of AI's economic gains are captured by just 20% of companies — and those leaders are 2-3x as likely to use AI to pursue growth opportunities and 2.6x as likely to say AI improves their ability to reinvent their business model.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]
- McKinsey's AI transformation manifesto lists 12 themes separating AI leaders from laggards, including "technology alone doesn't create advantage, enduring capabilities do," focusing AI on economic leverage points, and "relearn like your business depends on it."[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]
- McKinsey studied 20 AI-leader companies and found their transformations delivered on average a 20% EBITDA uplift, break-even in 1-2 years, and $3 of incremental EBITDA per $1 invested; they also argue more than 70% of AI talent should be in-house.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]
- George Zarkadakis's a16z piece "Institutional AI versus Individual AI" argues that while AI has made individuals 10x more productive, no company has become 10x more valuable — because institutional AI requires a coordination layer, not just aggregated individual productivity.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]
- OpenAI reported that 50% of usage of its new Codex app is not about coding specifically — evidence that giving agents the ability to write code unlocks knowledge work far beyond software engineering.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]
- Ramp co-founder Eric Glyman: 99% of Ramp uses AI daily, but most people were stuck on painful setup (terminal configs, MCP servers), so they built Glass — day-one configured workspaces with 350+ reusable skills, persistent memory, and scheduled automations.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]
- A viral post on Glass (a million views) by Seb, who runs internal AI at Ramp, argues "the models are good enough, the harness isn't" — most people drive "a Ferrari with the handbrake on" for lack of a well-configured environment.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]
- Glass's skill marketplace "Dojo" propagates workflows (e.g., a CX engineer's Zendesk investigation skill) team-wide, and an AI guide called "the Sensei" recommends the five most relevant skills per role rather than making users browse 350.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]
- Glass builds a per-user memory system from authenticated connections (Slack, Notion, Linear) and runs a synthesis and cleanup pipeline every 24 hours — which Whittemore flags as the piece with the most hidden technical complexity.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]
- Ramp's three reasons to build rather than buy: internal AI productivity is a moat, owning the tool means same-day fixes, and internal problems translate directly into their external AI-first finance product.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]
- Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch announced open-sourcing a reference platform for cloud coding agents, to help others build the systems that Stripe, Ramp, Spotify, and Block are building.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's summary of how the best companies use AI: first, they aim at growth, opportunity, and new business — not just efficiency and productivity; second, they recognize AI success is about creating systems that coordinate individual AI outputs, provide context, and build harnesses around AI use. He argues McKinsey's claim that "agentic engineering" is the next capability to master understates the frontier — at the truly leading organizations, agentic engineering is becoming everyone's work, not just software developers'. The deepest mindset shift he endorses is Ramp's refusal to segment employees into good, medium, and bad AI users: "We don't believe in lowering the ceiling. We believe in raising the floor" — because with AI as a tutor and build partner, everyone can be a superuser, and AI use is now a core primitive of organizational operations that cannot be outsourced.[^how-the-best-companies-use-ai-t8vitqIj7u4]
