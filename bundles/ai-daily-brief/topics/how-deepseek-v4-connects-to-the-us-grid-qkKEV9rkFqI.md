# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore connects two stories: the White House invoking Section 303 of the Defense Production Act to declare grid infrastructure — transformers, transmission lines, substations, and their upstream supply chains — essential to national defense, and the long-awaited release of DeepSeek V4. The through-line is the shift from last year's "excess compute" bubble worries to today's question of whether the US can provision the compute people want — with energy the binding constraint upstream. Goldman Sachs projected data centers going from ~6% to 11% of US electricity demand by 2030, and JP Morgan explicitly called the aging grid a national security risk before the presidential memo directed the Secretary of Energy to make purchases and commitments to expand domestic grid manufacturing capacity.[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]

DeepSeek V4 (1.6T-parameter V4 Pro plus V4 Flash, both with million-token context) lands near but not at the frontier — roughly Opus 4.5/4.6 and GPT 5.3/5.4 class — but at less than a seventh the cost of Opus 4.6, with DeepSeek publicly tying future price cuts to ramping Huawei chip production. Whittemore relays Matthew Berman's argument that "good enough, given away" is a serious threat: US enterprises building on Chinese open-source models is a geopolitical security risk in reverse. Headlines cover Google's $40B Anthropic investment, Meta renting Amazon Graviton 5 CPUs for agentic workloads, the AI trade driving markets to all-time highs (Nvidia becoming the first $5T company), and Beijing blocking Meta's $2B Manifold acquisition while curbing US investment in Chinese AI startups.[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]

# Key points

- Google confirmed a $40B investment in Anthropic — $10B upfront, $30B tied to undisclosed milestones — atop prior $3B investments and a 5 GW compute agreement with Broadcom; if priced at Anthropic's $350B February valuation, Google gets a 50%+ discount to the ~$800B secondary-market valuation, and its stake could pass 20%.[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]
- A Mirae Securities note argues Anthropic "must bind itself far more deeply and far more dependently to those who possess these physical resources"; its locked-in capacity rivals Microsoft's entire ~6 GW 2024 global footprint, and Amazon wins on three axes as OpenAI and Anthropic compete: cloud fees, in-house silicon adoption, and data-center capex recovery.[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]
- Anthropic announced reaching $30B ARR; OpenAI expects 30 GW of capacity by 2030 with 8 GW already "identified," partnering with Oracle, data-center developers, and neoclouds.[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]
- Meta signed a multi-billion-dollar deal to rent Amazon Graviton 5 CPUs — optimized for agentic workloads — amid a forecasted $135B buildout this year and deals with Nvidia, AMD, Google, CoreWeave, and Nebius.[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]
- The AI trade is back: S&P 500 up 12.5% in a month, erasing the Iran-war drawdown; without the Mag 7 plus Broadcom the index is down year-to-date; Cisco took 26 years to reclaim its 2000 peak; Nvidia became the first $5T company nine months after hitting $4T.[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]
- The presidential memo, under Section 303 of the Defense Production Act and a declared national emergency, finds US industry cannot supply grid components in time due to limited domestic capacity, long procurement timelines, and foreign supply dependence, authorizing the Energy Secretary to make purchases and financial commitments.[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]
- DeepSeek V4 Pro benchmarks: dead heat around 80% on SWE-bench Verified, slightly ahead of Opus 4.6 and slightly behind GPT 5.4 on Terminal Bench 2.0, slightly behind Western peers on Humanity's Last Exam; Bloomberg and Dean Ball called it underwhelming, with Ball saying R1 remains the closest China has come to the US frontier.[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]
- Pricing is the real story: V4 Pro at $1.74/$3.48 per million input/output tokens is under a seventh the cost of Opus 4.6 and under a quarter of GPT 5.4; V4 Flash undercuts Gemini Flash Lite by 80%; Simon Willison: "almost on the frontier at a fraction of the price."[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]
- DeepSeek says it is compute-limited and will cut prices further once Huawei production ramps in H2 — analyst Po Zhao: "DeepSeek is publicly tying its API economics to domestic chip infrastructure. That's the real headline."[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]
- Matthew Berman's "serious threat" argument: most businesses don't need frontier intelligence, so cheap, fine-tunable, open-source good-enough models will win enterprise adoption — meaning the US needs to go much harder on open source, and OpenAI/Anthropic need to get much cheaper much faster.[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]
- Beijing moved to curb US investment in Chinese tech (Moonshot and StepFun reportedly instructed to reject US capital) and blocked Meta's $2B acquisition of Manifold on national security grounds — after checks were cashed and the team had Meta IDs — calling it an effort to "drain China of AI talent and resources."[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]

# Takeaways

Whittemore argues the narrative has fully flipped from bubble fears of excess compute to a scramble over whether enough compute — and the energy behind it — can be provisioned at all, with suppliers who over-learned dot-com-era caution now caught short by agentic demand. DeepSeek V4 didn't catch America, but its near-frontier performance at commodity prices, tied to domestic Chinese silicon, plus Beijing's investment restrictions and the Manifold block, mark "a new phase of the China-US AI competition." He'll be watching what the Secretary of Energy actually does with the new Defense Production Act authority.[^how-deepseek-v4-connects-to-the-us-grid-qkKEV9rkFqI]
