# Overview

This Headlines edition covers the AI Impact Summit in New Delhi — the first time the global summit series (previously UK, France, South Korea) was held in a developing country. The event platformed calls to address AI inequality, with UN Secretary General Antonio Guterres declaring "AI must belong to everyone" and proposing a global fund for AI skills, data, and affordable compute, while India declared its own ambition to become a global AI power backed by $100B+ data-center commitments each from Adani and Reliance. But the moment that dominated coverage was Sam Altman and Dario Amodei refusing to hold hands during a raised-arms photo op with Prime Minister Modi — a symbol, Whittemore says, of how bitter the OpenAI-Anthropic rivalry has become.[^weird-vibes-at-ai-india-summit-m-ZSpDTpW5w]

The back half turns to enterprise AI: Walmart leaning on its shopping assistant Sparky as a growth driver after soft earnings, Amazon tracking employee AI usage through its internal Clarity system, and Accenture explicitly tying AI adoption to promotions — which Whittemore reads as a bellwether for corporate AI mandates.[^weird-vibes-at-ai-india-summit-m-ZSpDTpW5w]

# Key points

- A UN report found AI adoption growing faster in the developed world, risking a permanent technological divide; Guterres called for a global fund to "build skills, data, affordable computing power, and inclusive ecosystems everywhere" — a shift from last year's Paris summit focus on European competitiveness toward AI as a global public good.[^weird-vibes-at-ai-india-summit-m-ZSpDTpW5w]
- Adani and Reliance Industries each committed over $100 billion to local data centers over the coming decade, with the Indian government earmarking a $1.1 billion fund; attendees included Sundar Pichai, Demis Hassabis, and Arthur Mensch, while Bill Gates canceled his keynote over Epstein-files scrutiny.[^weird-vibes-at-ai-india-summit-m-ZSpDTpW5w]
- In the on-stage photo op with Modi, Altman and Amodei alone refused to hold hands (tape analysis suggested Amodei refused Altman's hand); meanwhile a viral Epoch AI chart suggested Anthropic could overtake OpenAI in revenue by mid-year.[^weird-vibes-at-ai-india-summit-m-ZSpDTpW5w]
- Amodei delivered a generic speech read off an iPhone that drew mockery; Altman spoke more eloquently on AI's interaction with democracy, social contracts, and job loss, calling for continued iterative deployment, and told CNBC there is "some AI washing where people are blaming AI for layoffs that they would otherwise do."[^weird-vibes-at-ai-india-summit-m-ZSpDTpW5w]
- Swyx (Shawn Wang) criticized the summit for caring "more about bad photo ops and hobnobbing with celebrities than... the builders that are supposed to drive the Indian AI economy forward."[^weird-vibes-at-ai-india-summit-m-ZSpDTpW5w]
- Walmart, after briefly hitting a $1 trillion valuation but losing the largest-company-by-revenue crown to Amazon after 17 years, guided lower and leaned into AI: about half of online customers have used the Sparky assistant, and Sparky users order 35% more; US CEO David Guggina said Sparky is moving Walmart "from traditional search to intent-driven commerce."[^weird-vibes-at-ai-india-summit-m-ZSpDTpW5w]
- The Information reported Amazon uses its internal Clarity system to track team-level AI usage, including external tools and non-engineering functions like supply-chain optimization, asking employees how they "accomplished more with less" while reducing or not growing headcount.[^weird-vibes-at-ai-india-summit-m-ZSpDTpW5w]
- Per an email seen by the Financial Times, Accenture told senior managers that promotion to leadership requires regular AI adoption; CEO Julie Sweet had previously said staff who fail to adopt AI workflows would be "exited from the company." Accenture stock was down 17% year-to-date and 45% over the past year.[^weird-vibes-at-ai-india-summit-m-ZSpDTpW5w]
- FT reported AI holdouts among senior managers and partners are a bigger problem across consulting than junior-staff adoption; one person said they would quit if the policy affected them, another called Accenture's tools "broken slop generators."[^weird-vibes-at-ai-india-summit-m-ZSpDTpW5w]

# Takeaways

Whittemore finds it hard to take "global talk fests" seriously — leaders exchange platitudes and return to what they were already doing — and advises spending less time on summit theater and more time building. On AI mandates, he pushes back on the cynical take that tracking-and-promotion schemes prove the tools lack value: the real problem surfaced by AI Daily Brief and Superintelligent surveys is time — employees are not given carve-outs to learn tools that would save them time, plus approved workplace tools lag far behind what people use at home ("at home I'm using Opus 4.6 and at work I have a terrible old version of Copilot"). He expects more Accenture-style mandates in the months and year to come.[^weird-vibes-at-ai-india-summit-m-ZSpDTpW5w]
