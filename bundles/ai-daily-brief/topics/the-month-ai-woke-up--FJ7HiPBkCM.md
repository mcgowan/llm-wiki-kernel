# Overview

In his KPMG-sponsored monthly recap, Nathaniel Whittemore calls February 2026 "the month AI woke up" — transformational because the recognition that "something big is happening" cascaded across distinct groups in sequence. AI insiders internalized Andrej Karpathy's observation that coding agents "basically didn't work before December and they basically do now," making programming "unrecognizable" and shifting the prize to orchestration. That autonomy ambition got a name and icon in OpenClaw, which turned powerful models into system-level agents; Whittemore himself built a 10-agent team and launched Claw Camp, a self-directed program nearly 5,500 people are following.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]

The awakening then spread: white-collar non-developers like CNBC's Deirdre Bosa built working tools in an hour; Wall Street entered the "SaaS apocalypse," dumping stocks in AI's crosshairs every time Anthropic announced a plugin; and Washington's power struggle with Anthropic over autonomous-weapons and surveillance red lines escalated into the administration designating the company a supply-chain risk. Whittemore frames it all as the first manifestation of an inevitable struggle over who determines how AI is used.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]

# Key points

- Karpathy: programming changed "not gradually" but specifically since December — models now have the quality, long-term coherence, and tenacity to power through large tasks; "the era where you type code into an editor is done," replaced by spinning up and managing natural-language agents, with orchestration as the biggest prize.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]
- OpenClaw (originally ClaudeBot, briefly MoltBot — renamed at Anthropic's request) became the clearest manifestation of the autonomy shift, going from inbox-cleaning assistant to ambitious semi-autonomous work; Mac Minis and Mac Studios became the era's hot visualization.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]
- Whittemore built a 10-agent team (one developer, two researchers, five project managers, one chief of staff) and turned the process into Claw Camp, now being followed by nearly 5,500 people despite its difficulty.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]
- Solopreneur Ben Sarah built Pulsia — an AI for running autonomous AI companies with access to GitHub through Meta ads — reaching a $1.25M+ annual run rate in a couple of weeks.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]
- The labs raced to "clawify": OpenAI released the Codex app and hired OpenClaw's creator; Anthropic shipped remote control (phone management of Claude Code sessions) and scheduled tasks in Co-work; Perplexity announced Perplexity Computer; Microsoft announced Copilot Tasks, with Satya Nadella reportedly using OpenClaw himself; Notion added custom agents.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]
- CNBC's Deirdre Bosa, expecting failure, built her own monday.com clone with Claude Co-work in an hour — plugged into calendar and Gmail, surfacing a forgotten kid's birthday.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]
- The SaaS apocalypse: every Anthropic plugin announcement cratered related stocks across games, productivity, finance, and legal; IBM saw its worst single-day drop in 25 years over a blog post about a months-old Cobol tool; Bloomberg and the WSJ dubbed dumping stocks "in AI's crosshairs" Wall Street's hot new trade.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]
- Citron Research's viral "2028 Global Intelligence Crisis" doom-loop piece landed in that caustic environment; Block's cut of 4,000 employees (~40% of staff) was cited as white-collar carnage evidence, amid debate over whether it was the biggest "AI washing" case yet.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]
- The Anthropic-Washington fight: Anthropic sought red-line carve-outs on autonomous weapons and domestic mass surveillance; the White House wanted "any lawful uses"; Trump and the Defense Secretary said the government would drop Anthropic and designate it a supply-chain risk, potentially forcing other contractors to sever ties. Senator Tom Tillis: "Why in the hell are we having this discussion in public?... This is sophomoric."[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]
- Model notes: ByteDance's Seedance 2.0 raised the question of Chinese open-weight models exceeding the US in video; DeepSeek 4 remained perpetually "next week"; Anthropic completed the 4.6 suite with Sonnet 4.6; Google shipped Gemini 3.1 Pro and the faster, cheaper Nano Banana 2.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]
- METR-style long-horizon task results shared by Meta showed Codex 5.3 high and Opus 4.6 "basically off the charts" — the key progress metric of last year can no longer keep up.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's summary of February: insiders raised their autonomy ambitions, broader white-collar workers were dragged into Claude Code, Co-work, and OpenClaw, Wall Street recognized "something big is happening" and turned jumpy — this time because AI might be too good rather than because of infrastructure-deal circularity — and the Anthropic-Pentagon "bun fight" opened a power struggle that is far from over. Heading into March, he flags rumors of GPT-5.4 and concludes 2026 is "off to a rollicking start," with AI now in uncharted territory beyond its own measurement tools.[^the-month-ai-woke-up--FJ7HiPBkCM]
