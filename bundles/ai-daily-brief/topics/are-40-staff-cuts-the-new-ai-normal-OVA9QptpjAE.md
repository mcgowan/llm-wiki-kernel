# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore examines Jack Dorsey's announcement that Block (formerly Square) is laying off 4,000 employees — a 40% reduction from over 10,000 to just under 6,000 — in what Whittemore calls one of the biggest percentage cuts in recent years, and one of the most direct examples yet of a CEO tying mass layoffs to AI. Dorsey's memo insisted the business is strong but that "intelligence tools... paired with smaller and flatter teams are enabling a new way of working which fundamentally changes what it means to build and run a company," and he chose one clean cut over the morale drain of continual layoffs. Whittemore notes Dorsey pointedly avoided the word "AI," and that Block's internal agent Goose had already spread from coding into sales, content, and project management work.[^are-40-staff-cuts-the-new-ai-normal-OVA9QptpjAE]

The episode weighs the loud skeptical response — that this is COVID over-hiring being unwound under an AI cover story, a phenomenon economist Alex Eames dubs "AI laundering" — against the market's 25%+ overnight surge and Dorsey's own claim that most companies are late, not early, to the same conclusion. Whittemore situates the story in what he calls a broader "recalibration moment": a chaotic collective repricing of everything as AI tools cross a critical capability threshold.[^are-40-staff-cuts-the-new-ai-normal-OVA9QptpjAE]

# Key points

- Dorsey announced the 4,000-person cut on Thursday, framing it as "one of the hardest decisions in the history of our company" while stressing gross profit growth and improving profitability — the change being AI-enabled ways of working, not business trouble.[^are-40-staff-cuts-the-new-ai-normal-OVA9QptpjAE]
- Block incubated the internal AI agent Goose, initially a coding harness, later used by non-technical teams: sales analyzing thousands of leads in hours, content teams automating asset management, and project managers cutting administrative time by 75%.[^are-40-staff-cuts-the-new-ai-normal-OVA9QptpjAE]
- Skeptics pounced: Austin Reif compared Block's headcount to Robinhood (2,500 employees, $70B market cap) and Coinbase (4,500, $50B) against Block's $30B; Will Slaughter noted Block tripled headcount from 3,900 to 12,500 between December 2019 and December 2022 and called AI "a much more appealing cover story" for managerial incompetence.[^are-40-staff-cuts-the-new-ai-normal-OVA9QptpjAE]
- Dorsey responded directly, admitting COVID-era over-hiring from running Square and Cash App as two company structures (corrected mid-2024), but arguing Block is now targeting $2M gross profit per person — 4x its flat pre-COVID $500K efficiency.[^are-40-staff-cuts-the-new-ai-normal-OVA9QptpjAE]
- Whittemore reviews the precedent set: Amazon's Jassy forecast AI-driven headcount shifts but never blamed AI for actual layoffs; Duolingo's von Ahn backtracked on contractor cuts; Klarna's ~40% reduction was attributed to attrition and partially reversed with contractors — so no clean prior example exists of AI-driven mass cuts that held up.[^are-40-staff-cuts-the-new-ai-normal-OVA9QptpjAE]
- Block stock soared more than 25% overnight — yet remains down 40% since the start of 2025 and more than 80% from its 2021 all-time high, still below its opening price for the year.[^are-40-staff-cuts-the-new-ai-normal-OVA9QptpjAE]
- On the earnings call Dorsey predicted the majority of companies will make similar structural changes within a year, and said "something happened in December of last year where the models got an order of magnitude more capable" — leaving only "an application gap."[^are-40-staff-cuts-the-new-ai-normal-OVA9QptpjAE]
- Balaji Srinivasan called it "the first AI cut" that "will send shockwaves" and urged workers to "get good now"; Isabella Kaminska warned of a doom loop where short-term stock gains outweigh long-term externalities; others predicted companies will copy the cut-and-blame-AI playbook.[^are-40-staff-cuts-the-new-ai-normal-OVA9QptpjAE]
- A Block developer-relations employee, Amanda, pushed back on the "not AI native" framing: everyone she met at Block was using AI at the forefront — "teams are getting leaner, period," and mastering the tooling alone won't protect anyone.[^are-40-staff-cuts-the-new-ai-normal-OVA9QptpjAE]

# Takeaways

Whittemore concludes we are in a recalibration moment: dramatic moves like a 40% cut, or an Anthropic plugin announcement wiping $40 off a company's market cap, happen not because anyone knows where things stand but because "we are unmoored" — in the midst of a chaotic repricing of everything as markets, workers, and companies grapple with tools that crossed a threshold in just the last few months. The bright side, he argues, is that the drama may shake people out of complacency. But he explicitly rejects efficiency cuts as AI's end game for work: this is a period — potentially a very painful one — to get through on the way to where the real opportunity lies.[^are-40-staff-cuts-the-new-ai-normal-OVA9QptpjAE]
