# Overview

In extended headlines devoted to "separating out fact from innuendo," Whittemore unpacks the resurfaced Economist quote in which Senator Mark Warner relayed NSA/Cyber Command chief General Joshua Rudd's claim that Mythos "broke into almost all of our classified systems not in weeks, but in hours." The reporter behind the quote cautioned against reading it literally, and follow-up reporting indicated it described an NSA red-team exercise under controlled conditions, not an actual breach — though NLW concludes the Fable ban clearly wasn't solely about the Amazon jailbreak. President Trump, meanwhile, struck a conciliatory tone on Axios ("Not now, but a week ago, maybe," on whether Amodei is a national security threat), explicitly ruling out the Defense Production Act. Other headlines: Nobel laureate John Jumper left DeepMind for Anthropic days after Noam Shazeer's exit, amid reports of plummeting DeepMind morale, and rumors swirled of a new Mythos model, Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6, and Fable 5's return.[^why-ai-users-are-raving-about-glm-5-2-HkVpSoaIKsc]

The main episode assesses second impressions of Z AI's GLM 5.2, which power users are calling a new "DeepSeek R1 moment" for open models: unlike prior Chinese releases that aced benchmarks and faded on contact with real usage, GLM 5.2 is winning praise from respected builders and beat Fable 5 on Design Arena's website category — while carrying real caveats on cost and generation speed.[^why-ai-users-are-raving-about-glm-5-2-HkVpSoaIKsc]

# Key points

- Peter Wildeford offered more plausible readings of the Warner/Rudd claim: a simulated exercise against replicas, code and architecture docs given up front, poorly secured internal IT described as "classified systems," or heavy tooling plus human expertise — while noting an AI compressing weeks of expert security research into hours is still a genuine threat.[^why-ai-users-are-raving-about-glm-5-2-HkVpSoaIKsc]
- DeepMind sources described frustration at falling to "a distant third or even fourth place," demoralization at GLM 5.2 overtaking Gemini 3.1 Pro on the Artificial Analysis index, four months without a flagship release, and a Gemini 3.5 Pro reportedly due June 30 that insiders called "not the step change we need"; Logan Kilpatrick pushed back that "everyone I know is hopeful and locked in."[^why-ai-users-are-raving-about-glm-5-2-HkVpSoaIKsc]
- Andrew Curran reported a new, more capable Mythos has "emerged from training" and made the underappreciated point that banning models from public serving does nothing to slow development — "The current continues to rage beneath the ice."[^why-ai-users-are-raving-about-glm-5-2-HkVpSoaIKsc]
- The DeepSeek R1 analogy: in January 2025 DeepSeek put a free reasoning model in a top App Store app, wiping $589B off Nvidia's market cap in a day — but its legacy was Chinese models that spike on benchmarks then fade; GLM 5.2's vibes are different, with Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch "genuinely impressed, almost shocked" and Itamar Golan calling it "a ChatGPT moment for public open models."[^why-ai-users-are-raving-about-glm-5-2-HkVpSoaIKsc]
- Design Arena found GLM 5.2 ranked first for websites (though behind Fable 5 on game dev, data viz, and 3D, and fourth on UI components), crediting better starting templates that avoid purple-gradient anti-patterns, natural use of Chart.js/Three.js, and Tailwind CSS in 91% of sessions versus 57% for Opus 4.8.[^why-ai-users-are-raving-about-glm-5-2-HkVpSoaIKsc]
- Cost caveats: GLM 5.2 outputs produced 25% more characters and roughly double Fable 5's generation time; Theo noted Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 (medium) are "cheaper and smarter"; self-hosting properly takes roughly eight H200s (~$400K to buy, ~$20K/month to rent) — NLW advises trying it via OpenRouter instead; OpenCode reported it hit sixth on their leaderboard in three days.[^why-ai-users-are-raving-about-glm-5-2-HkVpSoaIKsc]
- Elon Musk debated timelines for a full Chinese Mythos-class model, predicting Q1 by "true usefulness" even as Z AI said sooner on benchmarks; Aaron Levie called credible frontier-class open models "a huge win for the applied AI layer" — sovereign AI, post-training for specific workflows, and cost optimization.[^why-ai-users-are-raving-about-glm-5-2-HkVpSoaIKsc]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's conclusion: the past six weeks broke the idea that AI is a two-horse race between OpenAI and Anthropic (with a Google asterisk). Intensifying and costlier agentic workloads, plus the double-edged sword of frontier models attracting government restriction while slightly-behind models remain viable for many use cases, point to "an incredible potential flowering of new diverse model architectures" inside companies optimizing for speed, cost, or performance. His advice: don't race off core subscriptions or buy expensive hardware, but give some part of the organization a sandbox to experiment with alternative model stacks — "time and money well spent right now."[^why-ai-users-are-raving-about-glm-5-2-HkVpSoaIKsc]
