# Overview

Whittemore dissects Anthropic's first-ever Super Bowl commercials, a four-ad campaign that ignores Claude's capabilities entirely and instead satirizes OpenAI's planned ChatGPT ads: an AI counselor pivoting mid-advice into a dating-site plug, a muscle-bound AI trainer upselling insoles for "short kings," each ad opening with a title card — betrayal, violation, treachery, deception — and closing on "Ads are coming, but not to Claude." OpenAI, rather than shrugging it off, hit back hard: CMO Kate Rouch accused Anthropic of believing AI "should be tightly controlled in small rooms in San Francisco and Davos," and Sam Altman wrote a 400-plus-word response calling the campaign "Anthropic double speak" and labeling Anthropic an authoritarian company, both citing that ChatGPT has more free users in Texas than Claude has globally.[^no-one-wins-this-ai-super-bowl-ObFeNCxeFvs]

Whittemore criticizes both sides — OpenAI for abandoning market-leader poise, but Anthropic more, for a campaign he calls out of character, contextless, and corrosive to public trust in AI as a whole.[^no-one-wins-this-ai-super-bowl-ObFeNCxeFvs]

# Key points

- All four Anthropic ads target OpenAI's forthcoming ads rather than showcasing Claude; one even copies a shot from the previous year's Sora commercials.[^no-one-wins-this-ai-super-bowl-ObFeNCxeFvs]
- OpenAI CMO Kate Rouch: "Real betrayal isn't ads, it's control" — framing Anthropic as elitist; Altman escalated to calling Anthropic authoritarian, and OpenAI is expected to run its own Super Bowl ad.[^no-one-wins-this-ai-super-bowl-ObFeNCxeFvs]
- Whittemore on OpenAI's response: "this isn't how a market leader responds," invoking Don Draper's "I don't think about you at all" — the leader either ignores it or keeps it playful, and doesn't escalate to authoritarianism claims without sleeping on it.[^no-one-wins-this-ai-super-bowl-ObFeNCxeFvs]
- He concedes the ads are funny and notes roughly 90% of top-rated Super Bowl ads each year are humor-based — but argues the campaign fails because it critiques a pain (ChatGPT ads) that doesn't exist yet and that general audiences lack context for.[^no-one-wins-this-ai-super-bowl-ObFeNCxeFvs]
- His deeper objection: many viewers will like the ads because they confirm suspicions that AI is just tech billionaires' latest scheme — Anthropic is feeding an industry-wide critique in a US already more AI-skeptical than nearly any other country, leaving people unprepared for the "tidal wave coming to shore."[^no-one-wins-this-ai-super-bowl-ObFeNCxeFvs]
- He also calls the campaign the opposite of the brand Anthropic spent three years building — "petty, small," telling none of the stories behind Claude's recent insurgency.[^no-one-wins-this-ai-super-bowl-ObFeNCxeFvs]
- Postscript: the ads weren't even Anthropic's biggest impact that week — that came when a Claude Code plugin wiped billions of dollars off global markets.[^no-one-wins-this-ai-super-bowl-ObFeNCxeFvs]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's verdict is that no one wins this exchange: OpenAI looked rattled rather than dominant, and Anthropic undermined its own carefully built brand while stoking public cynicism about AI generally. He calls it "a big L for the entire industry" — though not one that will stop him from "using Claude Code for 24 hours a day."[^no-one-wins-this-ai-super-bowl-ObFeNCxeFvs]
