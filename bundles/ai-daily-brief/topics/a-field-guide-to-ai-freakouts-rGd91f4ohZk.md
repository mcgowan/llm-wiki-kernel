# Overview

In a single-theme episode prompted by the latest round of AI FUD — fear that cheap Chinese models like Moonshot's Kimi K3 will undercut OpenAI's and Anthropic's revenue just as both position to go public — Whittemore builds a "field guide" to the recurring patterns of AI market freakouts he has watched for nearly four years. The news hook: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent proposed sanctions on Chinese AI firms over "covert industrial-scale distillation attacks," and OSTP director Michael Kratsios made the first specific government allegation that Moonshot distilled Anthropic's Fable to build K3, using a platform that switched access methods to avoid detection and GB300 servers accessed in Thailand — while Wired reports the administration is split, with Commerce Secretary Lutnick preferring to fight Chinese AI with competition and incentives for American open source rather than "unworkable" restrictions.[^a-field-guide-to-ai-freakouts-rGd91f4ohZk]

The stakes are macro: AI investment represents 25% of US GDP growth per Bloomberg — the largest single-sector contribution in history — and JP Morgan attributes 75% of S&P 500 returns, 80% of earnings growth, and 90% of capital-spending growth since ChatGPT to AI, with roughly half the S&P 500 now AI or AI-exposed. Whittemore's contrarian conclusion: the market's determination to find a bubble at all times is precisely what keeps a true runaway bubble from forming.[^a-field-guide-to-ai-freakouts-rGd91f4ohZk]

# Key points

- Freakout type 1 — cheap models undercutting premium revenue: the current K3 scare echoes the DeepSeek moment of early 2025, when markets wrongly believed a Chinese lab had matched frontier AI for millions; in reality K3 is served at about a third of Fable's price or half of Opus's — meaningful savings, but not the "pennies on the dollar" many analysts imagine.[^a-field-guide-to-ai-freakouts-rGd91f4ohZk]
- Freakout type 2 — circular financing: Nvidia's investment in OpenAI (closed at $30B in February, alongside $50B from Amazon and $30B from SoftBank) revived dot-com vendor-financing analogies, but Whittemore notes key differences: OpenAI pays cash for GPUs, so an OpenAI failure leaves Nvidia with devalued stock, not a balance-sheet hole — unlike Cisco-era vendor financing, which was outlawed in the early 2000s.[^a-field-guide-to-ai-freakouts-rGd91f4ohZk]
- Freakout type 3 — revenue not growing fast enough to justify spend, with its twin, capex growing too fast: Google beat with 24% overall growth and 82% cloud growth that week, yet the stock fell 1.2% because capex guidance crossed the psychological "$200 billion do-not-cross line"; hyperscalers have raised capex guidance every quarter for three years, with combined capex heading past $1 trillion next year.[^a-field-guide-to-ai-freakouts-rGd91f4ohZk]
- Freakout type 4 — limits on AI spend: last year's version was the "absolute garbage" MIT 95%-of-pilots-fail report; this year's is token caps (Uber capping users at $1,500/month, Tesla at $200/week with request-based increases) — though most knowledge workers are nowhere near those budgets, leaving enormous growth headroom.[^a-field-guide-to-ai-freakouts-rGd91f4ohZk]
- Freakout type 5 — performance plateaus: the fall-2024 pre-training-wall narrative now looks "quaint, ridiculous, insane" after o1 revealed reasoning as a new performance vector and Fable 5 proved pre-training hadn't stalled.[^a-field-guide-to-ai-freakouts-rGd91f4ohZk]
- Caveat 1 — seasonality: FUD reliably peaks in summer; Morgan Stanley noted momentum stocks fell 40% that month, the worst on record (previous worst: 29% in early 2021), and Goldman reported record hedge-fund tech selling.[^a-field-guide-to-ai-freakouts-rGd91f4ohZk]
- Caveat 2 — bubble talk as pressure valve: constant Big Short-style bubble logic acts as a release that prevents 1999-2000-style frenzy; late-2025 bubble talk dissipated when agentic spend (employees consuming hundreds or thousands of dollars monthly) showed this isn't just another SaaS category.[^a-field-guide-to-ai-freakouts-rGd91f4ohZk]
- Why this round resolves: cheap models don't matter without inference capacity — Moonshot was "completely tapped out of compute on opening weekend"; data centers are slow to permit and build (half announced this year canceled or delayed — a construction problem, not a demand signal); and a flood of routers, fine-tuning plays, and verticalized post-training companies is distributing AI's revenue gains beyond two labs.[^a-field-guide-to-ai-freakouts-rGd91f4ohZk]
- Investor Nick Carter's framing, quoted at length: "The US government does not owe either of the large labs a business model" — even if token economics fail for OpenAI and Anthropic in their current forms, hyperscalers, neoclouds, enterprises, and consumers all benefit from cheap digital cognition, and every frontier token will likely sell at a premium for at least five years.[^a-field-guide-to-ai-freakouts-rGd91f4ohZk]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's thesis is that AI market freakouts are patterned, seasonal, and ultimately self-correcting: "rational concern followed by evidence to the contrary." He predicts a never-ending sequence of new FUD rounds, frequently coinciding with market seasonality, but argues these consistent panics reduce the likelihood of a full bubble forming. The current China scare, he expects, will fade as investors recognize that demand for intelligence vastly exceeds what's being consumed, that infrastructure capacity can barely grow as fast as demand, and that Chinese labs collectively can't serve a fraction of US-scale usage — while warning that data-center builders' failure to address community concerns is a self-inflicted brake on the buildout.[^a-field-guide-to-ai-freakouts-rGd91f4ohZk]
