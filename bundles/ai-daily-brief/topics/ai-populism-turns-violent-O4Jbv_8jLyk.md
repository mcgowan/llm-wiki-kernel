# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore covers a week of anti-AI political violence: at 4 a.m. Friday, 20-year-old Daniel Moreno Gamma threw a Molotov cocktail at Sam Altman's home and was later arrested outside OpenAI headquarters with an anti-AI manifesto, kerosene, and a lighter; an FBI raid found a document listing names and addresses of other AI executives. Two days later, a separate pair allegedly fired a gun at Altman's home. Moreno Gamma — who posted in the Pause AI Discord as "Butlerian Jihadist" — faces 11 charges including attempted murder, with prosecutors saying the attack could be treated as domestic terrorism.[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]

Whittemore surveys the ensuing discourse — blame directed at x-risk rhetoric, at the media, and at the AI industry's own doomerism — but argues all of it misses the forest for the trees: AI has become the "perfect cauldron" for a much larger pipeline running from real economic pain through perceived inequality to political violence, drawing on radicalization research to sketch what de-escalation would actually take.[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]

# Key points

- Altman responded with a late-night post sharing a family photo, stating beliefs (AI must be democratized, "power cannot be too concentrated," safety requires "a society-wide response"), admitting he is "a flawed person in the center of an exceptionally complex situation," and offering a Ring-of-Power analogy: the only solution is "for no one to have the ring" — via individual empowerment and democratic systems staying in control.[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]
- One discourse strand blamed x-risk ideology: Jordan Schachtel argued doomers "built a radical ideology" whose utilitarian math generates violent conclusions; skeptics like "Accelerate Harder" noted safetyists' objections rest on violence being ineffective — "a cost-benefit analysis," not genuine rejection.[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]
- Prominent safety voices condemned the attacks: Geoffrey Hinton ("please do not effing do it"), David Krueger (terrorism would trigger crackdowns and securitization), Nate Soares, and Pause AI itself.[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]
- Journalists objected to Altman's implication that Ronan Farrow's New Yorker piece contributed; Altman walked it back ("bad word choice... I'm not thinking the most clearly"). Others, including Mike Solana, blasted media for publishing photos and locations of Altman's home after two attacks.[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]
- A third strand blamed industry doomerism: Casey Newton wrote the public's disdain "was co-created by the people building the systems who have consistently told us that it is imminent and dangerous."[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]
- Whittemore situates the attacks in a broader pattern of celebrated elite victimization — Instagram comments cheering the attack (thousands of likes), the Titan submersible mockery, and Luigi Mangione's folk-hero status, with an Emerson poll finding 41% of 18–29-year-olds called killing a CEO somewhat or completely acceptable.[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]
- Four days before Altman's attack, Indianapolis councilman Ron Gibson had 13 rounds fired at his door with a "No data centers left" note; the Soufan Center warned in November that anti-AI resistance could turn violent.[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]
- Research he cites: perceived inequality drives radicalization more than actual inequality (DARE project; a 141-publication systematic review); a 2022 Journal of Conflict Resolution paper found projected economic decline — the "domain of loss" — motivates violence, directly relevant to AI job anxiety; real grievances include homes up 60% since 2019 and the top 1% owning 31.7% of US wealth.[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]
- What doesn't work: Rachel Kleinfeld's Carnegie review shows reducing polarization doesn't reduce violence support ("you cannot kumbaya your way out of this"), and Whittemore argues UBI from the people automating your job "ratifies" the domain of loss rather than addressing it — a dignity-destroying moral-typecasting dynamic.[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]
- What works: political efficacy (credible democratic channels for AI governance — possibly meaning the industry accepting meaningful regulation) and credibly improving economic trajectory, including what he calls a missing "Marshall Plan for AI education, reskilling, empowerment, entrepreneurial development."[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]

# Takeaways

Whittemore concludes that AI concentrates every downstream economic grievance simultaneously — job displacement anxiety, visible wealth concentration, existential-risk rhetoric as a moral urgency multiplier, and AI leaders "saying the quiet part loud" about job losses. De-escalation therefore cannot come merely from cooler rhetoric; it requires addressing the actual ingredients: the democratic deficit, economic trajectory, and moral urgency. And the people best positioned to do that, he notes, are the ones who have historically least wanted to. "It will not be easy, but I don't believe we have another choice."[^ai-populism-turns-violent-O4Jbv_8jLyk]
