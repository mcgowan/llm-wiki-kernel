# Overview

In this headlines edition, Whittemore rounds out Big Tech earnings season with Google and Amazon delivering a unified message: AI capex is accelerating faster than ever. Google guided $175-185 billion for the year (vastly above the $115 billion estimate and roughly double its $91 billion 2025 capex), and Amazon topped it with $200 billion guidance for 2026, a 60% jump. With Microsoft and Meta also lifting expectations, the four hyperscalers alone now project $650 billion in 2026 AI capex — more than the inflation-adjusted cost of the entire multi-decade US Interstate Highway project spent in a single year, about two and a half Apollo programs or four and a half International Space Stations.[^650-billion-on-ai-capex-gOTLCBfuDnQ]

Markets punished both stocks anyway, and Whittemore highlights an underappreciated reading: investors may be objecting less to AI spending per se than to the end of stock buybacks — capex means the money "is not there to spend on them." The episode also covers Amazon's rumored $50 billion investment in OpenAI with privileged model access for Alexa, Gemini hitting 750 million monthly active users, ElevenLabs raising $500 million at an $11 billion valuation, and OpenAI's launch of Frontier, an orchestration, governance, and optimization platform for deploying AI co-workers.[^650-billion-on-ai-capex-gOTLCBfuDnQ]

# Key points

- Google: first-ever $400 billion annual revenue, 18% overall growth, cloud up 48% to $17.7 billion for the quarter — still third behind Azure and AWS but the fastest-growing and the only hyperscaler accelerating its pace of growth.[^650-billion-on-ai-capex-gOTLCBfuDnQ]
- Amazon: $21.2 billion net profit in line with expectations, revenue up 13.6% to $213.4 billion, AWS up 24% (fastest in 3 years) to $35.6 billion; CEO Andy Jassy defended the capex ramp as "an extraordinarily unusual opportunity to forever change the size of AWS and Amazon," insisting "this isn't some sort of quixotic top-line grab."[^650-billion-on-ai-capex-gOTLCBfuDnQ]
- Both companies (like Microsoft) claimed cloud growth was capacity constrained — more GPUs on racks in 2025 would have meant stronger growth — yet Google fell 6% and Amazon 11% after their calls.[^650-billion-on-ai-capex-gOTLCBfuDnQ]
- Hyperscaler buybacks peaked at over $1 trillion in 2023; most analysts expect buybacks to shrink or end this year, and capex plans look likely to require debt funding across the board for the first time. Commentators noted Stanley Druckenmiller's argument that high corporate capex drains liquidity from financial markets.[^650-billion-on-ai-capex-gOTLCBfuDnQ]
- Amazon is in talks to invest as much as $50 billion in OpenAI's latest round — about half of what OpenAI seeks — and per The Information wants privileged access to OpenAI tech, including post-trained models powering Alexa with dedicated OpenAI researchers; OpenAI's spokesperson only reaffirmed "our strong existing compute partnership with Amazon."[^650-billion-on-ai-capex-gOTLCBfuDnQ]
- Gemini hit 750 million monthly active users (app-only counting, per Google), up from 650 million in December and 450 million at the start of that quarter; Sensor Tower put ChatGPT at 110 million MAU as of November, and Meta AI's claimed 500 million presumably includes incidental Instagram/WhatsApp encounters.[^650-billion-on-ai-capex-gOTLCBfuDnQ]
- ElevenLabs raised $500 million at an $11 billion valuation — triple its January prior-year round — with plans to expand from audio into video and agents.[^650-billion-on-ai-capex-gOTLCBfuDnQ]
- OpenAI Frontier helps businesses build, deploy, and manage AI agents "that can do real work" — shared context, onboarding, feedback-based learning, permissions and boundaries; OpenAI argues what slows agentic deployment isn't model intelligence but how agents are built and run, with the capability gap in live deployments growing due to governance complexity.[^650-billion-on-ai-capex-gOTLCBfuDnQ]
- Frontier's architecture diagram — enterprise systems of record at the bottom with business context, agent execution, evaluation, agents, and interfaces layered above — alarmed SaaS watchers: Gokul Rajaram called it the clearest representation of AI companies building "systems of action" atop systems of record, and Buko Capital summarized it as "your system of record is a dumb pipe... no wonder SaaS is in the gutter."[^650-billion-on-ai-capex-gOTLCBfuDnQ]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's key interpretive point is about reading market reactions correctly: the sell-offs following blowout capex guidance may signal not that investors think AI spending is wrong, but that they resent losing buybacks — "they just don't like that it's not there to spend on them" — a lens he urges listeners to keep as the debate plays out. He flags Frontier as a story deserving far more time, promising to return to it, and notes its layered diagram explains "why the markets are so worried about the future of software companies."[^650-billion-on-ai-capex-gOTLCBfuDnQ]
