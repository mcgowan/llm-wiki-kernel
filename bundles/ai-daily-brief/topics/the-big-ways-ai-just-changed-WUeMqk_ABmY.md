# Overview

Recorded over July 4th weekend with Fable 5 having just come back online, Nathaniel Whittemore argues June was "one of the most significant months in the post-ChatGPT history of AI," pairing it with May as two angles on one story: the shift from the AI subsidy era to token scarcity. June opened with that shift becoming concrete — Walmart moving to token budgets, Uber capping AI spend at $1,500/employee/month, token leaderboards being switched off — then escalated dramatically when Anthropic released Fable 5 on June 10. The model was immediately, clearly more powerful, especially at completing (not just starting) complex projects; but within days the US government invoked an export control directive demanding suspension of Fable 5 and Mythos 5 access for foreign nationals, and Anthropic shut it down for everyone. GPT-5.6 was delayed too, with the government approving access in waves — what many saw as "a messy ad-hoc AI licensing regime."[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]

The forced pause added a sovereignty dimension to the existing cost rationale for diversifying beyond closed frontier models. Z.ai's GLM 5.2 delivered what Whittemore calls the first legitimate "DeepSeek moment" since early 2025 — an open-weight model exceeding the Opus 4.6/GPT 5.2 level that launched the agentic era — while multi-model architectures (Harvey/Fireworks pairing a GLM worker with an Opus advisor, OpenRouter's Fusion panel-and-judge) and harness developments like Claude Tag filled the vacuum. Local AI became a genuine boardroom question for the first time in the show's history.[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]

# Key points

- Token discipline became real: Walmart shifted internal tools from unlimited use to token budgets, Uber set a $1,500/month per-employee cap, and companies turned off token leaderboards.[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]
- Fable 5 (released June 10) broke the pattern of underwhelming numerical jumps: Whittemore says it eliminated "completion energy," letting him finally finish long-stalled projects like the new AI Daily Brief website in one sweep; one story had it building a requested product feature during a live customer call.[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]
- Early friction: aggressive biology guardrails and a 30-day retention policy for Mythos-class prompts and outputs that made many enterprises refuse to use it — a preview of concentration-of-power concerns.[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]
- The US government used an export control directive to force suspension of Fable 5/Mythos 5 for foreign nationals; Anthropic said full shutdown was the only way to comply. A narrow jailbreak report from Amazon reportedly triggered the action, catalyzing a broader government "catch-up" on model power.[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]
- GPT-5.6 was delayed and split into three models, with the government approving each wave of access — an improvised licensing regime with no clear legal precedent.[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]
- GLM 5.2 became the first open-weight model making fallback "feel less like compromise and more like genuine competition for the frontier"; post-trains like Cursor's Composer 2.5 (built off Kimi) and integrated multi-model systems gained traction.[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]
- Harvey and Fireworks paired an open-weight GLM worker with an Opus advisor for legal tasks, beating Opus alone at a fraction of the cost; OpenRouter's Fusion used a model panel, judge, and synthesizer.[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]
- Claude Tag let anyone in Slack invoke Claude Code — Anthropic said 65% of its product team's code was now produced by initiating Claude Code from Slack — shifting AI from individual to group experience.[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]
- Microsoft quietly launched ground-up proprietary models plus a product post-training models to individual enterprise requirements — a story Whittemore calls "wildly under discussed"; Satya Nadella argued every company needs a compounding learning loop around AI usage.[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]
- Infrastructure realignments: memory companies outperformed as shortages loomed; SpaceX expanded its Anthropic compute deal to Google and Reflection AI, with Meta reportedly following into "accidental neocloud" territory; data centers kept brewing as a left-right political issue (Erin Brockovich and former Tea Party conservatives both mobilizing).[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]
- For average-adoption enterprises, a Glean report identified "bot sitting" — workers spending 6.4 hours/week feeding agents context, checking outputs, and rerunning results; KPMG found companies with CEO-accountable AI were over twice as likely to report meaningful business value.[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]

# Takeaways

Whittemore frames 2026's arc: the beginning was the explosion of real agentic use cases, the middle was recognizing the consequences of that capacity, and the rest of the year will be "figuring it out from here." Lasting legacies of June include an unresolved, improvised AI licensing regime (with more advanced models waiting in the wings) and a permanently shifted Overton window on frontier-model lock-in — sovereignty now joining cost as a reason to diversify architectures. His near-term advice: with Fable back and much of the corporate world checked out for summer, July and August offer a unique window to race ahead by learning what this new model class can do.[^the-big-ways-ai-just-changed-WUeMqk_ABmY]
