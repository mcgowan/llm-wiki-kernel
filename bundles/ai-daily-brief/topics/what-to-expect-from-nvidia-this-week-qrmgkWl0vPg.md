# Overview

This Headlines edition previews Nvidia's GTC developer conference in San Jose, where speculation centers on the first collaborative product since Nvidia's December acquisition of Groq: Groq's language-processing chips integrated into Nvidia's rack-scale servers — Nvidia's first product aimed directly at inference demand rather than training. Notably, production is ramping at Samsung's foundry (Nvidia's first AI chip made outside TSMC), the servers use Intel CPUs, OpenAI is expected as a launch buyer, and Groq technology may eventually be built into the Feynman GPU generation after Rubin. Analyst Patrick Moorhead frames the shift: "Nvidia is no longer a chip company" but "a full-stack heterogeneous AI infrastructure platform."[^what-to-expect-from-nvidia-this-week-qrmgkWl0vPg]

The rest of the episode covers Nscale's 2GW West Virginia data-center acquisition talks, the surge in SEC filings naming AI agents as a material business risk, ByteDance pausing SeeDance 2.0's global launch over Hollywood copyright disputes, ex-Anthropic founders raising for science startup Mirindal, Google's Ask Maps, and ServiceNow CEO Bill McDermott's warning that young-professional unemployment could exceed 30%.[^what-to-expect-from-nvidia-this-week-qrmgkWl0vPg]

# Key points

- The Information described the expected GTC announcement as Groq chips in Nvidia rack-scale servers; mass production at Samsung begins in the second half of the year, diversifying supply chains out of Taiwan, with OpenAI expected as a buyer and multiple hardware generations planned toward the Feynman GPUs.[^what-to-expect-from-nvidia-this-week-qrmgkWl0vPg]
- UK-based neocloud Nscale is negotiating to acquire a West Virginia data-center site targeting 2 gigawatts by 2027 — unusual since neoclouds typically rent — which would triple its 2027 revenue projections to $30 billion ahead of an IPO, with ByteDance in talks as a tenant.[^what-to-expect-from-nvidia-this-week-qrmgkWl0vPg]
- 27 firms have listed AI agents as a material risk in SEC filings this year, up from seven a year earlier — including Figma, Workday, and HubSpot, whose CEOs publicly downplayed the threat; Figma's 10-K conceded agentic AI may "reduce reliance on traditional software applications."[^what-to-expect-from-nvidia-this-week-qrmgkWl0vPg]
- ByteDance mothballed SeeDance 2.0's global launch (planned for mid-March via BytePlus API and a consumer app) after cease-and-desists from Disney, Warner Bros., Paramount, and Netflix; MPA CEO Charles Rivkin accused it of "unauthorized use of US copyrighted works on a massive scale." The viral Tom Cruise/Brad Pitt fistfight clip showed high-fidelity replication of real actors.[^what-to-expect-from-nvidia-this-week-qrmgkWl0vPg]
- The engineering holdup is refining guardrails so they don't over-block: Chinese users report the model now rejects prompts unrelated to copyrighted content — the same problem OpenAI faced with Sora.[^what-to-expect-from-nvidia-this-week-qrmgkWl0vPg]
- Mirindal, led by former Anthropic researchers Beth Nemesh and Harsh Mehta (long-horizon scientific reasoning, automated AI research), is in talks to raise $175M at a $1B valuation — unicorn status in a seed round — for AI-enhanced research in biology and materials science.[^what-to-expect-from-nvidia-this-week-qrmgkWl0vPg]
- Google launched Ask Maps, a Gemini-powered conversational interface in Google Maps that answers landmark questions, plans multi-stop trips, integrates Gemini's memory of user preferences, and adds a 3D navigation view.[^what-to-expect-from-nvidia-this-week-qrmgkWl0vPg]
- ServiceNow CEO Bill McDermott told CNBC that college-graduate unemployment could "easily go into the mid-30s in the next couple of years" as agents take work; current Fed data shows 5.6% unemployment for recent grads but 42.5% underemployment — the highest since 2020 — with CS majors at 7% unemployment but only 19.1% underemployment.[^what-to-expect-from-nvidia-this-week-qrmgkWl0vPg]

# Takeaways

Whittemore cautions that SEC risk disclosures shouldn't be read too literally, but argues the quadrupling volume signals the industry has "moved past the tipping point on agents" — software executives, or at least their lawyers, now take agent disruption seriously. He expects AI-for-science neo-labs like Mirindal to be a continuing trend through the year, and reads the SeeDance saga as illustrating that blocking copyrighted content is easy while doing so without frustrating users is the hard engineering problem. The McDermott jobs warning tees up the main episode's question of why job-loss discourse is so potent right now.[^what-to-expect-from-nvidia-this-week-qrmgkWl0vPg]
