# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore covers a wave of strategic consolidation at OpenAI as it responds to an insurgent Anthropic dominating the enterprise and coding conversation. Six months after its December "code red," OpenAI is doubling down: Sam Altman is narrowing his own role to capital raising, supply chains, and the data center buildout (with safety folded into Mark Chen's research org and security under Greg Brockman), applications CEO Fidji Simo is taking the commercial driver's seat with her product division renamed "AGI Deployment," and the company plans a desktop super app combining ChatGPT, Codex, and the Alice browser. Altman's memo also revealed pre-training is finished on a model code-named Spud that he claims "can really accelerate the economy."[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]

The most discussed casualty of the side-quest purge: Sora is being sunset entirely, with all video-model products discontinued — largely due to compute constraints, with the freed resources redeployed to run Spud. Whittemore calls it the first time OpenAI has so publicly chosen not to pursue a clear ambition because of compute scarcity and competitive necessity. The episode closes with the AGI-definition debate, where Whittemore offers his own frame: we already have "task AGI," and what remains contested is stringing tasks together without human oversight.[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]

# Key points

- Fidji Simo confirmed the refocus publicly: "when new bets start to work, like we're seeing now with Codex, it's very important to double down on them and avoid distractions"; Codex is now a real choice alongside Claude Code for many builders.[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]
- Altman told staff pre-training is complete on Spud, promising a "very strong model" in a few weeks that can "accelerate the economy" — phrasing Whittemore notes risks the overpromising OpenAI has avoided since GPT-5's misaligned expectations, though it may be aimed at rallying the troops.[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]
- Sora's shutdown was driven by compute: WSJ reported staff were surprised how compute-hungry the app was relative to demand; the freed compute goes to running Spud.[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]
- Disney canceled its partnership and its planned billion-dollar investment in OpenAI following Sora's end, though the split was amicable.[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]
- Reactions split between grave-dancing ("AI TikTok" critics glad the GPUs will do real work) and defenses: Machine Cinema's Min Do noted over a hundred companies now compete in AI video versus just Midjourney and Runway when Sora was announced, and Dax argued failed experiments are how anything valuable gets figured out.[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]
- No job cuts: Bill Peebles' Sora research team moves to world-model research — "learning to simulate arbitrary environments at high fidelity" — with robotics and "automating the physical economy" as the prize.[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]
- The ads push survives for now: former Meta exec Dave Duggan hired as VP of global ad solutions, ads rolling out to all free and Go subscribers — but ad buyers complain about minimal metrics and no modern sales platform; instant checkout is being pared back in favor of merchant-run checkout and product discovery.[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]
- Simon Smith's framing of the stakes: why fight for a $680B ad market dominated by incumbents when a roughly $40-trillion-plus market of automatable knowledge work sits largely untapped?[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]
- AGI debate: Jensen Huang told Lex Fridman "we've achieved AGI" for novelty software (an AI could build a briefly viral app now, though "the odds of 100,000 of those agents building Nvidia is 0%"); Benjamin Todd's essay concluded current AI is "impressively general, but not yet AGI."[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]
- Whittemore's "task AGI" frame: AI does almost any specific, discrete task well; capability breaks down on long strings of tasks without human intervention — which is where AGI definitions become debatable; he cites Ethan Mollick's quip to retroactively declare o3 AGI "so we can stop arguing about it," since AGI alone isn't enough for transformation.[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's conclusion: the labs' partnerships with consulting and private equity firms show they know AGI-capable models still require enormous diffusion work inside big companies. But the through-line of OpenAI's moves — and Anthropic's relentless shipping — is that "right now, more than ever for AI companies, the only type of AGI that matters to them is work AGI." The Sora shutdown demonstrates just how high the stakes of compute-allocation decisions have become: OpenAI publicly abandoned a much-hyped product to feed the machine that reinvents knowledge work.[^work-agi-is-the-only-agi-that-matters-gmkURB_HmQI]
