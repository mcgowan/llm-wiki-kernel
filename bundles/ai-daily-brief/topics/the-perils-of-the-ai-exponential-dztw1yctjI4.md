# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore examines the update to METR's "Moore's Law for AI agents" chart — which he notes became "the most important chart in the economy" as the bulwark against AI-bubble narratives — now that Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.3 Codex have been added. Opus 4.6 posted a time horizon of roughly 14.5 hours at the standard 50% success rate, the largest generational jump in METR's study, more than tripling Opus 4.5's 4-hour-49-minute result and implying the doubling rate has compressed from the original 7 months to about 1.5 months. Whittemore carefully explains what the metric actually measures (task difficulty in comparative human time, not how long an agent runs) and relays METR's own heavy caveats: task-set saturation, a confidence-interval upper bound of 98 hours, and researcher David Rein's warning that a slightly different task distribution could have yielded 8 or 20 hours.[^the-perils-of-the-ai-exponential-dztw1yctjI4]

He pairs this with the viral reception of Citrini Research's "2028 Global Intelligence Crisis" note — a doom-loop scenario of capital owners reaping AI's benefits while workers across every stratum are left jobless, ending in market collapse and mass immiseration. What interests Whittemore is less the piece's particulars than its reception: unlike earlier doomer sci-fi, it met widespread acceptance, revealing how many investors already believe some version of the thesis.[^the-perils-of-the-ai-exponential-dztw1yctjI4]

# Key points

- METR's study, first released in March of the prior year when Sonnet 3.7 was the frontier, found agentic task time horizons doubling roughly every 7 months back to GPT-2 — with late-2024/early-2025 models suggesting doubling as fast as 3 months.[^the-perils-of-the-ai-exponential-dztw1yctjI4]
- Methodology clarified: a task solved by a human in 2 hours has a 2-hour horizon even if Claude solves it in 2 minutes; the headline metric requires only 50% success (an 80% variant yields much lower horizons) — it measures the capability frontier's movement, not production reliability.[^the-perils-of-the-ai-exponential-dztw1yctjI4]
- Recent trajectory: GPT-5.1 Codex at 2h40m (November, barely above GPT-5), Opus 4.5 at 4h49m (December, nearly a doubling alone — validated by developer "vibes," per Swyx), then GPT-5.3 Codex at 6.5 hours and Opus 4.6 at ~14.5 hours.[^the-perils-of-the-ai-exponential-dztw1yctjI4]
- The Opus 4.6 result implies a 1.5-month doubling rate; investor Nic Carter: "This is the most important chart in the world and it's going absolutely ballistic" — even Bernie Sanders cited it at Stanford.[^the-perils-of-the-ai-exponential-dztw1yctjI4]
- Caveats: Dean Ball notes METR has signaled decreasing confidence due to saturation and scarce long-duration tasks; METR flagged scaffolding issues on the Codex results and said Opus 4.6 has "basically saturated" the task set, with the confidence interval's upper bound at 98 hours; METR is updating its methodology.[^the-perils-of-the-ai-exponential-dztw1yctjI4]
- The balanced read Whittemore endorses: something massive may really be happening even if some people mistakenly think it's bigger than it is — "that doesn't mean it's actually not very, very big."[^the-perils-of-the-ai-exponential-dztw1yctjI4]
- Citrini's "2028 Global Intelligence Crisis" applies Dario Amodei's "country of geniuses in a data center" to the real economy: AI consumes the whole economy, households give way to a capital-based society, markets crash, unemployment soars.[^the-perils-of-the-ai-exponential-dztw1yctjI4]
- Felix Salmon: the piece "smashes the common knowledge game" — its ideas weren't new, but now "everyone knows everyone knows"; another commentator predicted it hits LinkedIn in 2 weeks and the WSJ/FT in 4.[^the-perils-of-the-ai-exponential-dztw1yctjI4]
- Critiques: Dan Hockenmaier argues the piece profoundly misunderstands marketplace defensibility (liquid supply, optimization moats — not app-building — protect DoorDash and Uber Eats); economist Guy Berger asks why agent owners' profits wouldn't fuel employment, GDP, and stock prices.[^the-perils-of-the-ai-exponential-dztw1yctjI4]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's conclusion is that the individual pieces — dramatically improved coding models, agents becoming a real force, impact spreading from software engineering to everything, and markets beginning to reprice — add up to a broad-based sense that "something big is happening," with nothing visibly decelerating. He treats the Citrini phenomenon as an anthropological study of sentiment rather than a forecast, flags confirmation bias in its acceptance, and says the discourse "desperately" needs a non-doomer counterpart to the piece — something he is working on in the background.[^the-perils-of-the-ai-exponential-dztw1yctjI4]
