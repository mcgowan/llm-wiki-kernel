# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore argues a significant secular shift is underway: when you pay for AI, you will increasingly pay what the AI actually costs. Even $200/month plans often haven't covered the cost to serve power users, and the agentic era's explosion in token consumption has forced a price reckoning. He traces the arc from early-2026's mass recognition that the agentic era had arrived (SemiAnalysis calling Claude Code "the inflection point"), through Anthropic straining under its own success — outages, metering, forcing Open Claw usage onto the API, withholding its largest model — to GitHub Copilot's landmark shift to consumption-based billing with roughly 6x price hikes on frontier coding models. Commentators compare the moment to the 2010s "millennial lifestyle subsidy," though Whittemore notes AI differs because it's woven into how people actually work.[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]

Beyond markets — where he considers Wall Street "fundamentally behind," reacting to months-old OpenAI data while Codex users grew 20x this year — Whittemore's more interesting implications are for jobs and enterprises: the job-apocalypse discourse rarely accounts for the actual cost of intelligence, and physics-imposed compute constraints may slow AI diffusion more effectively than any protest or pause letter. He closes with five practical enterprise moves for the new cost era.[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]

# Key points

- GitHub announced consumption-based Copilot billing starting June: Claude Opus 4.7's multiplier jumped from 7.5x to 27x, and Gemini 3.1 Pro and GPT 5.3 Codex from 1x to 6x — roughly a 6x hike across frontier coding models, revealing "how deep their subsidies had been."[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]
- GitHub CPO Mario Rodriguez: Copilot has evolved from in-editor assistant to agentic platform; "a quick chat question in a multi-hour autonomous coding session can cost the user the same amount," making the premium-request model unsustainable.[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]
- Anthropic's strain: frequent outages, peak-hour metering, admitting performance-degrading changes, pushing Open Claw usage to the API, testing removal of Claude Code from the Pro plan, and withholding its most capable model (Ben Thompson asked whether the Mythos hold-back was security or simply insufficient compute).[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]
- A bug in third-party harness detection charged a Reddit user $200 merely for having "hermes.md" in Git history (later refunded); another user's 110-person agriculture company was banned with appeals going "to a black hole."[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]
- Tae Kim: Anthropic "vastly underestimated compute growth needs" — Dario called OpenAI's buying YOLO, "but now it looks like Sam Altman was right all along"; Altman declared "we have become an AI inference company now."[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]
- Whittemore personally used ~1 billion tokens last month (~7,500 books of words); between November and April, never-used-AI respondents fell from 26% to 17% while frequent users rose 17% to 24%.[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]
- Goldman (via Hedgeye): companies are blowing past AI inference budgets by orders of magnitude, with inference costs approaching 10% of headcount costs and potentially reaching salary parity within quarters; Abacus AI's CEO says their AI bill will overtake payroll in 6 months.[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]
- Wall Street hammered AI stocks over a WSJ report of OpenAI missing older targets, while Codex app users grew from ~200,000 on January 1 to 4 million before GPT 5.5's launch — "AI literally operates on a dog year time scale."[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]
- In his monthly pulse surveys (January-March), cost savings never registered as a top benefit; time savings fell from 19.7% to 12.7% as primary benefit while "new capabilities" rose from 21.9% to 29.3%.[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]
- Five enterprise moves: (1) find AI spending leaks via a use-case audit; (2) hold a "cheap model bake-off"; (3) enshrine a "model sommelier" role owning bargain-intelligence selection; (4) build escape-hatch architecture that escalates from cheap models on low confidence or high stakes; (5) build an AI cost scoreboard making agent economics visible.[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]

# Takeaways

Whittemore is emphatic this is not a short-term blip: planned compute won't arrive fast enough, much of it will go to training rather than serving, and once usage-based billing normalizes everyone will follow. But he sees medium-term upside — more sustainable business models, honest AI cost accounting, more sophisticated multi-model systems tuned for cost-performance per task, and, echoing Jamie Dimon's worry about change happening "too fast for society," a somewhat forced slowdown in dramatic job displacement. If agents cost close to the humans they'd replace, cost savings — already the least-cited AI benefit in his surveys — becomes an even weaker driver of displacement than the doom discourse assumes.[^the-ai-subsidy-era-is-over-5MPFyOKlASc]
