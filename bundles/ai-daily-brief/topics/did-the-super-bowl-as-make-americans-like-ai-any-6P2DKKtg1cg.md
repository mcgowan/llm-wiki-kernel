# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore treats the Super Bowl's crop of AI ads — 23% of all spots by Adweek's count were for AI companies or highlighted AI in their production — not as an advertising story but as a public-perception one. The backdrop is significant American antipathy toward AI: an Edelman study finding only 32% trust it, Pew finding 59% have little or no confidence companies will develop AI responsibly and 52% more concerned than excited (versus 10% more excited), Gallup finding 73% expect net job loss, and Searchlight finding 51% think AI will replace rather than supplement human work. Whittemore argues this antipathy is mostly not a hardened position but economic anxiety among a "middle" majority still trying to figure out what AI means for them — and the ads should be judged by how they landed with that group.[^did-the-super-bowl-as-make-americans-like-ai-any-6P2DKKtg1cg]

Going ad by ad, he finds Anthropic's anti-ads spot backfired badly with general audiences, OpenAI's Codex-branded "you can just build things" ad was positively if forgettably received (with a bizarre hoax claiming it replaced a device teaser), Google's Gemini ad was the consensus best, and ai.com's crashed website — a thin OpenClaw wrapper on a $70 million domain — read to many as "the absolute peak of the AI bubble."[^did-the-super-bowl-as-make-americans-like-ai-any-6P2DKKtg1cg]

# Key points

- Anthropic's "ads are coming to AI, but not to Claude" spot required knowing OpenAI had announced ads in ChatGPT; without that context it read as a critique of AI generally. An iSpot survey of 500 viewers put its likeability in the bottom 3% of Super Bowl ads over five years, with purchase intent 24% below Super Bowl norms and the most common viewer reaction described as "WTF."[^did-the-super-bowl-as-make-americans-like-ai-any-6P2DKKtg1cg]
- OpenAI's ad, tagline "you can just build things," was Codex-branded, flashing vignettes of makers plus Alan Turing, Grace Hopper, and Einstein; CMO Kate Rauch framed it around "participation, agency." Ad Age called it OpenAI positioning ChatGPT as "the Kleenex of AI"; the New York Times list ranked it around 17th of 66.[^did-the-super-bowl-as-make-americans-like-ai-any-6P2DKKtg1cg]
- An elaborate hoax — including a fake Alexander Skarsgård device ad, a fake Ad Age headline, and an entire fake website — claimed OpenAI swapped its ad last-minute; OpenAI's CMO and the impersonated reporter both debunked it, and Whittemore says he doesn't know what the hoaxer's incentive was.[^did-the-super-bowl-as-make-americans-like-ai-any-6P2DKKtg1cg]
- Google's Gemini ad — a mom and son imagining a new house's potential — drew the most acclaim; Whittemore notes Google dares to pull heartstrings rather than go for laughs, making the ad "not really about AI" but about AI as part of life's journey.[^did-the-super-bowl-as-make-americans-like-ai-any-6P2DKKtg1cg]
- Amazon's Alexa+ ad with Chris Hemsworth played on and normalized fears of AI killing you; Whittemore predicts it will be divisive but thinks leaning into the fear opened genuinely funny territory. Meta's Oakley smart-glasses ad (Marshawn Lynch, Spike Lee, IShowSpeed) played to a real strength in wearables.[^did-the-super-bowl-as-make-americans-like-ai-any-6P2DKKtg1cg]
- Genspark's Matthew Broderick/Ferris Bueller ad, made with AI help in five weeks, was ranked most effective by some lists but drew "this is pretty much telling our employers they don't need us anymore" reactions.[^did-the-super-bowl-as-make-americans-like-ai-any-6P2DKKtg1cg]
- Whittemore calls Base44's (Wix) spot the most underrated: it shows people gaining capabilities they never had rather than AI doing their core work, making it "inherently empowering" — and funny, with the tagline "It's app to you."[^did-the-super-bowl-as-make-americans-like-ai-any-6P2DKKtg1cg]
- Svedka's "first fully AI-generated Super Bowl ad" was widely panned; ai.com — bought by crypto.com's founder for $70 million, reportedly the most expensive domain ever — ran a $10 million ad that crashed its site with a 504 error, and turned out to be a wrapper on OpenClaw, an open-source agent framework that went from nonexistent to a Super Bowl ad in three weeks.[^did-the-super-bowl-as-make-americans-like-ai-any-6P2DKKtg1cg]

# Takeaways

On the episode's central question — could these ads make Americans love something they don't like — Whittemore is honest that he isn't sure: "There were highlights and lowlights, but I guess overall it could have been a lot worse. So maybe we'll call that a win." His broader stance is that the persuadable middle majority, not the committed haters or the already-sold, is the audience that matters, and that empowerment-framed ads (Google's, Base44's) served that audience far better than in-joke swipes (Anthropic's) or bubble spectacles (ai.com).[^did-the-super-bowl-as-make-americans-like-ai-any-6P2DKKtg1cg]
