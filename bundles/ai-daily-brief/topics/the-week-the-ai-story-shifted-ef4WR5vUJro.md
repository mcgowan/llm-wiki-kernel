# Overview

In the second installment of his weekly-theme experiment, Nathaniel Whittemore argues the AI story shifted — or at least forked — across three fronts. On jobs, essays from Ezra Klein, Chicago Booth economist Alex Tabarrok-style relational-sector arguments, and a16z's David George pushed skepticism of the job apocalypse into mainstream, non-tech-sympathetic discourse. On markets, Wall Street leaders and price action stopped treating the buildout as a bubble. And on product, the launches reflected what he calls the harness engineering era, focused on making models workable in practice rather than raw capability.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]

He frames this as a sequel to the prior week's "AI grew up" theme: the new maturity has begun to diffuse into the stories people tell about AI. Central to his reading is that a more realistic understanding of how slow enterprise diffusion will be lengthens disruption timelines, and timelines are what determine whether change is painful or absorbable.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]

# Key points

- Ezra Klein — who previously hosted Eliezer Yudkowsky and ran a Jack Clark interview titled around how fast agents would rip through the economy — published a post arguing the AI job apocalypse probably won't happen, inspired by Alex's essay "What Will Be Scarce?" on the relational sector, where the value of a good depends on who made it and how.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]
- David George of a16z published "The AI Job Apocalypse Is a Complete Fantasy," with data: US agricultural employment falling from just under 70% in 1850 to under 5% today alongside labor-market diversification since 1950; spreadsheets shrinking bookkeeping while growing financial analysts and auditors; nail salons, pet care, tutoring, and sports-related work each growing from under 100,000 workers in 1990 to between 150,000 and 350,000; and augmentation out-mentioning substitution 8 to 1 on public earnings calls.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]
- Both labs launched enterprise deployment joint ventures — OpenAI's at a $10 billion starting valuation with a $4 billion investment, Anthropic's with $1.5 billion — bringing in partners including Blackstone and Goldman Sachs; Whittemore reads fierce rivals willingly distracting themselves with boring deployment problems as evidence of how hard closing the capability overhang will be.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]
- Jamie Dimon said the trillion-dollar data center investment will make sense; Larry Fink said not only is there no AI bubble but "the opposite," citing supply shortages and demand growing faster than anticipated.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]
- The Information valued the Anthropic-Google 5-gigawatt deal at $200 billion over years, part of Google Cloud's $462 billion backlog; despite Google's up-to-$40 billion investment in Anthropic reviving circular-funding arguments, Google rose 10% on the backlog news, another 1.5% overnight, and held the gains.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]
- Anthropic partnered with Elon Musk to take over the entire Colossus 1 data center capacity, and endorsed the idea of data centers in space; xAI is being folded entirely into SpaceX, which Whittemore reads as Musk accepting that his role in the AI race is infrastructure rather than model development.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]
- New York Times reporting on a Grimes County legal filing put Musk's Terafab chip project at $55 billion to as much as $119 billion, far above earlier $20-25 billion estimates; if completed it would be the largest chip fab on the planet, and the Anthropic deal supplies the demand that previously seemed missing.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]
- Nvidia partnered with Corning — holder of over 70% share in data center fiber optic glass — on three new facilities in Texas and North Carolina adding 3,000 manufacturing jobs; Jensen Huang called it "the single largest infrastructure buildout in human history" and a chance to revitalize American manufacturing.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]
- Product launches fit the harness theme: Code with Claude brought memory features, human-review tooling, and multi-agent orchestration infrastructure; Cursor added a recursive /orchestrate skill; OpenAI shipped three real-time voice models (GPT Real-Time 2, Real-Time Translate covering 70+ input and 13 output languages, and Real-Time Whisper), with Sam Altman citing use when users "have a lot of context to dump."[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]
- ElevenLabs hit a half billion dollars in annualized revenue and added Nvidia, BlackRock, Wellington, and Santander as investors; Blitzy raised a couple hundred million at a $1.4 billion valuation.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]
- Counter-evidence to the shift: Coinbase and Cloudflare both blamed layoffs on AI and most outlets accepted it, but more observers than before checked the specifics — Cloudflare cut 1,100 after hiring 2,000 months earlier, and Coinbase's transaction revenue had fallen 40% year over year.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]
- Codex shipped /goal, described by OpenAI's Philip Corry as their take on the Ralph loop; Andrew Chen ran it 14 hours overnight on a device driver project and predicted it would multiply token use enormously, while Alex Finn argued it only works with meta-prompted goals rather than handwritten ones.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's synthesis is that the narrative shift stems less from new optimism than from a maturing understanding of diffusion — and that longer timelines make reskilling and role redesign viable in ways a one-to-two-year disruption would not. He follows the chain from insatiable token demand through a compute shortage that looks monumental once enterprise agent adoption moves from a small slice to a majority, concluding that the data center buildout is not a temporary construction burst but a likely decades-long project, which he expects to convert Wall Street optimism into blue-collar enthusiasm; he notes construction unions are already leading efforts to reconcile data centers with communities. He cautions against overreading narrative shifts, and cites the more skeptical reception of AI-blamed layoffs as evidence the change is a rejection of AI as the default explanation rather than a denial that AI matters — he still expects companies to be smaller in five years while producing more. He is watching whether the White House moves toward vetting models before release, calling it a fluid and genuinely contested situation, and recommends listeners experiment with Codex's /goal feature.[^the-week-the-ai-story-shifted-ef4WR5vUJro]
