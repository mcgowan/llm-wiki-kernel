# Overview

In this headlines episode, Nathaniel Whittemore leads with Anthropic's announcement — tucked into a blog post about a new Google/Broadcom compute deal — that it has reached $30B in annualized revenue, a 3x increase since the end of last year and up 58% since the end of February, which on the latest available numbers suggests Anthropic has flipped OpenAI in annualized run rate. The milestone lands as both labs' financials face IPO-grade scrutiny: a Wall Street Journal deep dive highlighted sky-high training costs (OpenAI ~$30B this year; Anthropic almost tripling to $28B by 2028) and both companies' habit of reporting profitability excluding training costs, which critics like Ram Maluwalia deride as financial engineering.[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]

The rest of the episode covers Anthropic's expanded partnership with Google and Broadcom adding 3.5 gigawatts of TPU capacity from 2027; Google productizing its Gemma 4 small model in a fully on-device dictation app; Meta's plans to eventually open-source part of its forthcoming Avocado model; and Meta's internal "Claudonomics" leaderboard, emblematic of the "token maxing" culture in which token consumption is treated as a proxy for AI-enhanced productivity.[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]

# Key points

- Anthropic announced $30B ARR — 3x since end of last year, +58% since end of February — apparently surpassing OpenAI's annualized run rate, though Whittemore cautions the two companies don't calculate revenue identically and expects OpenAI to push back if the flip isn't real.[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]
- Per the WSJ analysis: OpenAI expects ~$30B in training spend this year (triple last year); Anthropic's training costs nearly triple to $28B by 2028; both report an alternate profitability measure excluding training costs, under which both eke out a small profit this year.[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]
- Anthropic's revenue is almost entirely enterprise; OpenAI's still skews consumer, carrying inference costs for many free users. OpenAI expects cash-flow positivity in 2030; Anthropic forecasts conventional profit by 2028. The Journal's framing: both will burn giant amounts of cash and are "counting on their IPO investors to help buoy their businesses."[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]
- Fleeting Bits called Anthropic's growth "an annualized 9700% — the fastest revenue growth at this scale in history"; the closest comparison found was Nvidia's best-ever quarter at a 1240% annualized rate (Q2 FY2024). John Arnold noted that 18 months ago Anthropic was "broadly considered the odd man out of the AI race."[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]
- Anthropic expanded its Google/Broadcom partnership with 3.5 gigawatts of TPU capacity coming online from 2027, with most new data centers in the US; TPUs are used exclusively for inference while AWS runs Anthropic's training clusters. Enterprise customers spending $1M+ annually doubled from 500 to 1,000 in under two months.[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]
- Whittemore notes the deal arguably matters more to Google and Broadcom: Google has built a multi-billion-dollar external TPU chip business around a single customer, and Broadcom gets guaranteed demand as long as Anthropic grows.[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]
- Google released AI Edge Eloquent, a dictation app built on Gemma 4 that runs entirely on-device; Gemma 4 was downloaded 2 million times in its first week (vs Gemma 3's 6.7M over a year), and even the 2B version shows strong agentic performance — a DeepMind lead demoed Wikipedia-querying agent skills running on an iPhone.[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]
- Axios reported Meta will keep part of its forthcoming Avocado model proprietary at launch for safety reasons but plans an open-source version, with AI CEO Alexander Wang positioning Meta as the consumer-focused, democratizing US open-source option versus enterprise/government-focused OpenAI and Anthropic.[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]
- The Information reported Meta's internal "Claudonomics" leaderboard tracks the top 250 token users among 85,000 employees, with ranks like "session immortal" and "token legend"; Meta is spending ~90 million tokens per developer per day. Jensen Huang has said he'd be "deeply alarmed" if a $500K engineer wasn't using $250K of tokens annually, and Meta CTO Andrew Bosworth called salary-scale token spend "easy money. Keep doing it. No limit."[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]
- Joe Weisenthal compared token-consumption-as-productivity to Mao's backyard steel furnaces; Meta Critic Capital countered with China's GDP-target analogy — imperfect metrics can still work when the opportunity is vast — estimating Meta's spend at $4-5B/year at Opus 4.6 rates and predicting 98% of corporations would be better off token maxing over the next 18 months.[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's throughline is that the lab race has entered a new financial phase: Wall Street is now analyzing OpenAI and Anthropic as public-market behemoths rather than growth-stage startups, and the cash-burn narrative the WSJ sketched is the default story both companies will have to fight against through their IPOs. On compute, he frames the Google/Broadcom deal as "the AI arms race turning into a full-on power plant competition" (quoting Mohammad Hassan), and on Gemma 4 he sees the potential for a breakout moment for local models. On token maxing, he acknowledges the obvious Goodhart's-law flaw — engineers burning tokens to climb leaderboards rather than be productive — while airing the counterargument that at this stage of the opportunity, even a crude metric may point companies in the right direction.[^anthropic-now-leads-openai-in-annualized-revenue-iRMIUmDZrtY]
