# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore argues it is "one of our great failures" that the AI industry spends enormous energy assessing which jobs are at risk and almost none exploring what new jobs an AI-enabled future creates. The job-apocalypse narrative, he says, rests on a hidden assumption — AI as a pure labor-supply story with demand held constant, a version of the lump-of-labor fallacy. Correcting it means asking where demand expands, and he proposes six forms of demand elasticity AI unlocks: price, access, complexity, continuity, personalization, and relational value. These produce two kinds of unlock: the affordability unlock (the same menu at a lower price — millions of small businesses becoming first-time buyers of design, legal, and marketing services) and the possibility unlock (entirely new service models, like continuous preventative healthcare, that were never operationally viable before).[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]

Against the "won't AGI just eat those jobs too?" objection, Whittemore introduces the "human premium" — seven categories of value that stay attached to human involvement even when AI can perform the tasks: relationship, embodied presence, trust, accountability, translation, behavior change, and provenance/status. He then walks a healthcare case study, sketching net-new roles like continuous care navigators that could plausibly number in the hundreds of thousands to over a million.[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]

# Key points

- The hidden assumption: AI is analyzed as increasing labor supply while demand stays constant — but that assumption "has never held true" historically, and the real question is which parts of the economy grow as AI lets us produce more.[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]
- Six demand elasticities: price ("it cost too much"), access ("I couldn't get it"), complexity ("the system was too confusing" — taxes, insurance, immigration), continuity ("I'd benefit from help all the time"), personalization ("I'd value something made for me"), and relational value — the last drawing on Chicago Booth economist Alex Imas's "what will be scarce" essay on the relational sector.[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]
- Affordability unlock example: a small business that can't buy a $5,000 design project, $3,000 marketing campaign, $2,000 legal review, and $1,500 analytics report becomes a buyer when AI turns $5,000 jobs into $500 jobs — activating an enormous long-tail market.[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]
- Possibility unlock example: continuous preventative healthcare — nobody demands it today because it was never operationally viable; AI collapsing the cost of the informational layer (monitoring, documentation, scheduling, escalation routing) makes a personalized, data-driven, preventative, continuous care model possible.[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]
- The AGI objection reframed: "can AI perform the task?" is the wrong question; the right one is "does AI-only delivery satisfy the demand?" — a service-design question, since trust, accountability, presence, and relationships are part of the value.[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]
- Seven human premiums: relationship, embodied presence (the nurse in the room, the trainer correcting form), trust (wanting a person to validate before acting), accountability ("someone I can sue if something goes wrong"), translation (turning messy desire into usable AI-mediated work), behavior change (humans listen to humans), and provenance/status (a human made it).[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]
- On margin compression: even if AGI makes $500 work cost 50 cents, "it is a failure of economic understanding to assume that there is no room for margin on top of the underlying cost of goods sold" — translators will upsell AI work fitted to buyers' constraints.[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]
- Healthcare case study, continuous care navigator: conservative case of 40M enrolled US patients at 150 patients per navigator yields ~276,000 jobs (comparable to personal financial advisors or loan officers); aggressive case of 120M patients at 100 each yields 1.2 million jobs — roughly the number of US high school teachers.[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]
- Additional sketched roles: care plan outcome specialists (owning the gap between medical advice and real-world execution — cost, transportation, fear, family) and health data operations specialists (governance, consent, integration accountability at maybe one per 1,000-2,000 patients — tens of thousands of jobs).[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]
- Six broad new role families across sectors: navigators, continuous support workers, AI-augmented service operators, data/ops specialists, QA/safety/compliance roles, and escalation specialists — with parallel patterns in legal, education, mental health, personal finance, and elder/child care.[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's argument in a nutshell: the AI jobs question is an AI economy question that must include demand, demand stretches along six elasticities, unlocks come in affordability and possibility flavors, and the human premium is why AGI doesn't simply eat the resulting work. More AI means more labor supply, which changes the cost structure of industries, which means more demand — and more demand, even under AGI, means more human work. He maintains his standing position: short-term disruptions will be painful and require meaningful interventions, "we should not be Pollyannaish," but on the other side things are "massively better and even bigger than they are today."[^the-new-jobs-ai-will-create-WhAAKxPlMhw]
