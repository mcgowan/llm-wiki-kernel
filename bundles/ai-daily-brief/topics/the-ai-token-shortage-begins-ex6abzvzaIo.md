# Overview

In this June 1 month-in-review, Nathaniel Whittemore declares May "one of the single most consequential AI months" and frames it as 2026's second big transitional moment. The first — the agent era unleashed by Claude Code, Codex, Opus 4.5, and GPT-52 around the turn of the year — shifted AI's core economic unit from the seat to the token, driving staggering foundation-model revenue: OpenAI surged to $30B ARR while Anthropic went from $3B at the start of 2025 to a $47B annualized run rate, closing May with a fundraise valuing it just under a trillion dollars and anticipating the first profitable quarter of any big lab. Those numbers forced a media mea culpa on the Q4 "AI bubble" narrative (The Atlantic's "So About That AI Bubble").[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]

But the month's back half revealed the next phase: "AI sticker shock" (Axios) and a secular shift from the AI subsidy era — where $200/month max plans delivered power users perhaps 10-20x that in token value — to a token scarcity era defined by a structural shortage of compute. Whittemore catalogs the responses: usage-based billing cascading across GitHub Copilot, Google, and Anthropic; companies like Amazon scrapping token-maxing leaderboards; labs launching enterprise-deployment ventures; market innovation on cheaper models; and, most dramatically, Elon Musk realigning SpaceX/xAI into a Neocloud serving Anthropic via Colossus 1 and 2 ahead of the SpaceX IPO.[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]

# Key points

- Anthropic hit a $47B annualized run rate (from $3B at the start of 2025), closed May with a $65M round valuing it just under $1T, led OpenAI in business adoption per Ramp data, and expects the first profitable quarter of any major lab.[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]
- Whittemore's own contextportfolio.ai project racked up ~$5,000 in API costs in six weeks — more than two years' worth of $200/month Claude Max seats — illustrating seat-to-token economics.[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]
- Uber burned through its entire 2026 AI budget in four months; its COO's later ROI skepticism was reduced to headlines like The Information's "Uber COO says AI lacks ROI," feeding the sticker-shock narrative.[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]
- Usage-based billing cascaded: GitHub Copilot (late April) said the premium-request model was "no longer sustainable"; Google IO paired cheaper headline plans ($200 Ultra, new $100 tier) with usage limits and overage billing; Anthropic moved third-party harness usage to per-token billing, causing "a bit of an uproar."[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]
- Amazon and others scrapped token-maxing leaderboards — partly Goodhart's-law concerns, partly because token maxing became too expensive under the new billing models.[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]
- Both labs launched enterprise-transformation ventures: OpenAI a majority-owned deployment company placing forward-deployed engineers in clients; Anthropic partnered with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs on a services firm built on Fractional.[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]
- Market responses to scarcity: Cursor's Composer 2.5 performs well at far lower cost than Opus 4.7/GPT-55; Google's Gemma models are seeing adoption outpace comparable Chinese models (though Gemini 3.5 Flash costs ~5x Gemini 3 Flash per Artificial Analysis); DeepSeek made a 75% V4 price cut permanent to scoop up cost-driven defectors.[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]
- SpaceX became a Neocloud: SpaceX/xAI first offered Anthropic capacity on Colossus 1, then (temporarily) Colossus 2 — recasting Musk as a compute czar and reframing the SpaceX IPO; Bezos now calls 2-3 years "a little ambitious" for orbital data centers rather than dismissing them.[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]
- Infrastructure went vertical: Baseten raising $1B at an $11B valuation (doubling in a quarter), OpenRouter's Series B making it a unicorn, memory stocks surging with SK Hynix and Micron becoming trillion-dollar companies, and Meta floating a cloud business to de-risk its ~$130B compute spend.[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]
- Model releases felt incremental — Opus 4.8 landed late May to iPhone-release comparisons (Greg Eisenberg) — with attention shifting to harnesses: Claude Code's dynamic workflows and /goal becoming a cross-harness primitive.[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]
- Narrative and policy shifts: Altman (with articulated reasoning) and Amodei walked back job-apocalypse rhetoric; Democrats split between Bernie/AOC data-center moratoriums and Elizabeth Warren's "Why We Need to Tax AI" op-ed in Time; the White House's opposition to expanding Mythos access was reportedly partly about reserving scarce tokens for government use.[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's thesis: the anchor characteristic of the coming period is a structural shortage of AI tokens — there simply isn't enough compute to serve the AI people want to consume — and everything from business models to enterprise practice to policy will recalibrate around it. He expects the compute-buildout theme to intensify around the SpaceX IPO, anticipates a Mythos release and a new OpenAI model in June, and argues serious advantage will accrue to enterprises that learn to manage token scarcity faster and more efficiently than their peers.[^the-ai-token-shortage-begins-ex6abzvzaIo]
