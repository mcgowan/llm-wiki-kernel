# Overview

Ahead of Google I/O, Whittemore frames the event as a test of how Google will handle what he sees as a growing divergence between consumer AI and work AI. He builds the setup from OpenAI's launch of Codex inside the ChatGPT mobile app — a full work experience (initiating, reviewing, steering, approving) rather than mere remote control — which he reads as further evidence that knowledge work is shifting from doing things to managing fleets of AI agents. Invoking the "AI as normal technology" essay by Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor, he argues consumer AI has turned out to be roughly "normal" in its diffusion, while work AI is "extremely abnormal," and notes that OpenAI (post-Sora shutdown) chose the work lane while Google alone still pursues both in equal measure.[^what-google-needs-to-do-at-i-o-this-week-8uCkUyRFUqY]

The headlines cover Cerebras's explosive public-market debut, Figma's AI-driven revenue reacceleration, Nvidia nearing $6 trillion, reports of OpenAI considering breach-of-contract action against Apple, Anthropic's rumored $30B raise at a $900B valuation, Microsoft canceling Claude Code licenses, and Claude 3 Mythos's escalating cybersecurity capabilities.[^what-google-needs-to-do-at-i-o-this-week-8uCkUyRFUqY]

# Takeaways

Whittemore is skeptical that maximal personal context is the winning play — he spends significant time telling Claude and Codex to forget stale context, and doubts shopping/travel agents are what consumers actually want, though he concedes Google's data and billions of users make it the right company to try. His wish list for I/O: Spark for consumers, a frontier-class model at 15-20x lower cost, and clarity — ideally consolidation — on Google's core agent harness. He predicts Wall Street may not know what to make of such a result since investors expect a state-of-the-art model headline, but argues there is a clear lane for Google to win back builders on cheap high-quality inference. On the Cerebras froth, he notes fundamentals arguments feel "divorced from the reality of the moment, which is that fundamentals don't matter if everyone is bidding AI."[^what-google-needs-to-do-at-i-o-this-week-8uCkUyRFUqY]
