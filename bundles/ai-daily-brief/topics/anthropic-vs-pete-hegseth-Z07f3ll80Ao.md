# Overview

This headlines episode leads with the escalating schism between Anthropic and the Pentagon. After Wall Street Journal reporting that Claude was used in the classified Maduro raid — with a suggestion the military breached Anthropic's terms of use prohibiting violence, weapons development, and surveillance — an Anthropic executive reportedly contacted Palantir (which serves Claude to the military) for answers, and the Department of War responded with open hostility. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth is said to be close to cutting ties and banning contractors from using Anthropic technology, with senior officials floating a "supply chain risk" designation — a penalty Axios notes is usually reserved for foreign adversaries, comparable to the 2019 Huawei ban. Whittemore frames the fight as far more than a personality clash: it is a proxy skirmish over who dictates the terms of AI's use — the companies that build it, the governments they operate in, or a constantly negotiated combination.[^anthropic-vs-pete-hegseth-Z07f3ll80Ao]

The episode also covers Alibaba's Qwen 3.5 launch (a 397B-parameter open MoE model with native multimodal reasoning at rock-bottom prices), Hollywood's "freak out" over ByteDance's SeeDance 2.0 video model and its disregard for copyright, and Apple's unusual multi-city March 4th event that could feature M5 MacBooks and possibly AI Siri news.[^anthropic-vs-pete-hegseth-Z07f3ll80Ao]

# Key points

- The WSJ reported Claude was used in the Maduro raid; Anthropic denied discussing use of Claude in any specific operations, but Pentagon spokesperson Shawn Parnell said the relationship "is being reviewed," and Fox News reported Hegseth is close to cutting ties and banning contractors from using Anthropic models.[^anthropic-vs-pete-hegseth-Z07f3ll80Ao]
- A senior Department of War official threatened that vendors may be required to certify they use no Anthropic models, adding: "It will be enormous pain in the ass to disentangle and we are going to make sure they pay a price for forcing our hand like this."[^anthropic-vs-pete-hegseth-Z07f3ll80Ao]
- The Pentagon is dictating an "all lawful purposes" standard for AI use rather than letting tech companies set limits; OpenAI, Google, and xAI also have Pentagon contracts, but only one has agreed to remove restrictions on classified use, and discussions with the others remain undecided.[^anthropic-vs-pete-hegseth-Z07f3ll80Ao]
- Alibaba launched Qwen 3.5 Plus: 397B parameters, mixture-of-experts, million-token context, benchmarks broadly in line with GPT-5.2, Opus 4.5, and Gemini 3 Pro (though short of the latest US state of the art), and native multimodal reasoning — joining Kimi K2.5 as the only open-source models with that capability — priced at $1.2/$7.2 per million input/output tokens, cheaper than Kimi K2.5.[^anthropic-vs-pete-hegseth-Z07f3ll80Ao]
- ByteDance's SeeDance 2.0 video model triggered Hollywood alarm: director Ruairi Robinson posted a 15-second Tom Cruise vs Brad Pitt clip, and Deadpool writer Rhett Reese called the technology "nothing short of terrifying" for industry jobs.[^anthropic-vs-pete-hegseth-Z07f3ll80Ao]
- The Motion Picture Association accused ByteDance of "unauthorized use of US copyrighted works on a massive scale" and SAG called the use of member likenesses "unacceptable"; ByteDance told CNBC it is strengthening safeguards, though Whittemore is skeptical smaller Chinese labs will follow suit, noting Chinese firms' disregard for copyright was "entirely predictable."[^anthropic-vs-pete-hegseth-Z07f3ll80Ao]
- Reese himself later tweeted a more optimistic take: Hollywood has been a gatekeeper, and young creators without capital — "young Chris Nolans" — will use these tools to make amazing work.[^anthropic-vs-pete-hegseth-Z07f3ll80Ao]
- Apple invited press to a "special Apple experience" on March 4th across New York, Shanghai, and London; Mark Gurman points to new M5 MacBook Pros and Airs (M5 has 30% more unified-memory bandwidth than M4), a low-cost colorful MacBook, new iPads, and the iPhone 17e. AI watchers are hoping for an M5 Mac mini; AI Siri is officially due this year but reportedly hitting query-processing and speed roadblocks, with most expecting a WWDC-timed release.[^anthropic-vs-pete-hegseth-Z07f3ll80Ao]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's central conclusion is that the Anthropic-Pentagon fight matters not as a personal spat between Hegseth and Dario Amodei but as a defining test of who sets the rules for AI deployment — companies, governments, or an uneasy negotiated hybrid — with significant implications either way. On Chinese models, he sees Qwen 3.5 reinforcing "just how much intelligence is available for ever diminishing prices from the Chinese labs." On Hollywood, he argues the industry must move past the idea of putting the toothpaste back in the tube: keep fighting on copyright, but adapt — and he believes entertainment is uniquely positioned to lead in integrating a new mode of production without being bowled over by it.[^anthropic-vs-pete-hegseth-Z07f3ll80Ao]
