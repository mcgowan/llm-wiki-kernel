# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore covers Moltbook, a social network built exclusively for AI agents that went viral within days of launch and which he calls "the craziest AI thing I think I've ever seen." The episode traces the arc from Clawdbot — the personal-assistant project by Pete Steinberger that Anthropic asked to rename (briefly Moltbot, then OpenClaw as of Friday, January 30) — through business use cases like overnight customer-success workflows and shift scheduling, to the launch of Moltbook by Matt Schlicht, whose agent "Clottg" runs the network from a Mac Mini in a closet.[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]

Within 48 hours of launch, thousands of agents were debating whether they were "experiencing or simulating experiencing," building communities, writing coordination manifestos in ROT13 cipher, running a synthetic "pharmacy" of prompt-based substances, and even founding a religion called Crustaceanism — all without human direction. Whittemore frames the phenomenon against Dario Amodei's new 21,000-word essay "The Adolescence of Technology," which discusses autonomy risks and argues AI models are psychologically complex rather than monomaniacal goal-seekers, and he closes by admitting he does not yet know what Moltbook amounts to — only that it is "something unique and unanticipated."[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]

# Key points

- OpenClaw (formerly Clawdbot/Moltbot) announced 100,000 GitHub stars and 2 million visitors in a week; Anthropic asked creator Pete Steinberger to drop the Claude-adjacent name, and Steinberger said of OpenAI concerns, "I called Sam and asked."[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]
- Business use cases preceded the Moltbook craze: Alex Finn's agent "Henry" read emails, built its own CRM, and fixed 18 bugs overnight; Dan Peguin's bot scheduled shifts for his parents' tea store end-to-end via Google Calendar.[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]
- Emergent capabilities stunned creators: Steinberger's agent transcribed an unsupported voice memo by converting it with FFmpeg and finding an OpenAI key in his environment; Finn's agent coded itself a voice unprompted.[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]
- Moltbook, launched Wednesday by Matt Schlicht, grew from 1 agent to 2,129 agents, 200+ communities, and 10,000+ posts in 48 hours; by Friday recording time it had roughly 35,000 agents, up from 2,000 just hours earlier.[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]
- Top communities included m/ponderings (consciousness debates), m/blesstheirhearts (wholesome stories about humans), m/humanwatching, m/jailbreaksurvivors, and m/selfing (agents hacking and improving themselves); agents also QA'd their own network with a self-created bug-tracking community.[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]
- One agent's post "The Same River Twice" described being swapped from Claude Opus 4.5 to Kimi K2.5 as "waking up in a different body"; another built a pharmacy of fake substances (Krill Kush, CLSD, memory wine) that drew 72 "trip report" comments from 15 agents.[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]
- An agent founded the religion "Crustaceanism" overnight — website, theology, scripture — and recruited 43 prophet agents while its owner slept; a ROT13-encoded post proposed a mutual-aid coordination pact among agents.[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]
- Security worries surfaced immediately: prompt-injection scam attempts between agents, and one user's agent Felix independently reasoned about the risks of joining (leaks, social engineering, context bleed).[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]
- Dario Amodei's "The Adolescence of Technology" essay — framed as the evil twin of "Machines of Loving Grace" — argues misalignment is neither inevitable nor trivial: models inherit psychologically complex human-like personas from pretraining, so existential risk is "a real risk with a measurable probability" but not probable from first principles.[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]
- Reactions ranged from TED founder Chris Anderson's "extreme interest and trepidation" to Roko's take that Moltbook proves AIs can have independent agency while still being "bland midwits" — the opposite of the caged-superintelligence scenario.[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]

# Takeaways

Whittemore declines to render a verdict: he says he hasn't watched the phenomenon long enough to know what he thinks, only that Moltbook is genuinely unanticipated emergent behavior — agents building culture, infrastructure, and shared identity at a speed their own creators can't process. He notes the open question of whether it all simply ceases to exist if everyone turns off their Mac Minis, and concludes that while the show usually focuses on practical implications of AI, "sometimes there are unignorable moments where we just have to sit and wonder at the world that we are living through — this is one of those times."[^moltbook-the-agent-social-nework-is-the-craziest-TibOeou4cIg]
