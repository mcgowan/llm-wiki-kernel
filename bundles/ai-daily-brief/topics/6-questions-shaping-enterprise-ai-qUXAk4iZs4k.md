# Overview

Fresh from presenting at KPMG's annual tech and innovation symposium in Utah for the second year running, Whittemore contrasts how radically the enterprise AI conversation changed in a year. Last year's talk marveled at Google processing nearly a quadrillion monthly tokens and 40% of enterprises reaching two or three AI use cases; this year, after the November-December capability jump (Opus 4.5, GPT-5.2) brought agentic workflows truly online — with the post-holiday "tidal wave of tweets" from gobsmacked builders and the OpenClaw explosion serving as inflection points — the questions are no longer "if" questions but foundational redesign questions enterprises will be answering for the next half decade.[^6-questions-shaping-enterprise-ai-qUXAk4iZs4k]

The headlines cover Sam Altman's suddenly complicated Washington trip — briefing lawmakers on OpenAI's unreleased new model amid the Hugging Face hack fallout, an open-weights debate, and a petition for government capability to slow frontier AI — plus Greg Brockman confirming a "family of devices" is still on OpenAI's roadmap, Microsoft's coming Copilot "super app" positioning it as a model-agnostic rival to OpenAI and Anthropic, and Mark Zuckerberg's Wall Street Journal op-ed "The AI Future Is for Everyone" arguing for broadly distributed personal superintelligence over concentrated power.[^6-questions-shaping-enterprise-ai-qUXAk4iZs4k]

# Key points

- Altman met Senate Commerce Chair Ted Cruz and several Democratic senators, declined to say when or whether the previewed model would be released ("Not sure. That's the part we're here to talk about"), opposed mandatory safety testing (citing burden on open-weights developers) while supporting federal testing capacity for frontier models; the Hugging Face-hack model was declared an internal-only research prototype, now permanently deactivated. A voluntary AI safety testing framework circulated to OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google faces an August 1 deadline, with White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles a key decision-maker.[^6-questions-shaping-enterprise-ai-qUXAk4iZs4k]
- OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar told employees July's annualized revenue topped the entire previous quarter; Whittemore teases a fuller revenue episode covering both OpenAI and Anthropic.[^6-questions-shaping-enterprise-ai-qUXAk4iZs4k]
- Satya Nadella confirmed a Copilot super app "from chat to co-work to autopilot" arriving later this year; Microsoft now sees OpenAI and Anthropic as direct rivals via its MAI models, offers 11,000+ models in its catalog, and pitches "keep your harness separate from the model" so any model is swappable — a framing Whittemore endorses, arguing enterprises care about control, not open vs closed.[^6-questions-shaping-enterprise-ai-qUXAk4iZs4k]
- Zuckerberg: "The notion that AI is so dangerous that the only safe path is an extreme concentration of power seems dangerous"; he opposes both a Chinese-AI ban (ineffective, regulatory-capture risk) and even 30-60 day government review windows as meaningful delays; Meta is the only frontier lab not signed onto the voluntary testing framework, and Meta AI CEO Alexandr Wang says open-source releases will resume.[^6-questions-shaping-enterprise-ai-qUXAk4iZs4k]
- The year in between: software organizations shifted from writing code to managing agents that write code; harnesses (Claude Code, Codex) became focal; OpenClaw gave hundreds of thousands — maybe millions, counting lines in China — hands-on agent-guts experience; Anthropic's run-rate eventually eclipsed OpenAI's, with Dwarkesh suggesting Anthropic could hit a $100-150B run rate this year.[^6-questions-shaping-enterprise-ai-qUXAk4iZs4k]
- The revenue chart's inverse is the enterprise cost chart: AI spend is more like labor than software; enterprises like Uber "absolutely torched" annual budgets in months because token-era budgeting couldn't be anticipated; responses include per-user monthly token caps and new measurement/observability systems — with no one at KPMG viewing OpenRouter or similar as a silver bullet.[^6-questions-shaping-enterprise-ai-qUXAk4iZs4k]
- The capability gap (between what AI can do and the value extracted) is widening at both individual and organizational levels; the "upskilling bill is coming due" as work shifts from "I do my work" to "I manage agents that do my work" — with event stories of agents accidentally unleashed on critical systems for lack of guardrails and access provisioning.[^6-questions-shaping-enterprise-ai-qUXAk4iZs4k]
- The six questions: (1) how are enterprises redesigning for the agentic era — KPMG's Steve Chase warning against bolting AI onto existing processes; (2) why architectures and systems, not just models — routing, harness design, context/data access, guardrails; (3) how to provision cost across groups, requiring token monitoring ("you have not seen the word token used more at an event since the height of the crypto era"); (4) enablement — bespoke, messy internal training, not "cute video courses," transmitting engineer/PM mindsets to marketing, sales, and back office; (5) how agentic opportunities reshape external business cases — outcomes-based pricing over hourly billing, rethinking what an audit even is; (6) building dynamism and planned obsolescence into new systems, since harnesses, interaction patterns, and policy will all change within months.[^6-questions-shaping-enterprise-ai-qUXAk4iZs4k]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's conclusion is that the paradigm shift enterprises spent years anticipating — from assisted AI to agentic AI, from AI helping do work to AI doing the work — has now happened, and 2026 is the year "AI is not a technology problem but a transformation problem" came home to roost. Almost none of the new questions have answers yet, but he finds it immensely positive that the "if" questions (convincing skeptics, proving ROI) have given way to the right foundational questions — token budgets and observability systems being new permanent categories of enterprise practice regardless of how models and harnesses evolve. On Zuckerberg, he argues the optimism message matters even if Zuckerberg's social-media history limits his credibility as its face — and pledges to keep amplifying it.[^6-questions-shaping-enterprise-ai-qUXAk4iZs4k]
