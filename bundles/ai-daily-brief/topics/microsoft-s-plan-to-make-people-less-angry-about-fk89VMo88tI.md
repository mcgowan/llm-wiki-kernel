# Overview

With 2026 an election year, Nathaniel Whittemore argues AI will enter politics through the affordability theme: if data centers are perceived to raise Americans' cost of living, the AI industry "is going to have a not so fun time politically." President Trump amplified exactly that on Truth Social — "I never want Americans to pay higher electricity bills because of data centers" — announcing his administration is securing commitments from major tech companies, starting with Microsoft. Microsoft's answer, via vice chair Brad Smith, is a five-part "community first AI infrastructure" plan: paying utility rates high enough that costs aren't passed to communities, minimizing and replenishing water use, creating local jobs, adding to the tax base for hospitals and schools, and investing in local AI training and nonprofits.[^microsoft-s-plan-to-make-people-less-angry-about-fk89VMo88tI]

Whittemore endorses the move emphatically — calling the industry's failure to make data centers visibly good for their communities "a complete own goal" — and echoes Chamath Palihapitiya's argument that hyperscalers should go further and simply buy community goodwill, even zeroing out residents' electricity costs, since it's a tiny fraction of project budgets. The headlines also cover Beijing's effective ban on Nvidia H200 imports amid US export approvals, Cerebras's billion-dollar raise talks, and OpenAI's acquisition of health-records startup Torch.[^microsoft-s-plan-to-make-people-less-angry-about-fk89VMo88tI]

# Key points

- Whittemore says debates over what percentage of electricity-price increases is actually AI's fault are "completely losing political arguments" — all that matters is the perception that big companies pay their own way and then some.[^microsoft-s-plan-to-make-people-less-angry-about-fk89VMo88tI]
- Chamath Palihapitiya had pushed this policy since mid-last year: "The hyperscalers should take the electricity cost of local residents to zero and start buying goodwill," or expect local pushback that complicates the AI buildout.[^microsoft-s-plan-to-make-people-less-angry-about-fk89VMo88tI]
- Reuters reports Chinese customs officials have effectively banned Nvidia H200 chips from entering the country, with tech companies summoned and told not to order chips "unless necessary"; The Information's sources call the Beijing directive "deliberately vague," allowing exceptions like university research and R&D.[^microsoft-s-plan-to-make-people-less-angry-about-fk89VMo88tI]
- The same day, the US Commerce Department finalized H200 export approval with conditions: third-party lab inspection of AI capabilities before shipping, a cap of 50% as many chips to China as sold to US customers, and Chinese buyers demonstrating security procedures and no military use; Nvidia called it "a thoughtful balance that is great for America."[^microsoft-s-plan-to-make-people-less-angry-about-fk89VMo88tI]
- Analysts read Beijing's ban as a power play before April trade negotiations — the CFR's Chris McGuire: "Beijing believes the US is desperate to sell AI chips to China," giving China leverage to extract concessions; an Nvidia stock drawdown in an election year could motivate US flexibility.[^microsoft-s-plan-to-make-people-less-angry-about-fk89VMo88tI]
- Cerebras is in talks to raise $1 billion at a $22 billion valuation — up from $8 billion in October when it scuttled IPO plans — with rumors of a second-half IPO.[^microsoft-s-plan-to-make-people-less-angry-about-fk89VMo88tI]
- OpenAI acquired Torch, a four-person startup unifying medical records (labs, prescriptions, appointment notes) in AI-discoverable format, reportedly for $100 million in OpenAI equity — aimed at the hundreds of millions using ChatGPT for health questions weekly.[^microsoft-s-plan-to-make-people-less-angry-about-fk89VMo88tI]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's view: while corporate community initiatives invite cynicism, Microsoft's plan "is exactly the type of thing that needs to happen from all of the big tech companies" mid-buildout — and they should double down, going beyond cost-neutrality to actively making data centers value-accretive for host communities, which he considers a no-brainer given the relative costs. On chips, the H200 saga illustrates how murky and leverage-driven US-China AI trade has become, with both sides using "necessary" ambiguity as a negotiating instrument.[^microsoft-s-plan-to-make-people-less-angry-about-fk89VMo88tI]
