# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore debuts the show's first "AI lab power rankings," scoring eight players — Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, Anthropic, Amazon, Meta, xAI, and Apple — across nine weighted categories (compute and infrastructure, enterprise positioning, platform/ecosystem control, consumer positioning, model leverage, momentum, branded narrative, wedge, and X-factor). He also asked Gemini, Grok, ChatGPT, and Claude to run the same scorecard: the AI aggregation put Google first (91.4/100), with OpenAI, Microsoft, Anthropic, and Amazon all above 80, while Whittemore's own harsher scores tied OpenAI and Google at 74 with Anthropic at 70.[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]

The headlines cover the amended Microsoft–OpenAI agreement — ending cloud and IP exclusivity and removing the pre-AGI clause — followed immediately by OpenAI models launching on AWS, Amazon's new "Quick" desktop agent, and a caution that a Wall Street Journal report on OpenAI missing revenue targets reflects out-of-date pre-agent-era data.[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]

# Key points

- Microsoft–OpenAI amendments: Microsoft stays primary but not exclusive cloud partner (clearing the AWS path); its OpenAI IP license runs through 2032 but is now non-exclusive with an Azure-first release window; Microsoft drops its rev share to OpenAI while OpenAI keeps paying ~20% rev share through 2030; Microsoft keeps its 27% stake; and revenue/IP sharing is no longer conditional on being pre-AGI.[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]
- Whittemore endorses Rezo's framing: "OpenAI has grown too big for any single cloud to fully serve" — the deal was a constraint structured for a different time, and the outcome is win-win.[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]
- AWS CEO Matt Garman announced GPT-5.4 in limited preview on AWS with 5.5 coming within weeks, Codex on AWS infrastructure, and Bedrock managed agents "powered by OpenAI" — no workarounds required.[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]
- Amazon Quick is a desktop computer-use assistant (local files, live dashboards, slideshows, calendar/email/Slack/Jira connections) that learns personal context over time — Amazon's play in the "agentic do-everything, work-everywhere desktop app" space alongside tools like Claude Co-work.[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]
- On the WSJ report of OpenAI missing revenue targets, Whittemore warns the next few months will bring research "unbelievably disconnected" from on-the-ground reality because pre-agent-era data no longer reflects the agentic era.[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]
- Ranking methodology: compute/infrastructure is the heaviest category (20 points); enterprise (15) outweighs consumer (10); momentum is 10; "wedge" captures unique un-copyable assets; several AI models argued compute should weigh even more.[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]
- AI aggregation order: Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, Anthropic, Amazon, Meta, xAI, Apple. All four models put Google first on full-stack strength; Claude put Anthropic second, ChatGPT put OpenAI second, and both Grok and Gemini put Microsoft second.[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]
- Whittemore's key deviations: Google far ahead on compute (17 vs OpenAI's 12, Anthropic's 10) because owned compute beats financed deals; on enterprise he gave Anthropic 14, OpenAI 10, Google 8, arguing incumbency is worth less than people think and enterprises want to "go direct to the source."[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]
- Momentum: Google gets a 3/10 — 2026 has been dominated by agentic/coding use cases where "basically no one is looking to Gemini" — with Google I/O weeks away as its momentum moment; OpenAI gets 10 on the GPT-5.5/Codex shift, Anthropic 8.[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]
- xAI scores an off-scale 8-out-of-5 X-factor "because simply put, of Elon," plus high compute — Whittemore judges xAI to have the most room to rise over 6–12 months; Meta remains behind pending outcomes from its restructuring, despite the Ray-Bans wedge.[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]

# Takeaways

Whittemore stresses the exercise is less about picking a winner than surfacing what matters to labs right now — and that his scorecard offers "72 different places to either agree or disagree." He closes by rejecting zero-sum framing, quoting Miles Brundage ("mostly, there is just a rapidly expanding pie") and Dylan Patel's point that even tier-two and tier-three labs "are going to be sold out of tokens" because the economic value of the best models is growing faster than infrastructure can serve them: all the tokens that can do agentic things will be used, and there is room for a lot of winners.[^ai-lab-power-rankings-4qv6NIpY9Fo]
