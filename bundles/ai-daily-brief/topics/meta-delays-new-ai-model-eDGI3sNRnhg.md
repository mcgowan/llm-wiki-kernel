# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore leads this headlines edition with the New York Times report that Meta's frontier model, code-named Avocado, has been delayed until at least May after internal benchmarks showed shortfalls in reasoning, coding, and writing — "basically every major category for modern LLMs." After nearly nine months of development, the model reportedly beats Gemini 2.5 but not Gemini 3, and Meta leadership is even said to be considering licensing Gemini as a stopgap, even as researchers express excitement about the follow-on model code-named Watermelon. Ethan Mollick's summary of industry sentiment: with xAI and Meta falling behind, "frontier AI models are really a three-way race at this point."[^meta-delays-new-ai-model-eDGI3sNRnhg]

The episode also tracks xAI's turbulence (Cursor leadership hires, six co-founder departures this year), Cursor's talks to raise at a $50 billion valuation, Anthropic's Pentagon-stalled consulting venture with Blackstone, an AMA survey showing 81% of doctors using AI, and Sam Altman's BlackRock-conference vision of intelligence as a metered utility.[^meta-delays-new-ai-model-eDGI3sNRnhg]

# Key points

- Meta's Avocado: delivered for internal testing in January (CTO Andrew Bosworth called it "very good" but flagged remaining RL work); a new applied AI division reports to Bosworth rather than AI CEO Alexandr Wang, feeding — strenuously denied — rumors that Zuckerberg was done with Wang.[^meta-delays-new-ai-model-eDGI3sNRnhg]
- xAI hired Cursor's product-engineering heads Andrew Milich and Jason Ginsburg, reporting directly to Musk, who admits xAI is behind on coding but expects to "catch up and exceed" competitors by mid-year.[^meta-delays-new-ai-model-eDGI3sNRnhg]
- xAI co-founder exodus: Zhigang Dai left and Guodong Zhang plans to, making six departures this year — only three of twelve co-founders remain, one being Musk; Musk frames it as controlled demolition: "xAI was not built right the first time around, so it's being rebuilt from the foundations up. Same thing happened with Tesla."[^meta-delays-new-ai-model-eDGI3sNRnhg]
- Cursor is in talks to raise at a $50 billion valuation, nearly double November's $29.3 billion round, after doubling revenue to $2 billion; CEO Michael Truell declared "wartime" internally, with a pivot to automated coding tools and training its own state-of-the-art models to reduce lab dependency.[^meta-delays-new-ai-model-eDGI3sNRnhg]
- Anthropic is in talks with Blackstone and other PE firms to launch a dedicated AI consulting venture, but Blackstone leaders including Stephen Schwarzman are wary of announcing while Anthropic remains in conflict with the Pentagon; Blackstone discussed a similar plan with OpenAI.[^meta-delays-new-ai-model-eDGI3sNRnhg]
- AMA survey: 81% of doctors now use AI professionally — more than double 2023 levels — led by research summarization, discharge instructions, and appointment documentation; only 17% use assistive diagnosis, matching the AMA's "augmented intelligence" framing.[^meta-delays-new-ai-model-eDGI3sNRnhg]
- Altman at BlackRock: "Fundamentally, our business is going to look like selling tokens... intelligence is a utility like electricity or water, and people buy it from us on a meter" — with skyrocketing agentic demand possibly meaning high prices or rationing.[^meta-delays-new-ai-model-eDGI3sNRnhg]
- Altman says "AGI" has lost all meaning; his two milestones: the majority of the world's intelligence living in data centers (possibly by 2028, with huge error bars), and leaders being unable to do their jobs without AI — plus his warning that AI-driven abundance will force "a rapid shift in how capitalism works," with a painful few-year adjustment despite his not being "a long-term jobs doomer."[^meta-delays-new-ai-model-eDGI3sNRnhg]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's editorial notes: on Meta, waiting to ship a genuinely good model beats releasing one nobody is impressed by, but the model battle remains "distinctly uphill." On the consulting stories, the common thread is that enterprises are lagging and implementation will take enormous time-on-task and human bodies — he predicts massive expansions of forward-deployed engineering departments, partnerships with existing consulting firms, and new venture spin-ups "all at once and more." And on Altman's milestone of jobs becoming supervision of AI, he quips: "that pretty much describes my job already."[^meta-delays-new-ai-model-eDGI3sNRnhg]
