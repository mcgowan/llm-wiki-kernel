# Overview

Kicking off the AI Daily Brief's Q2 build week, Nathaniel Whittemore presents highlights from an 87-slide quarterly state-of-AI report drawing on the prior quarter's episodes, monthly pulse surveys, and 400-plus sources tracked by his team of Open Claw researchers. His framing: Q1 2026 was the most consequential quarter in AI since ChatGPT launched, and constitutes "AI's second moment" — where the first moment was viable chatbot assistants, the second is workable agentic systems, with far higher stakes across capability, capital, corporate reality, and politics.[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]

He traces the inflection to the holiday break, when the combination of new models and harnesses like Claude Code and Codex became visible to people with time away from work, and follows the consequences through the Open Claw phenomenon, the SaaS selloff, Anthropic's enterprise surge, function-by-function enterprise adoption data, and the Pentagon-Anthropic standoff.[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]

# Key points

- Whittemore contrasts the two moments by stakes: from 100 million users in five weeks to billions of weekly actives; from speculative venture bets to a planned $650 billion in hyperscaler capex this year, a $400 billion SaaS wipeout, and funding rounds worth tens of billions; from first explorations to AI-first mandates and 40% staff cuts.[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]
- Claude Code grew from $1 billion to $2.5 billion annualized in a couple of months; Claude Co-work launched in January, reportedly triggered emergency meetings at Microsoft, and was itself built entirely with Claude Code.[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]
- More frontier capability shipped in 90 days than any quarter in AI history — GPT-5.2 Codex, Genie 3, Opus 4.6, GPT-5.3 Codex, Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Nano Banana 2, GPT-5.4 — with no single benchmark winner and constant jockeying across GPQA Diamond, SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-bench, METR long task horizon, and GDPval.[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]
- Open Claw became the most-starred open source project ever on GitHub after evolving from Claude Bot through Multbot; Jensen Huang called it maybe the most important software release ever, and Notion, Perplexity, and Nvidia (Nemo Claw) raced to match it while Anthropic absorbed its features natively.[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]
- Whittemore describes OpenAI and Anthropic converging from opposite directions: OpenAI consolidating product sprawl inward into a super app after Sam Altman's December code red, Anthropic extending its single core tool outward into an ecosystem.[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]
- Revenue growth across the sector: Cursor doubled from $1B to $2B annualized, Lovable reached $400M including a $100M single-month jump, Replit projects $1B ARR by end of 2026, and Anthropic hit a $19 billion run rate against OpenAI's roughly $25 billion.[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]
- Per Ramp data, Anthropic took 70% of first-time enterprise AI buyers versus OpenAI's 25%; Gartner projects 40% of enterprises will have working agents in production by end of 2026, aided by agent credit cards from Ramp and Stripe.[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]
- Pulsia, which produces fully agentic companies, reached $6 million annualized with a single founder and zero employees; founder Ben Sera said the zero-employee company "isn't a thought experiment anymore."[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]
- Pulse survey findings: 71%+ of respondents vibe coded in the past month, automation or agentic use cases reached 62%, and the average respondent uses 3.5 models. Time savings fell from 19.9% of surveyed use cases in January to 13.6% in February, while increased output ranked first and new capabilities second, rising from 22% to over 26%.[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]
- Function-level data: 91% of businesses at least experimenting with customer service chatbots while 64% of customers say they prefer no AI; Anthropic research found AI within reach of ~80% of legal work but adoption observed in only 15%; 91% of finance firms report low impact, citing data quality; HR AI deployment grew from 19% to 61% in twelve months; 63% of tracked sales use cases are "primetime"; the generative engine optimization market is projected to grow from under $1B in 2025 to nearly $34B by 2034.[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]
- Politically, the Pentagon-Anthropic standoff escalated to Anthropic being designated a supply chain risk and suing; OpenAI's Department of War agreement drew a 775% surge in one-star ChatGPT reviews while Claude hit number one in the App Store for the first time. Data center politics, Trump's hyperscaler commitments on consumer costs, a Time magazine "people versus AI" cover, and a White House legislative framework rounded out the quarter.[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's headline conclusion is that the competitive battle has moved past the model to the agent platform — the defining contest of the quarter was Claude Code versus Codex versus Open Claw, not GPT-5.4 versus Opus 4.6 — while every AI product converges toward every other AI product, a trend he illustrates with Lovable and Replit's expansions and Peter Yang's line that code is the foundation of all knowledge work. For enterprises he insists "time savings ain't it," and that reasoning about new capabilities is far more profitable. He predicts the capability overhang between leaders and laggards will widen before it closes, giving those on the leading edge extreme compounding gains, and notes AI will only grow as a political issue heading into the midterms. He characterizes the moment as one of the most exciting and most destabilizing transitions ever seen, with the discourse "at an 11."[^the-state-of-ai-q2-2026-LZeMZgJszKU]
