# Overview

Returning from two weeks in South America, Nathaniel Whittemore identifies the most important trend he missed as "the OpenClaw-ification of AI" — a wave of products suddenly resembling OpenClaw. Anthropic shipped Claude Code remote control (kick off a task in the terminal, manage it from your phone) and, one day later, scheduled tasks in Co-work (recurring automated work like morning briefs and Friday spreadsheet updates). Perplexity announced Perplexity Computer, an orchestration system running parallel agents across 19 models; Notion launched custom agents ("the AI team that never sleeps"); Airtable shipped Superagent — and OpenAI hired OpenClaw creator Peter Steinberger outright.[^the-openclaw-ification-of-ai-GR-j31Nrl0Y]

Whittemore's thesis is that these moves are not companies chasing a hot project. OpenClaw was simply first to popularize the fundamental primitives of the agentic era: interactive modalities that follow you across devices, persistent work that happens without prompting, and agents with permissioned access to your personal context and systems. These primitives, he argues, will become ubiquitous because they unlock things people clearly want.[^the-openclaw-ification-of-ai-GR-j31Nrl0Y]

# Key points

- Claude Code remote control lets users start a task in the terminal and control the session from the Claude app or claude.ai/code while it runs on their machine; reactions ranged from "Anthropic just saved the San Francisco nightlife" to "OpenClaw for grown-ups."[^the-openclaw-ification-of-ai-GR-j31Nrl0Y]
- The on-the-go interaction pattern — OpenClaw's Telegram/WhatsApp access — was the feature people most wanted mainstreamed; demand was so strong Claude Code's PM had to apologize for capacity issues the day after launch.[^the-openclaw-ification-of-ai-GR-j31Nrl0Y]
- A circulating comparison framed Claude Code as "your work engine" (repo-aware, session-based, surgical) versus OpenClaw as "your life engine" (24/7 butler on a VPS); Whittemore disputes the personal-only framing of OpenClaw, noting his most useful agents are 24/7 research agents feeding the show and AIDB Intelligence.[^the-openclaw-ification-of-ai-GR-j31Nrl0Y]
- Co-work scheduled tasks mirror OpenClaw's core magic — heartbeats (default 30-minute self-reminders of mission) plus cron jobs; Whittemore's five project-manager agents run on exactly such schedules.[^the-openclaw-ification-of-ai-GR-j31Nrl0Y]
- Akash Gupta: "Scheduled tasks means Claude stopped being software you talk to and became software that works while you sleep. That's a category change, not a feature update" — each automated task "used to be a SaaS product, a Zapier workflow, or a junior employee's morning routine," now a line in a $20/month subscription.[^the-openclaw-ification-of-ai-GR-j31Nrl0Y]
- Simon Willison found remote control "a little bit janky" and noted scheduled tasks only run while the computer is awake with the desktop app open — a local-device-management gap Whittemore felt personally when Hudson Valley snowstorms knocked his machines offline abroad.[^the-openclaw-ification-of-ai-GR-j31Nrl0Y]
- Perplexity Computer "unifies every current AI capability into one system," orchestrating parallel agents and matching tasks to the best of 19 available models; CEO Aravind Srinivas's blog "The AI is the computer" argues the computer, not chat, is the UI for entire workflows, and the future is "massively multimodal orchestration."[^the-openclaw-ification-of-ai-GR-j31Nrl0Y]
- Notion custom agents are autonomous, trigger- or schedule-driven, multiplayer, and model-agnostic — "OpenClaw for regular people," no Mac Mini or cron-job vocabulary required, though per-seat pricing is a drawback.[^the-openclaw-ification-of-ai-GR-j31Nrl0Y]
- On white-collar fears, Whittemore says the view that this only eliminates employees "rather than the mass empowerment of employees to do more will seem in retrospect fairly reductionist."[^the-openclaw-ification-of-ai-GR-j31Nrl0Y]

# Takeaways

Whittemore concludes these are "not features to be copied, they are new fundamental primitives of the agentic era" — and OpenClaw will be remembered not as a hype phase but as "the starting gun for a totally new era of AI." He pushes back on the advice to skip OpenClaw's difficult setup now that productized versions exist: doing the hard work of configuring it yourself is the best education in the primitives that the polished products abstract away. He expects the OpenClaw-ification to continue throughout the year.[^the-openclaw-ification-of-ai-GR-j31Nrl0Y]
