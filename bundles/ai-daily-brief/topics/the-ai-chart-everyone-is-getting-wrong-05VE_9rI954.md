# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore's main-story target is the Silicon Data LLM Token Expenditure Index, featured in a Citadel Securities research note called "Tokenomics," whose downward-sloping line sent Wall Street and social media from "token maxing to token panic" almost overnight. He argues the chart says nothing about token demand, token volume, or total expenditure: Silicon Data itself clarified it is a usage-weighted average price index — what the market is currently paying per million tokens — and, crucially, it draws data only from third-party token routers, services whose entire purpose is routing users to cheaper models. That sampling bias, Whittemore argues, greatly exaggerates any shift away from expensive frontier tokens, and even Citadel's actual note is far less bombastic than the viral screenshots, arguing only that frontier inference will concentrate among firms best equipped to use it.[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]

The headlines segment covers the SpaceX IPO — the largest in history, priced at $135/share for a valuation just shy of $1.8 trillion — plus Jeff Bezos's Prometheus raising $12B at a $41B valuation, Meta's forced operational split from Manas under Chinese government orders, Google diversifying chip suppliers beyond TSMC, KKR and Nvidia's $10B Helix Digital Infrastructure venture, and Goldman Sachs's claim that consensus AI CapEx estimates are too conservative.[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]

# Key points

- SpaceX's IPO drew over $100B in retail orders against $75B of stock sold; the flat $135/share pricing implies a ~$1.8T valuation, debuting as the world's seventh-largest company, and would put Elon Musk (est. $971B) on the verge of becoming the first trillionaire.[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]
- Whittemore rejects reading SpaceX as a proxy for coming Anthropic/OpenAI IPOs: Musk "operates in his own vortex," and the eleventh-hour Neocloud pivot makes it an infrastructure story, not a model-company pricing.[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]
- Bezos's Prometheus raised $12B at $41B to build an "artificial general engineer"; Bezos predicts AI creates a labor shortage ("shrinking the number of people needed by 10x, AI will create 10x more opportunities"), and a reported $100B fund would roll up industrial companies — acquiring factories to get physical-economy data that "can't be scraped."[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]
- Meta completed a firewalled operational split from Manas ($2B acquisition) on Beijing's orders; the crackdown on the "red-chip" Singapore structure is pushing StepFun, Moonshot, and Kling to reincorporate in China, with officials reportedly seizing researchers' passports.[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]
- TSMC backlogs are driving Google to evaluate Samsung's 2nm process for parts of its 10th-gen "Icefish" TPUs and to order Intel advanced packaging for 2028 — a supply chain splitting the processor from less-sensitive components.[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]
- KKR and Nvidia announced Helix Digital Infrastructure with $10B committed, Kuwait sovereign wealth and Vistra attached, led by former AWS CEO Adam Selipsky — while JLL reports nearly half of US data center projects are delayed.[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]
- Goldman strategists led by Ryan Hammond call consensus 2027 hyperscaler CapEx "too conservative," projecting $1.1T baseline ($1.4T bullish) versus the $920B median estimate, on an assumption of 24x token-consumption growth through 2030.[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]
- Silicon Data admitted its index "should really have been named the token expenditure price index"; it measures average price paid per million tokens — mid-June prices simply fell back to early-May levels — not demand, volume, or spend.[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]
- The index excludes all direct customer relationships with OpenAI and Anthropic — the vast majority of token expenditure — sampling only routers built to reduce cost, which Whittemore says exaggerates the apparent shift to cheaper models.[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]
- Ramp data: the top 1% of "fully AI-pilled" firms spend ~$7,500 per employee per month on AI, the top 10% spend $610, and the median firm just $11.38 — versus Uber's $1,500/employee cap, implying enormous headroom for total-demand growth.[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]
- On rumored OpenAI price cuts: analyst Max Weinbach estimates ~70% margins on inference-intensive API tokens, meaning OpenAI "could cut prices by like 60% and still be profitable."[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]

# Takeaways

Whittemore concludes the chart does carry signal — it reflects the shift from the token subsidy era to the token scarcity era he has been tracking, with every AI company now "in the token efficiency business" — but the doom reading is wrong: growth in total AI consumption as median firms move from $11 toward $1,500 per employee will "massively outweigh" any migration from expensive to cheap tokens. Expensive AI concentrating among firms that use it best "is not an AI bubble popping, that's an AI market rationalizing" — markets allocating scarce resources at the right price. And his parting heuristic: "if you ever see someone end a tweet with an ellipsis, run in the other direction."[^the-ai-chart-everyone-is-getting-wrong-05VE_9rI954]
