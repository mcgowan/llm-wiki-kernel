# Overview

In this action-oriented Big Think/Long Reads weekend episode, Nathaniel Whittemore covers two practical items: Ethan Mollick's latest "opinionated guide to which AI to use to do stuff," and the launch of AIDB's newest free training program, the AI Summer Adventure at summeradventure.ai. Whittemore's reading of Mollick's guide is that its real contribution isn't tool-by-tool recommendations but a bright line between two interaction patterns — old chat-based back-and-forth, for which almost any model suffices, versus the new "managing agents" pattern, where an agentic system with a computer can do hours of human work in one go.[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]

The Summer Adventure is the fourth in AIDB's sequence of free programs (after AIDB New Year, Claw Camp, and Agent OS), designed as a choose-your-own-adventure with 20+ "destinations" spanning beginner quick trips, excursions, and ambitious expeditions — all aimed at closing each person's individual "capability overhang," the gap between what AI can do and what they actually use it for.[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]

# Key points

- Mollick's top-line advice: for low-stakes questions any free model is fine; for answers you care about, use a premier model like Fable or GPT 5.6 Soul at a high thinking level; for real intensive work there are "effectively only two choices right now — ChatGPT or Claude."[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]
- Gemini is officially out of Mollick's rankings: Google "has no leading frontier model and has nothing close to Codex and Code," though he still points to Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) and rich-media models like Gemini Omni.[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]
- Mollick's email-prep demo: both ChatGPT and Claude connected to his Gmail, correctly inferred "Monday the 21st" meant September, and produced teaching materials in ~10 minutes — but ChatGPT actually sent an email to his colleague because he'd previously granted it send permission, while Claude drafted and asked first. Lesson: permissions matter a lot; leave everything on ask-for-approval until you trust the system.[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]
- With computer use enabled, the AI "can literally take over your mouse, browser, and computer" — Mollick had GPT 5.6 Soul and Codex download Blender and model "an otter using a laptop on an airplane."[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]
- Anti-hallucination update: given his professionally edited book PDF, the AI worked 30 minutes, chased down 195 references, and produced pages of notes with no hallucinated page numbers or invented text — its flaw was being "incredibly nitpicky," reinforcing that working with these systems "is more like managing than it is chatting."[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]
- The AI Summer Adventure organizes 20+ destinations by level (beginner/intermediate/advanced) and topic (tools, agents and automation, building and creating, knowledge and context), with a passport-stamp gamification ladder: day tripper (1), certified AI explorer (3), trailblazer (6), globetrotter (10).[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]
- Example quick trip "Pack Your ID": build a 150-300 word global identity block any AI can load, with install steps, a verification test question, and extensions like "how to push back on me"; the advanced version, "Personal Brain," is a small context-file set akin to Whittemore's contextportfolio.ai builder.[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]
- Expedition "Lemonade Stand": design an AI-staffed microbusiness across three sprints — idea discovery (5-8 ideas, ranked top three with founder-fit notes), business plan with an AI org chart, and a validation sprint testing real demand before overbuilding.[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]
- Expedition "The Loop": build an actual agentic loop in non-technical work, with background learning on what a loop is and isn't, how loops fail, and how to fire a loop in Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex.[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]
- About half the destinations are unlocked at launch, with more added weekly through early September; the program is completely free.[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]

# Takeaways

Whittemore argues Mollick's seemingly simple guide is doing important work: making the agent-management interaction pattern approachable for a wide audience that would glaze over at words like "harness," because the shift from chatting to managing agents requires "not only learning new skills, but reprogramming your brain." He contends that nobody — not even lab researchers or full-time AI content creators — is free of a capability overhang, and that vibe coding a first application remains "one of the biggest unlocks," shifting people from seeing AI as an assistant that speeds up current work to something that unlocks entirely new capabilities.[^how-to-get-the-most-from-ai-this-summer-kvmkj3dj_kw]
