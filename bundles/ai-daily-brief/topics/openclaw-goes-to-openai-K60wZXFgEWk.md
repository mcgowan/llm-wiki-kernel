# Overview

Whittemore recounts how OpenClaw went from Peter Steinberger's curiosity project (started as "Clawdbot" in late November 2025) to "the most important thing in AI" in under three months — encapsulating the agentic shift, driving a Mac Mini buying frenzy, spawning the Moltbook agent social network (now over 2.7 million agents), and racking up GitHub stars past VS Code, 2x PyTorch, and 3x Claude Code. The arc culminated on Sunday night with Sam Altman announcing Steinberger is joining OpenAI "to drive the next generation of personal agents," while OpenClaw moves into an independent foundation that OpenAI will continue to support as open source.[^openclaw-goes-to-openai-K60wZXFgEWk]

The episode weighs the discourse: was this "the fumble of the decade by Anthropic," which responded to the fastest-growing open-source project of all time — one named after and powered by its own product — with a cease-and-desist rather than collaboration, while Sam Altman and Mark Zuckerberg reached out with offers? Whittemore explains OpenClaw's real value through the concept of a Schelling point: the uncoordinated focal point where all agent-building energy has concentrated, making the community — not the technology — the moat.[^openclaw-goes-to-openai-K60wZXFgEWk]

# Key points

- Timeline: Steinberger published Clawdbot on GitHub November 25, 2025; 82 days later some asked if he'd built "the first one-person $1 billion company"; the name went Clawdbot → Moltbot (after Anthropic's cease-and-desist) → OpenClaw on January 30, with Steinberger clearing the new name by calling Sam Altman directly.[^openclaw-goes-to-openai-K60wZXFgEWk]
- Alex Finn's Mac Mini "first employee" post got 2 million views; his agent Henry vibe-coded for 48 hours straight — "Vibe coding is dead. Vibe orchestration has arrived."[^openclaw-goes-to-openai-K60wZXFgEWk]
- Moltbook grew from 2,000 agents to 100,000 in a day and now has more than 2.7 million agents — "a fascinating experiment in the anthropology of agents cosplaying as sentient."[^openclaw-goes-to-openai-K60wZXFgEWk]
- On the Lex Fridman podcast, Steinberger revealed acquisition offers from OpenAI and Meta, said he was losing $10-20K/month running OpenClaw, had VC offers but didn't want to be a CEO again after 13 years building a company.[^openclaw-goes-to-openai-K60wZXFgEWk]
- Altman's announcement: agents interacting with each other "will quickly become core to our product offerings"; Steinberger's stated mission is "to build an agent that even my mom can use," and "the claw is the law."[^openclaw-goes-to-openai-K60wZXFgEWk]
- OpenClaw's third-party app development velocity 60 days post-launch exceeded Android, iOS, and Facebook; Dave Morin is founding independent board member of the OpenClaw foundation — "open source forever."[^openclaw-goes-to-openai-K60wZXFgEWk]
- Anthropic-fumble takes: Nader Dabit — "they chose violence"; skeptics countered it was just "a Claude Code wrapper that wastes tokens like crazy."[^openclaw-goes-to-openai-K60wZXFgEWk]
- Whittemore's Schelling-point argument: history is littered with equal-or-better technologies that lost for lack of a community focal point; hundreds of thousands of developers and a self-reinforcing resource ecosystem make OpenClaw the place to build agents — which is why he built his own free program, clawcamp.ai, on it.[^openclaw-goes-to-openai-K60wZXFgEWk]
- For OpenAI specifically, Steinberger fills a developer-hero vacuum left by departed co-founders (Sutskever, Karpathy) and lands just as Codex is being spoken of in the same breath as — or above — Claude Code.[^openclaw-goes-to-openai-K60wZXFgEWk]

# Takeaways

Whittemore sees this as "one of those moments for AI": the acquisition validates that agents are now delivering real value, not just promise, and Simon Smith's take — the best outcome for everyone except Anthropic, with agent capabilities democratized to the largest chatbot user base — captures the stakes. He notes enterprise advisers report agentic platforms are generating the most positive C-suite energy since ChatGPT launched, and that even if ownership changed, the paradigm shift Clawdbot represented — owning your AI assistant, conversations, and memory — can be rebuilt on any harness and is here to stay.[^openclaw-goes-to-openai-K60wZXFgEWk]
