# Overview

Whittemore reviews two enterprise AI surveys — KPMG's quarterly pulse survey of leaders at organizations with $1B+ revenue, and BCG's AI Radar — and finds a consistent through-line: CEOs are no longer delegating AI. KPMG's Steve Chase frames AI as "becoming the backbone of enterprise strategy" with a growing divide between leaders scaling fast and organizations stalling after early deployments. Whittemore argues this shift "couldn't come soon enough," because the coming challenges of AI are far bigger than those to date and only the top of the organization has the power to drive the necessary changes.[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]

He also reads the surveys against his own show's AI ROI benchmarking study — notably that C-levels and founders report significantly higher ROI than other title levels — and interprets a drop in reported agent deployments not as retreat but as a maturation in what respondents understand agents to actually be.[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]

# Key points

- KPMG: average planned AI spend is $124M over the next 12 months (up from Q1's $114M and Q2's $88M dip, near Q3's $130M peak); 67% say AI stays a top priority even in a recession; 59% will keep investing even without measurable ROI — "AI investment becoming recession proof."[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]
- ROI expectations pulled forward dramatically: in KPMG's 2024 CEO outlook, 63% expected ROI in 3-5 years; in 2025, two-thirds said 1-3 years and a fifth said 6-12 months; in the Q4 pulse, 59% expect measurable ROI within 12 months.[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]
- ROI metrics are diversifying: decision-making improvement in the C-suite jumped nearly 20 percentage points as a measure — matching NLW's survey, where strategic use cases like decision-making reported higher overall ROI.[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]
- Reported agent deployment went 11% (Q1) → 33% (Q2) → 42% (Q3) → 26% (Q4); NLW reads the drop as terminology maturation — his own survey of 5,000+ use cases classified ~56% assisted, ~30% automation, and just under 14% truly agentic, with automation+agentic nearly matching KPMG's inflated 42%.[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]
- Maturation evidence: two-thirds cite agentic system complexity as the top barrier; inconsistent agent use rose from 19% to 45%, unclear enterprise strategy from 20% to 32%, and lack of organizational infrastructure (41%) more than tripled in two quarters — a number NLW expects to "do nothing but rocket up" as data and context strategies hit their limits.[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]
- Workforce impact: 41% say agents changed hiring for experienced roles, 64% for entry-level; 71% have hired an AI prompt engineer, 59% an AI performance analyst, 58% an AI trainer/data curator; 76% would pay up to 10% more for strong AI skills, with adaptability (63%) and critical thinking (61%) most sought.[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]
- Security: half plan $10-50M in the coming year to harden model governance, data lineage, and agentic architectures; 71% call cyber a key factor in re-evaluating GenAI strategy; 80% call cybersecurity one of the greatest barriers to AI goals.[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]
- NLW's ROI study: C-levels and founders reported mean use-case ROI of 3.59 (between "modest" and "significant"), over 50% of their use cases rated significant or transformational — nearly double other levels — and they were least likely to report negative ROI.[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]
- BCG AI Radar: 94% of CEOs will keep investing even without a 2026 payoff; 72% say they are the main AI decision maker (a CIO-led to CEO-led shift); 82% are more optimistic about ROI than last year; half believe their job stability depends on getting AI right; ~90% believe agents enable measurable ROI this year, with over 30% of AI spend committed to agentic AI.[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]
- Regional divide: Western CEOs act from fear of falling behind; CEOs in China and India act because they see value; 58% of leading organizations expect AI-driven changes in governance and decision rights, and 90% of CEOs believe AI will significantly transform what success looks like by 2028.[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's conclusion ties the data to his emerging 2026 thesis: an inflection point is fundamentally changing AI's disruption profile for big organizations, and if that's right, CEO leadership isn't optional — "they're the only ones who have enough power to actually make the changes that are going to be necessary." He highlights the consistency across multiple independent studies as the most convincing signal.[^why-ceos-need-to-lead-ai-strategy-9UWShopAFaE]
