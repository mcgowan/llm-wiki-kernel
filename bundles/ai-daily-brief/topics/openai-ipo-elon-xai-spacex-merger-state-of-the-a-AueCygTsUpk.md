# Overview

Whittemore surveys the state of the AI race entering February 2026 through four big-player stories. Apple made the second-largest acquisition in its history — $2 billion for stealth startup Q.ai, whose patented optical-sensor technology can read facial skin micro-movements as speech, potentially enabling silent communication with an AI assistant through headphones or glasses. Whittemore reads it as Apple effectively passing on the foundation-model race (having partnered with Google for Siri's models) and doubling down on hardware, where its M-series chips and the Mac Mini — flying off shelves thanks to the OpenClaw craze — are its real AI wins.[^openai-ipo-elon-xai-spacex-merger-state-of-the-a-AueCygTsUpk]

Meanwhile Amazon is reportedly negotiating a $50 billion investment in OpenAI even as it cuts 16,000 more jobs; OpenAI has pushed up IPO plans to Q4 over private concerns Anthropic could beat it to market; and Reuters reports SpaceX and xAI are in merger talks, with a Tesla merger floated as an alternative — the consolidation of "Elon Inc."[^openai-ipo-elon-xai-spacex-merger-state-of-the-a-AueCygTsUpk]

# Key points

- Apple paid $2B for Q.ai (per the Financial Times) — second only to the $3B Beats deal; Q.ai was acquired out of stealth with no product, and its CEO Aviad Maizels previously sold PrimeSense (the tech behind Xbox Kinect and Face ID) to Apple.[^openai-ipo-elon-xai-spacex-merger-state-of-the-a-AueCygTsUpk]
- Whittemore notes Anthropic is now valued around $350B — versus ~$60B when he argued Apple should buy it — putting it far outside acquisition range.[^openai-ipo-elon-xai-spacex-merger-state-of-the-a-AueCygTsUpk]
- Apple posted its biggest iPhone quarter ever ($85B in smartphone revenue, 23% top-line growth) while Tim Cook's AI monetization answer was, in Whittemore's words, "impressive word salad"; TechCrunch: "Guys, I don't think Tim Cook knows how to monetize AI."[^openai-ipo-elon-xai-spacex-merger-state-of-the-a-AueCygTsUpk]
- The WSJ reports Amazon is the lion's share of a rumored $60B investment round in OpenAI (with Nvidia and Microsoft), at roughly $50B — about 6% of OpenAI at its rumored $830B valuation target and about half of Amazon's prior-year capex — with Andy Jassy negotiating directly with Altman; Amazon already holds a stake north of 10% in Anthropic from its $8B 2024 investment, a "win-win-win" hedge in Whittemore's framing.[^openai-ipo-elon-xai-spacex-merger-state-of-the-a-AueCygTsUpk]
- Amazon announced 16,000 further layoffs (after 14,000 in October — roughly 8% of its 350,000 corporate base); laid-off L7 and Texas congressional candidate Lee Plum argued "AI becomes the excuse, not the cause," blaming outsourcing and a guardrail-free global labor market; ASML similarly posted record orders while cutting ~1,700 jobs.[^openai-ipo-elon-xai-spacex-merger-state-of-the-a-AueCygTsUpk]
- Per the WSJ, OpenAI has pushed IPO plans up to Q4, hired a chief accounting officer and corporate finance officer, and executives have "privately expressed concerns about Anthropic beating the company to an IPO"; Anthropic has told bankers it's open to a year-end IPO too.[^openai-ipo-elon-xai-spacex-merger-state-of-the-a-AueCygTsUpk]
- Whittemore games out why going second is dangerous for OpenAI: a smash Anthropic IPO would cement an "ascendant Anthropic" narrative and absorb investor dollars; a flop could slam the IPO window shut and force OpenAI to raise another $100B privately at a markdown.[^openai-ipo-elon-xai-spacex-merger-state-of-the-a-AueCygTsUpk]
- Tesla invested $2B in xAI despite a failed shareholder vote; Reuters reports SpaceX-xAI merger talks with new holding companies already formed, and Bloomberg reports a possible SpaceX-Tesla merger as an alternative — synergies include xAI models for Optimus robots and orbital data-center compute; commentator "Signal" noted Twitter-buyout investors' equity morphed into xAI exposure with a path into SpaceX, "a control layer bet with insane optionality."[^openai-ipo-elon-xai-spacex-merger-state-of-the-a-AueCygTsUpk]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's TL;DR: "In January of 2026, the AI race got even crazier than it's ever been." He sees Apple's strategy — intentional or not — resolving toward hardware and devices rather than models; Amazon constructing a position where it wins whichever lab prevails; OpenAI's IPO urgency as rational rather than vanity; and Musk's empire consolidation as unsurprising to anyone following his ambitions at the intersection of AI, space, and the physical world. He also flags a trend to watch: companies growing strongly while still downsizing, and outsourcing — not AI — emerging as the real political bugaboo in layoff narratives.[^openai-ipo-elon-xai-spacex-merger-state-of-the-a-AueCygTsUpk]
