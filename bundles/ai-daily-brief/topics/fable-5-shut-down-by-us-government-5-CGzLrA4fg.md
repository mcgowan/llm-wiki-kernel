# Overview

In the first Friday-night emergency episode in the show's three-year history, Nathaniel Whittemore covers the US government's export-control directive — delivered to Anthropic at 5:21 p.m. and announced by the company just before 9 p.m. Eastern — suspending all access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 by any foreign national, inside or outside the United States, including Anthropic's own foreign-national employees. The net effect: Anthropic abruptly disabled both models for all customers to ensure compliance. The Wall Street Journal reported Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick sent the letter to Dario Amodei; the trigger was apparently a jailbreak report — per the Journal, from researchers at Amazon, a Glasswing partner and Anthropic investor — that Anthropic says demonstrated only a narrow technique surfacing minor, previously known vulnerabilities also findable with public methods and other models including GPT-5.5.[^fable-5-shut-down-by-us-government-5-CGzLrA4fg]

Whittemore surveys a discourse whose summary he endorses via Dan Robustus: "Am I mad at Anthropic or the US government? Both? Probably both." Critics called the government's action cartoonish, hypocritical (weeks after the White House disclaimed model oversight as "government overreach"), and possibly personal retaliation against Anthropic — while much of the industry blamed Anthropic's own safety "fear-mongering" for inviting exactly this outcome.[^fable-5-shut-down-by-us-government-5-CGzLrA4fg]

# Key points

- Anthropic's response: no tester has found a universal jailbreak; "perfect jailbreak resistance is not currently possible for any model provider," which is why it adopted defense-in-depth plus the controversial 30-day data retention; the government provided only verbal evidence of a narrow potential jailbreak (asking the model to read a codebase and fix flaws). Applying this standard industry-wide "would essentially halt all new model deployments."[^fable-5-shut-down-by-us-government-5-CGzLrA4fg]
- Per Axios (via Prinz's timeline), the government had asked Anthropic to pause the release beforehand and was refused.[^fable-5-shut-down-by-us-government-5-CGzLrA4fg]
- Many Anthropic technical staff — including Andrej Karpathy — are non-citizens on visas like EB-1 and are barred from touching the models internally.[^fable-5-shut-down-by-us-government-5-CGzLrA4fg]
- Government-side critics: Katie Moussouris called it "a complete overreaction... exactly the kind of prompting that defenders would do"; Dean Ball couldn't tell "if this is lawfare against Anthropic... Regardless, it's simply cartoonish"; Chris Miller called Commerce's export-control strategy "incoherent and self-defeating" — sending chips to China while blocking US models. A Department of War CIO's tweet jabbing "pre-IPO valuation" suggested animosity aimed at Anthropic specifically.[^fable-5-shut-down-by-us-government-5-CGzLrA4fg]
- Anthropic-side critics: Jeremy Howard — "how did Anthropic not see this coming?"; Nick Carter — "I can't believe Anthropic comparing their product to nuclear weapons 800 times backfired on them"; a viral cartoon had Dario warning "this is the most dangerous AI yet," Trump saying "Okay, it's banned," and Dario replying "You can't do this." Critics also resurfaced Anthropic's own blog line that government "should have the power to block or deter deployment"; Anthropic countered that this action wasn't the transparent, statutory process it endorsed.[^fable-5-shut-down-by-us-government-5-CGzLrA4fg]
- Eliezer Yudkowsky notably refused to celebrate, saying he couldn't tell on day one whether the outcome lands good or bad.[^fable-5-shut-down-by-us-government-5-CGzLrA4fg]
- Precedent concerns: Aaron Levie called it "a big turning point for AI regulation"; Sterling Crispin coined "capability thought crimes"; Brian Chau mapped compliance chaos — citizenship verification across Cursor, Devin, OpenRouter, and every downstream API — and noted OpenAI and Google DeepMind now have incentives not to ship Mythos-caliber models.[^fable-5-shut-down-by-us-government-5-CGzLrA4fg]
- Sovereignty fallout: Garry Tan said the US's "rule of law, predictable provider" asymmetry versus China "just evaporated," giving procurement officers in Brussels, Tokyo, or São Paulo a defensible case for sovereign hedging or Chinese open-weight alternatives; UK politician Tom Tugendhat and European commentators framed it as what technological dependence looks like.[^fable-5-shut-down-by-us-government-5-CGzLrA4fg]
- Market stakes: Daniel Wu argued the ban undermines the AI capex bull case if frontier models can't be monetized; others questioned Anthropic's trillion-dollar IPO math with 25% of global market share cut off, and Robert Scoble questioned whether Amodei survives the week.[^fable-5-shut-down-by-us-government-5-CGzLrA4fg]

# Takeaways

Whittemore compares the moment to Sam Altman's firing and reinstatement at OpenAI — an event that permanently reshaped Microsoft's trust and strategy — suggesting that whatever the resolution, trust in US frontier access has been structurally damaged: "everyone around the world who is not American has to be feeling very different about things than they were just a day ago." He believes the American economy now substantially rests on Anthropic's and OpenAI's revenue growth and continued investor funding of the AI buildout, making the damage from this move "hard to overstate." His base case is that the directive gets reversed in some way, but he calls it an incredibly dramatic step and promises updates as they happen.[^fable-5-shut-down-by-us-government-5-CGzLrA4fg]
