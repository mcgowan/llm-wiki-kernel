# Overview

In the headlines, OpenAI confidentially files IPO paperwork — joining Anthropic and SpaceX in what one commentator calls potentially "the most incredible IPO run in history" — while Elon Musk details SpaceX's space data-center plans (150 kW AI satellites, a "Gigasat" factory, 1 GW annualized orbital capacity by end of 2027) ahead of a red-hot SpaceX listing that is roughly two times oversubscribed. Whittemore also covers Google and Nvidia quietly adding Intel as a backup fab as TSMC runs out of capacity, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan exploring compute futures, and Washington's flurry of AI bills from federal preemption negotiations to Adam Schiff's Pentagon restrictions.[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]

The main episode examines OpenAI's blog post "Built to Benefit Everyone," co-authored by Sam Altman and Jakub Pachocki, which declares OpenAI's "third phase": three goals — build an automated AI researcher (with "a significant fraction" of research done by AI systems by March 2028), accelerate the economy, and give everyone on Earth a personal AGI — wrapped in rhetoric about broad distribution of power. Contrasting this with Apple's underwhelming-but-fine Siri AI launch at WWDC, Whittemore floats his own thesis: consumer chatbot AI and agentic work AI may now be fundamentally different things that we should stop lumping together.[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]

# Key points

- OpenAI filed its IPO confidentially on Monday; the company said timing is undecided and "it may be a while," while Anthropic's Daniela Amodei similarly framed her company's filing as just creating an option — judging by SpaceX's pace, September is the earliest realistic listing.[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]
- Musk's satellite design targets ~150 kW of AI compute each (about one rack of Nvidia Blackwells), edge-on solar orientation with radiator cooling, an 11-million-sq-ft "Gigasat" factory, 1 GW/year orbital capacity by end-2027 (implying ~7,000 launches/year), then order-of-magnitude annual scaling toward a terawatt; SpaceX's prospectus floats a $23 trillion space data-center market.[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]
- The SpaceX IPO — the largest in history — drew ~$150B of orders for $75B of stock, with an unusually high 30% retail allocation and Friday expected as the first trading day.[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]
- Google reportedly ordered 3 million TPUs from Intel for 2028 — Intel's first major AI-era chip order — and Nvidia is testing Intel for its next-gen Feynman chips; Whittemore stresses this reflects TSMC being completely out of spare capacity, not dissatisfaction. Intel stock jumped 11% on the news.[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]
- Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan are looking at compute futures — betting on GPU rental prices like oil or wheat — as a genuine hedge against data-center overbuilding risk.[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]
- In Washington: the White House is negotiating federal preemption of state AI laws with Senator Marsha Blackburn bundling child-safety and creator protections; Adam Schiff's bill would mandate human-in-the-loop for autonomous weapons and bar AI domestic surveillance — essentially legislating Anthropic's red lines from its March Pentagon fallout; Bernie Sanders introduced his 50% AI equity tax as legislation.[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]
- OpenAI's post declares AI doing AI research "the determining factor of the pace of progress," calls for an international coordinating organization, and notably backs off full automation: "entirely automating everything is not the future we want. It would be unfulfilling and it would be dangerous."[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]
- Interpretations split: skeptics tied the post to the same-day S-1 filing ("consumers buy the dream, investors buy the TAM, regulators buy the public benefit corporation"); others read tea leaves about recursive self-improvement, noting co-author Pachocki runs automated AI R&D and the Codex team spent the weekend posting about loops.[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]
- Apple's WWDC finally shipped the Siri AI promised since 2024 (message summaries, calendar events, web search) — after a class-action settlement over unshipped Apple Intelligence features; Mark Gurman called it the right foundational reboot, while power users dismissed it as "ChatGPT 1.0 that has no impact on our work."[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]
- Whittemore observes ChatGPT's seat-based revenue looks "ridiculously inconsequential" next to Codex API usage, and wonders whether OpenAI still cares about consumer AI at all — though ChatGPT survives as top-of-funnel and as a public-markets differentiator versus Anthropic.[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]

# Takeaways

Whittemore argues we may be at a bigger fork than the companies admit: consumer AI (chatbots answering questions, ordering burritos) and work AI (managing fleets of synthetic employees) are diverging into fundamentally different technologies, and treating them as one thing may be distorting both discourse and strategy. He suggests the agentic transition and its economic consequences will ultimately matter more than the ChatGPT moment itself, and notes that if OpenAI's goal with the post was to get people talking, "mission accomplished."[^openai-declares-the-next-phase-of-ai-pnxRfJRAh5A]
