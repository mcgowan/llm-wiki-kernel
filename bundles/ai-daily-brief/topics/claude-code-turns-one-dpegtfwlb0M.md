# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore marks Claude Code's first birthday — Anthropic threw it a party — as a reminder of how fast things are changing: a year after Andrej Karpathy coined "vibe coding," when agentic coding was seen as a quirky fascination too unreliable for production, Claude Code has become the central pillar of Anthropic's strategy, generating $2.5 billion in ARR and coding its own upgrades. Creator Boris Cherny recalls Dario Amodei asking why everyone internally was using it ("all he needed to do was make it available and everyone voted with their feet") and predicts coding "will be generally solved for everyone."[^claude-code-turns-one-dpegtfwlb0M]

The rest of the headlines cover the chaos that change brings: Anthropic's new Claude Code Security plug-in triggered a cybersecurity-stock flash crash that Whittemore reads as part of a broader repricing of software, GPT-5.3 ("Garlic") rumors point to a possibly GPT-3-to-GPT-4-sized leap, and leaked OpenAI financial projections show revenue forecast at $282.5 billion by 2030 alongside doubled cash-burn estimates, soft user metrics, and firming plans for a family of screenless AI devices.[^claude-code-turns-one-dpegtfwlb0M]

# Key points

- Claude Code turned one year old; it generates $2.5 billion in ARR, and Anthropic's API analysis found almost half of all tool calls relate to software engineering — AI coding is the biggest use case for its models "and it's not close."[^claude-code-turns-one-dpegtfwlb0M]
- Cherny: "Today, coding is practically solved for me and I think it will be the case for everyone regardless of domain."[^claude-code-turns-one-dpegtfwlb0M]
- Anthropic unveiled Claude Code Security, a plug-in that scans codebases for vulnerabilities and suggests patches; the next day CrowdStrike fell 8%, Okta 9%, and Cloudflare 7%, even though (as Cloudflare tech lead Kenton Varda noted) the tool's features don't overlap with those firms' customer-facing security products.[^claude-code-turns-one-dpegtfwlb0M]
- The First Trust cybersecurity index had lost 11% over six months versus 24% for other software indices before the crash; Buccocapital argued "maybe you shouldn't pay 25x revenue when the landscape is shifting this quickly."[^claude-code-turns-one-dpegtfwlb0M]
- GPT-5.3, known internally as "Garlic" and the focus of Sam Altman's December code-red push, was rumored for imminent release; GPT-5.3 Codex already shipped, competitive with or ahead of Opus 4.6. AI engineer Dan Mac reported it surpasses the human baseline on SimpleBench at 83.7% and called it potentially "a GPT-3 to GPT-4 moment."[^claude-code-turns-one-dpegtfwlb0M]
- OpenAI's investor projections (via The Information): $282.5 billion revenue by 2030 (up 27% from prior projections), $30.1 billion expected this year, $62 billion in 2027 — but peak cash burn of $85 billion in 2028 and $665 billion total burn over five years, with profitability still targeted for 2030.[^claude-code-turns-one-dpegtfwlb0M]
- Costs to serve models quadrupled over the past year, compressing gross margins from 40% in 2024 to 33% in 2025 (versus a forecast 46%); training costs are expected to quadruple to $32 billion this year, hit $65 billion in 2027, and total $440 billion through 2030.[^claude-code-turns-one-dpegtfwlb0M]
- Weekly active ChatGPT users are at 910 million, short of the billion-user target for 2025, blamed partly on the GPT-5 release slowdown; an Epoch chart suggested Anthropic could overtake OpenAI in revenue as early as this year.[^claude-code-turns-one-dpegtfwlb0M]
- A 200-person team is working on OpenAI's device family — a $200-300 camera-equipped smart speaker first (with facial recognition for purchases), plus possibly smart glasses (2028 at earliest) and a smart lamp; none will have screens, and hardware is forecast to contribute $1.3 billion in revenue next year.[^claude-code-turns-one-dpegtfwlb0M]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's read on the cybersecurity crash is that the specific catalysts don't matter: the moves are overblown in the short term, but they're part of a broad-based repricing of software when nobody — himself or the market included — knows where value will land while the landscape shifts this quickly, so "weird moments" like mini flash crashes will keep happening until a reset point is reached. On GPT-5.3 he separates whether a model ships this week from how big a deal it is, and expects OpenAI to keep the modest version number given past naming burns. On devices, he suggests assuming "every object and device that you interact with in the real world is probably going to be tested for its capability to become an AI device."[^claude-code-turns-one-dpegtfwlb0M]
