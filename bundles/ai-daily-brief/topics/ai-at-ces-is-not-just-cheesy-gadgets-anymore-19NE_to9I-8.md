# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore argues that CES 2026 marks a clear tonal shift in the show's third consecutive AI-saturated year: where last year was small companies shoving AI into wacky gadgets, this year is the industry's biggest players — Nvidia, Amazon, AMD, Google, Samsung — using the show to roll out their definitive 2026 product lineups. Quoting Anshel Sag's "Everything is AI now, so nothing is AI," he notes AI-branded novelty no longer differentiates; the show is instead about category-defining products, and about iteration cycles speeding up so much that giants like Nvidia cannot wait for their own conferences to make announcements.[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]

The episode surveys the major announcements: Nvidia's Vera Rubin chip generation and full-stack robotics push, AMD's MI455 with OpenAI's Greg Brockman on stage, Samsung's plan to put Gemini-powered Galaxy AI on 800 million handsets, and Amazon's twin bets on the B wearable and a device-agnostic Alexa via alexa.com.[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]

# Key points

- Jensen Huang's keynote framed the entire "five-layer stack" of computing as being reinvented: software is trained not programmed, runs on GPUs not CPUs, and applications generate every pixel and token from scratch.[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]
- Vera Rubin (Vera = CPU, Rubin = GPU) is already in full production despite ongoing Blackwell deliveries: claimed 3.5x faster than Blackwell on training, 5x on inference, 8x inference compute per watt, and a 90% decrease in token cost; designed for 10-trillion-parameter models trainable in about a month using a quarter as many chips as Blackwell.[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]
- Vera Rubin adds a new external storage tier for KV cache to support agentic and long-horizon workloads, per Nvidia's Dion Harris; Futurum's Daniel Newman called Rubin "an incredible generational leap" with full availability later this year.[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]
- Nvidia's embodied-AI push — Cosmos Transfer 2.5, Cosmos Predict 2.5, Cosmos Reason 2, Isaac Groot N1.6, the Osmo ecosystem, and the Blackwell-powered Jetson T4000 — aims to make Nvidia "the Android of embodied AI" (TechCrunch), with Hugging Face compatibility and users from Boston Dynamics to Caterpillar.[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]
- AMD's MI455 GPU claims a 10x performance boost over the prior MI generation; Greg Brockman appeared with Lisa Su (OpenAI signed tens of billions in AMD chip purchases in October), saying billions of GPUs will be needed and "No one has a plan to build that scale." Su promised a 1,000x jump in four years by 2027.[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]
- Newman captured market bullishness: "Every GPU and XPU that can be built between now and the end of the decade will be sold... Bubble bears must really hate to see it."[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]
- Samsung plans to double Gemini-powered Galaxy AI handsets to 800 million in 2026 — comparable to OpenAI's ~900 million total active users — and Google has also secured the contract to power Siri's AI on iPhones later this year; Apple and Samsung together hold ~40% of global handset sales.[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]
- Amazon reintroduced the B wearable (acquired last year) with actions, daily insights, voice notes, and templates; co-founder Maria del Lourdes Zollo said users unexpectedly relied on it for personal life questions across months of conversations, email, calendar, and HealthKit data.[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]
- Amazon launched alexa.com, giving device-agnostic chatbot access to Alexa Plus; VP Daniel Rausch claimed "76% of what customers are using Alexa Plus for no other AI can do." Alexa Plus is free for 200 million Prime members atop 600 million sold Alexa devices; Bank of America reiterated its Amazon buy citing the launch.[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]
- Connor Grennan's framing: Amazon "activated a network of 600 million devices that people already talk to like a person. The behavioral shift is already done. Now, the AI just got smarter."[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]

# Takeaways

Whittemore concludes that CES 2026 shows the world's biggest companies planting flags for the year ahead across infrastructure and devices, with the AI competition getting visibly more serious. He notes Google enters 2026 in "an extremely advantageous narrative position" via its Samsung and Apple deals, which may explain why OpenAI and Meta are chasing new device categories. For consumers, he believes the shift from gimmicks to real products and experiences "is going to be hugely to our benefit."[^ai-at-ces-is-not-just-cheesy-gadgets-anymore-19NE_to9I-8]
