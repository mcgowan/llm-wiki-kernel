# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore examines a cluster of superficially unrelated announcements — OpenAI's reported plan for a desktop super app combining ChatGPT, Codex, and its browser; Google's rebuilt vibe-coding experience in AI Studio; Lovable's expansion into "General Tasks"; and Claude Code adding Telegram and Discord control — and argues they reveal a single convergence. The temptation, he says, is to read these kitchen-sink products as companies flailing, but what is actually happening is a recognition that the capability to code unlocks not just software engineering but basically everything else in knowledge work.[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]

Whittemore connects this to his earlier "Code AGI is functional AGI" thesis and to Peter Yang's line that "code is the foundation of all knowledge work": an agent that can write code can also generate apps, presentations, animations, and analysis, so codegen tools naturally grow into everything apps. He closes by noting the deeper structural shift — an era with no barriers to entry and no moats — that makes this vicious convergence and constant pivoting feel like the only operational strategy.[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]

# Key points

- Google AI Studio's vibe-coding upgrade integrates Antigravity directly, adding multiplayer, persistent builds, one-click databases, sign-in with Google, and modern web tooling (Shadcn, Framer Motion, NPM) — going "from prototypes to production apps."[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]
- Google is leaning into its multimodal edge — YouTube corpus, Genie 3 — pushing real-time multiplayer games as the flagship use case; Google also relaunched Stitch as a "vibe design partner" with an AI-native canvas and voice-driven design.[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]
- Logan Kilpatrick's AI Studio roadmap includes design mode, Figma integration, Google Workspace integration, planning mode, agents, and simplified deploys in the coming weeks.[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]
- Lovable CEO Anton Osika announced Lovable General Tasks, positioning it as "your data scientist, your business analyst, your deck builder, and your marketing assistant" — a step toward a "general-purpose co-founder"; critics like Adam Barta and Hardik Pandya called it desperation and "complete strategic dilution."[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]
- Whittemore counters the desperation reading: Lovable reported ARR jumping from $300M to $400M in a single month just a week earlier.[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]
- Replit's Agent 4 made the same move weeks earlier — infinite canvas, parallel agents, shipping "apps, sites, slides, and more"; Whittemore built his February AIDB Usage Pulse Survey as simultaneous slides and website with it.[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]
- The Wall Street Journal reported OpenAI plans a desktop super app merging ChatGPT, Codex, and its browser — a shift from standalone "side quests" under applications CEO Fidji Simo, who framed it as doubling down on Codex.[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]
- Anthropic released Claude Code "channels" (Telegram and Discord control from your phone), continuing to absorb popular OpenClaw features into Claude Code; Gokul Rajaram contrasts OpenAI's consolidation with Anthropic's extensibility strategy, though Whittemore sees both converging on the same experience.[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]
- In the show's February AI Usage Pulse Survey, 71.3% of respondents were vibe coding and 62% had use cases beyond assistant into automated or agentic AI, with diversification into data analysis and strategic planning.[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]
- Ed Sim's framing of the competitive backdrop: "When shipping new features cost near zero, every company becomes every company. And when switching costs are also near zero, who wins?"[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]

# Takeaways

Whittemore concludes that the everything-app convergence is not a lack of product vision but the natural path from coding capability to broad knowledge-work capability — though he concedes that doesn't guarantee the everything-app approach works as product strategy, citing users who valued focused tools. More broadly, he argues we are in a barely-understood new company-building paradigm: no barriers to entry combined with no moats produces a strange, viciously competitive environment where continual pivots are the only operational strategy and nothing sits still for long. For now, he says, at least there are a lot of fun new toys to play with.[^why-every-ai-product-seems-the-same-634oIgg3v5c]
