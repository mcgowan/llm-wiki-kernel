# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore opens with "one of the absolute strangest policy processes I've seen": Trump's AI executive order, pulled hours before a scheduled signing ceremony after David Sacks intervened, then quietly signed in private in substantially the same form — voluntary pre-release model sharing with the government (30 days instead of the draft's 90), NSA-led testing, a Treasury-run cybersecurity clearinghouse, and an explicit disclaimer against any licensing or pre-clearance regime. Commentators read it as a Rorschach test: safety advocates like Steve Bannon see the first bite of the elephant toward mandatory review, while critics like Dean Ball call it classified-threshold infrastructure for a future licensing regime.[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]

The main episode covers dueling enterprise-AI events from OpenAI and Microsoft against the backdrop of the shift from AI's subsidy era to its scarcity/token-shortage era. OpenAI positioned Codex — now 5 million weekly active users, growing fastest among non-technical knowledge workers — as the "factory redesign" of knowledge work, shipping annotations, role-specific plugins, and Sites. Microsoft answered with seven in-house MAI models explicitly aimed at cost: frontier tuning delivered McKinsey higher quality than GPT-5.5 at 10x lower cost.[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]

# Key points

- The executive order's catalyst was the cyber capabilities of Anthropic's Methuselah/Mythos model; Trump pulled the first draft saying "I didn't like certain aspects of it" and fearing it would hurt the US lead over China, with ex-AI czar Sacks placing an eleventh-hour call.[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]
- The signed order keeps testing voluntary (all major labs already agreed), swaps 90-day for 30-day pre-release access, provides no mechanism to block releases, and expressly forbids "mandatory government licensing, pre-clearance, or permitting"; Sacks says it covers only "meaningful step change in cyber capabilities" models like Mythos, not incremental releases like Opus 4.8.[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]
- Strange bedfellows: Bannon ("we're going to eat the elephant one bite at a time... heading towards mandatory within the next couple of months") and Bernie Sanders ("his executive order is voluntary and does almost nothing... Congress must act") both want more; CNAS's David Remler notes the order "effectively formalizes what has already been happening."[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]
- Anthropic's Project Glasswing expanded Mythos to 150 new partners across 15 countries and new sectors (energy, water, communications, healthcare, hardware), each one where "a major attack could affect more than 100 million people" — while appearing to walk back the promised general release timeline pending safeguards that "no AI developers have yet developed." Testers burn millions of dollars in tokens, subsidized by Anthropic.[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]
- SK Hynix plans to double memory-chip capacity by decade's end, treating AI demand as structural; HBM prices have more than doubled this year, and the chairman warns the shortage could last until 2030.[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]
- OpenAI's "Next Era of Knowledge Work" report: Codex hit 5M weekly active users, with non-technical knowledge workers adopting 3x faster than developers; its "strange abundance" thesis says workers produce artifacts faster than ever but lose over a quarter of the week to email and nearly a fifth to finding internal information (McKinsey).[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]
- Codex usage data: 72% of knowledge workers produce artifacts weekly; research 41%, data analysis 27%, business-function workflows 15%; ~50% of users now run parallel tasks (up from under a third in mid-April) — "the user becomes the orchestrator of work streams."[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]
- New Codex features: annotations (highlight parts of a document for the model to reason over), six role-specific plugins (sales, data analytics, creative production, product design, public-equity investing, investment banking — bundling 62 apps and 110 skills), and Sites, which turns artifacts into shareable websites and web apps.[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]
- Whittemore's Sites thesis: these are "disposable software" — building websites and throwaway web apps will become a core knowledge-work primitive alongside decks, docs, and spreadsheets, and "vibe coding" is the wrong term for it.[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]
- Uber capped employee token spending at $1,500/month — evidence cost management is the other vector of the next enterprise wave.[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]
- Microsoft Build: seven MAI models including the 1-trillion-parameter MoE MAI Thinking 1 (trained with zero synthetic data or distillation), placed in the Sonnet 4.6-Opus 4.6 range but weaker on agentic coding benchmarks; Swyx praised Suleyman for building "a full-fledged neo lab inside Microsoft in 2 years... from chip to model to harness."[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]
- Microsoft's frontier tuning pitch: custom company-specific agents "that only you control"; Nadella: "the time has come for every company to just move from consuming a frontier model to fully participating... in the frontier ecosystem."[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's synthesis: the second half of 2026 will be about "wrestling into a workable cost-effective approach all of the opportunities that the first half of 2026 unlocked." OpenAI is betting the Codex app becomes the new interface for knowledge work; Microsoft is playing a different game — not raw benchmark competition but state-of-the-art-adjacent performance at dramatically lower cost, backed by the strongest enterprise distribution anywhere, a play he says is worth taking seriously. The race for the next wave of enterprise AI adoption is fully on.[^the-next-wave-of-enterprise-ai-yTPGY13ym5s]
