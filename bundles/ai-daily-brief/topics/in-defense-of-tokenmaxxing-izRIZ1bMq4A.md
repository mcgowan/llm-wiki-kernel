# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore (NLW) wades into the raging debate over "token maxing" — the practice of companies incentivizing employees to consume as many AI tokens as possible, via leaderboards, dashboards, and performance-review credit. Press coverage from The New York Times, The Information, Business Insider, and the Financial Times has framed it as Silicon Valley's newest conspicuous consumption, culminating in reports of Amazon staff running unnecessary AI tasks to inflate usage scores. Whittemore argues the mockery is lazy thinking: critics are reviving the discredited "AI actually isn't good" and "AI is a bubble" narratives in new clothes, committing selection bias, hasty generalization, and category error by treating gamed metrics as evidence about the technology itself rather than about incentive structures.[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]

His positive case: the shift from assisted to agentic AI is a bigger disruption than the ChatGPT moment, managing agents is a genuinely new knowledge-work primitive with no experts and no best practices, and the only way to close the growing "capability overhang" is experimentation. Incentivizing token consumption — even accepting some fraud and waste — is "R&D translated to the unit level," and he would bet without question on companies that fund that experimentation over those that sit it out fearing wasted tokens. The episode's headlines cover Google's pre-I/O launch of Gemini Intelligence and the Google Book laptop, Google-SpaceX orbital data center talks, Google's forward-deployed-engineer consulting push, and Anthropic's Claude for Legal expansion.[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]

# Key points

- Kevin Roose at The New York Times reported in late March that an OpenAI engineer processed 210 billion tokens in a week (enough text to fill Wikipedia 33 times) and a single Anthropic Claude Code user racked up a bill over $150,000 in a month; Meta and Shopify managers factor AI use into performance reviews.[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]
- The Information reported Meta runs an internal leaderboard aggregating AI usage across its 85,000 employees, listing the top 250 power users with titles like "session immortal" and "token legend"; Business Insider reported Disney has an AI adoption dashboard, and Visa gives internal awards for heaviest AI use.[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]
- The Financial Times reported Amazon employees were automating non-essential tasks to inflate token-usage scores for managers — the story, together with a viral (likely joke) Slack screenshot about a $600 Anthropic bill versus a $23 Uber Eats order seen by 2 million people, crescendoed the backlash.[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]
- Whittemore concedes the obvious problem is Goodhart's law — when a measure becomes a target it ceases to be a good measure — but identifies three fallacies in the broader critique: selection bias, hasty generalization ("nut-picking"), and category error (gaming is evidence about incentives, not technology quality).[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]
- He argues the "Gary Marcusian/Ed Zitronian" AI-isn't-good and Q4-2025 bubble narratives now look silly: models have only improved, and revenue growth — Anthropic 80X-ing in the first part of the year toward tens of billions in annual revenue — is without precedent in business history, with token demand radically outstripping supply.[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]
- Of his own roughly one billion tokens used last month, "somewhere around 0%" led to direct financial gain, yet he argues the learning value — including future token efficiency — was massive; experimentation value cannot be judged by immediate quarterly-reportable returns.[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]
- Token usage is highly traceable: a company whose accountant jumps to a billion tokens in a month will ask "show us what you built," not hand out trophies — so the fraud problem is self-limiting.[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]
- More nuanced metrics are already emerging: Salesforce unveiled "agentic work units," covered by Axios as measuring output and impact rather than token consumption.[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]
- Headlines: Google announced Gemini Intelligence (agentic suite with a "personal intelligence" memory system rolling out to Google and Samsung devices), the Google Book AI-first laptop mixing Android and Chrome OS, talks with SpaceX on orbital data centers with prototypes targeted for next year, and plans to hire hundreds of forward-deployed engineers; Robinhood co-founder Baiju Bhatt unveiled orbital-data-center startup Space Cowboy Corp fundraising at $2 billion.[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]
- Anthropic launched Claude for Legal with connectors for DocuSign, Trellis, Thomson Reuters CoCounsel, and even Harvey, plus 12 pre-built practice-area agents; Anthropic says lawyers are now the most frequent Claude users of any profession except software engineers.[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]

# Takeaways

Whittemore's conclusion is emphatic: two "dreary cynical views" — that token usage without immediate financial gain is worthless, and that companies are too stupid to catch incentive exploits — are both wrong, and cynicism "may make you feel clever on X, but it does so at the cost of precluding you from participating in the world as it is." Because managing agents is a new work primitive where "there are only people who have experimented more than you," he would bet without question on the long-term success of companies that incentivize token experimentation, even at the cost of some fraud, over those that sit it out. His closing directive: "Do not, and I mean do not, be afraid of burning tokens on valuable mistakes."[^in-defense-of-tokenmaxxing-izRIZ1bMq4A]
