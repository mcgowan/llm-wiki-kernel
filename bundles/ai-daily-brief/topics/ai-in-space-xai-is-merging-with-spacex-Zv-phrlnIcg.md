# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore covers the official merger of SpaceX and xAI — the next stage of "Muskian consolidation" after xAI absorbed X/Twitter — valuing the combined entity at $1.25 trillion (SpaceX at $1 trillion, xAI at $250 billion). SpaceX framed the deal as forming "the most ambitious, vertically integrated innovation engine on and off Earth," with Musk claiming that within 2 to 3 years the lowest-cost way to generate AI compute will be in space, and that lunar manufacturing could eventually put 500 to 1,000 terawatts of AI satellites into deep-space orbit annually — versus roughly 200 gigawatts of total data center capacity on Earth today.[^ai-in-space-xai-is-merging-with-spacex-Zv-phrlnIcg]

Whittemore weighs the space-compute vision against the widespread skeptical reading — voiced by The Information's Martin Peers — that the merger is financially motivated, folding a cash-incinerating AI lab into the credibility and balance sheet of a rocket company ahead of what could be the biggest IPO in history. As always with Musk, he notes, the news functions as a Rorschach test.[^ai-in-space-xai-is-merging-with-spacex-Zv-phrlnIcg]

# Key points

- The merger values the combined entity at $1.25 trillion; xAI's $250B is roughly flat against its rumored $230B valuation from a $20 billion Series E closed earlier in the month.[^ai-in-space-xai-is-merging-with-spacex-Zv-phrlnIcg]
- SpaceX filed an FCC application to launch one million AI satellites — against ~15,000 active satellites in orbit today, a two-orders-of-magnitude density increase — with the filing calling orbital data centers a "first step towards becoming a Kardashev level two civilization." Whittemore notes SpaceX habitually opens regulator negotiations high (its original Starlink ask was 30,000 satellites; it was approved to double to 15,000 by 2031).[^ai-in-space-xai-is-merging-with-spacex-Zv-phrlnIcg]
- SpaceX's case for orbit: heat vents into space (less cooling and water use) and solar harvesting is far more efficient without atmosphere — potentially the only politically viable path if AI needs hundreds of terawatts.[^ai-in-space-xai-is-merging-with-spacex-Zv-phrlnIcg]
- The Information argued the move is financially motivated: SpaceX never explained how xAI contributes to orbital data centers, and Musk faces the same problem as other AI startups competing against ad-cash-machine giants like Google and Meta.[^ai-in-space-xai-is-merging-with-spacex-Zv-phrlnIcg]
- Financials: SpaceX reportedly generated $15B revenue and $8B EBITDA profit last year (a measure critics say flatters a depreciation-heavy rocket business); xAI lost $1.46B in the September quarter on $107M revenue and spent $7.8B over three quarters. The combined entity trades north of an 80x revenue multiple.[^ai-in-space-xai-is-merging-with-spacex-Zv-phrlnIcg]
- Skeptics: Ross Gerber ("X was out of money, merged with xAI. xAI out of money, merged with SpaceX..."); others called the 80/20 split "ridiculously bad for SpaceX shareholders" since "SpaceX doesn't need xAI. xAI does need SpaceX."[^ai-in-space-xai-is-merging-with-spacex-Zv-phrlnIcg]
- Engineer Andrew McCalip's blog post "Economics of Orbital versus Terrestrial Data Centers" concluded: "It might not be rational, but it might be physically possible."[^ai-in-space-xai-is-merging-with-spacex-Zv-phrlnIcg]
- Whittemore doubts the theory that a SpaceX IPO drains liquidity from OpenAI, arguing whichever of Anthropic or OpenAI goes public first will affect the other more than a SpaceX IPO affects either.[^ai-in-space-xai-is-merging-with-spacex-Zv-phrlnIcg]
- xAI also launched Grok Imagine 1.0 (10-second video, 720p, improved audio), which Swyx and Latent Space called a bigger story than Genie 3 on launch day — evidence Musk is not just playing financial engineering.[^ai-in-space-xai-is-merging-with-spacex-Zv-phrlnIcg]

# Takeaways

Whittemore concedes it is easy to be cynical about data centers in space and about the polarizing Musk, but poses the broader question: "Do we want all of our AI efforts to be about better short-form video and more automated ad units? Or can we still get ourselves excited about big, crazy, ambitious things?" He points to Starlink's precedent — launch costs fell 20x and turned a "pipe dream" economical — and closes by paraphrasing Arthur C. Clarke: sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from "the batshit crazy ravings of an online lunatic."[^ai-in-space-xai-is-merging-with-spacex-Zv-phrlnIcg]
