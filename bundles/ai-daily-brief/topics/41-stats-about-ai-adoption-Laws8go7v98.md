# Overview

In this Long Read Sunday / weekend big-think episode, Nathaniel Whittemore returns to a format he had largely abandoned — mining big professional-services surveys for adoption statistics — explaining that he stopped because survey stats felt disconnected from the reality of AI capability after the shift to agentic AI at the start of the year. But because the complete story of AI adoption is that "we are still very, very early," and the gap between the vanguard and everyone else is widening rather than shrinking, he compiles 41-ish stats (researched with the assistance of Fable and GPT-5.6 Soul) spanning workplace adoption, ROI, token costs, labor-market effects, public opinion, and personal AI use.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]

The picture that emerges is deeply divergent: majority adoption at the individual level but nascent bottom-line ROI at the organizational level; enormous spend gaps between median and top-1% companies; workers hiding their AI use under social stigma while simultaneously delegating 8-hour tasks to agents; and a public that both mistrusts AI companies and believes China leads the US in AI.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]

# Key points

- A mid-May Gallup poll found 52% of US workers now use AI on the job — the first time past the halfway mark — shifting the question from how much adoption to how well it's used.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]
- ROI remains murky: Domino Data Lab found 93% of enterprises reported improved production capability but 57% said AI's ROI still fails to outpace spend; PwC's mid-year CEO Snapshot saw 39% of CEOs reporting positive tangible outcomes; KPMG's Global AI Pulse found only 7% of global leaders with established ROI even as those citing meaningful business value jumped 12 points in a quarter to 76%.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]
- Token costs are biting: in an EY AI Pulse Survey, 98% of C-suite leaders said token costs were forcing them to reconsider AI plans, yet only 64% actually meter their usage.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]
- Ramp's card data from 70,000+ businesses shows the median AI-buying business spends $11.38 per employee per month versus about $7,500 a month for the top 1%; the same index showed Anthropic flipping OpenAI in business adoption — 42.4% paying for Anthropic versus 39.5% for OpenAI in July spend data — confirming a two-company race.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]
- KPMG's quarterly pulse found employee resistance to AI agents quadrupled in a single quarter, from 5% to 20%; meanwhile OpenAI reported at end of June that over 25% of Codex users have handed the agent a task estimated at more than 8 hours of human work.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]
- Stigma drives shadow use: an Atlassian controlled experiment found workers who disclosed AI use were rated 10 times lazier than identical quiet peers; PagerDuty found 66% of office professionals have used AI tools they believed violated company policy.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]
- OpenAI's Work at the Frontier report (800,000+ work messages) found 43.5% of occupation-specific ChatGPT use at work is for tasks belonging to a different occupation than the user's own; BCG's June AI at Work study found 47% of workers spending more time managing and supervising AI than doing "actual work."[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]
- DX found that across 500+ engineering organizations, more than 50% of code was AI-generated in Q2, up from 34% a quarter earlier — the shift is no longer confined to early adopters.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]
- Labor signals conflict: ZipRecruiter (June) found 38% of employers shifted entry-level data work to AI and 31% raised experience requirements, yet 35% expect AI to grow headcount; Resume Templates found 48% of hiring managers would rather invest in AI than train a new graduate; but Ramp/Revelio Labs payroll data on 21,000+ firms showed heavy AI adopters increased entry-level hiring growth 12% in the two years after adoption.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]
- Indeed Hiring Lab found 15% growth in software developer postings since February 2025 (71% of it senior roles) even as overall US postings fell 7%; AI has been the number-one stated reason for US job cuts for 5 months per Challenger, Gray & Christmas, yet the Yale Budget Lab finds zero clear AI fingerprints in aggregate US occupation data nearly three years after ChatGPT.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]
- Public opinion: only 15% of Americans trust AI companies to decide how AI is developed (Anthropic study); Pew (June) found Americans by three to one say China, not the US, is more advanced in AI; Reuters/Ipsos found 77% worry AI will make electricity more expensive and 57% would oppose a data center in their community.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]
- Other notable numbers: an ECB survey found ~50% of firms plan to train current staff for AI versus 12% hiring AI specialists; Adobe found AI-referred Prime Day shoppers converted 40% better; Axiom found 92% of 528 in-house legal leaders expect or are negotiating AI-related cuts from outside counsel; Elon University found 27% of US adult internet users have social or emotional interactions with AI, 31% of whom call it a friend, while 74% predict AI will deepen loneliness; Common Sense Media found 86% of kids 9-17 use AI and over 40% say no parent has ever discussed AI safety with them.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]

# Takeaways

Whittemore reads the data as confirmation that the world has split between those adapting to a new way of working and those laboring under the old model — and the gap is widening. He argues companies treating AI as a way to eliminate junior employees may simply be "doing it wrong," given evidence that heavy adopters hire more juniors; that layoffs blamed on AI are often politically palatable cover; and that the software-developer-extinction narrative is "not only overblown, but outright wrong." He is most emphatic about kids and AI: parents must not repeat the social-media mistake of leaving young people to navigate a transformative technology alone. His closing charge to listeners: frontier users embedded in normal, non-AI lives — not full-time AI commentators — will be the translators who shape broad public opinion.[^41-stats-about-ai-adoption-Laws8go7v98]
