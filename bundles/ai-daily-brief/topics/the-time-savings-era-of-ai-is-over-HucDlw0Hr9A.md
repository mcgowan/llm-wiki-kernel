# Overview

Nathaniel Whittemore walks through the AI DB Intel January AI usage pulse survey, a 583-respondent sample drawn entirely from show listeners. He is explicit that this is not representative of AI users generally — 97.6% use AI daily and 43% spend more than ten hours a week on it — and frames the results as a way to skate to where the puck is going rather than a picture of where AI is now.[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]

The headline finding is that time savings, which dominated his end-of-2025 ROI benchmarking survey as the universal entry point, has fallen to third place behind increased output and new capabilities. Alongside that, vibe coding has spread well beyond engineering, more than a third of respondents report agentic usage, and Claude has become the primary model of the builder-practitioner segment.[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]

# Key points

- Sample: 583 respondents, 38% from companies with 0-50 people and 27% from enterprises with 5,000 or more; 97.6% use AI daily and 43% use it more than 10 hours a week for work.[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]
- Reach versus primacy: ChatGPT had the broadest usage at 87%, with Claude and Gemini both at 80%, but Claude was the primary model for 45.8% versus ChatGPT's 31% and Gemini's 16%. Copilot was used by 39% (4% primary) and Grok by 23% (1% primary).[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]
- Claude-primary users skew heavier and more agentic: 53% use AI 10+ hours weekly versus 40% for ChatGPT-primary, 52% report agentic usage versus 24%, 87% vibe code versus 52%, and 88% report increased value versus 74%. Their number-two benefit is new capabilities; ChatGPT users' is time savings.[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]
- Momentum: 71% increased usage month over month while 83% saw value increase — a 12-point "value premium" — and 63% of those with flat usage still reported rising value.[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]
- Benefit ranking shifted: increased output and throughput first at 38%, new capabilities second at 22%, time savings third at 20%, improved decision-making 11%, improved output quality 8%. Among 10+ hour users, only 10% cited time savings versus 49% output and 27% new capabilities.[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]
- In the end-of-2025 ROI survey, 76.7% cited time savings, but respondents focused only on time savings reported lower overall ROI than those pursuing strategic benefits.[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]
- Agentic threshold: 84% assisted, 40% automated, 37.6% agentic — up from 14% agentic among the 5,000-plus use cases in the November ROI survey. Agentic users skew heavy (57% at 10+ hours) and use 3.8 models on average; 57% of C-suite respondents reported agentic use versus 32% of VPs and directors.[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]
- Coding was the single most common use case at 36% and the highest-value at 38%, with 49.5% of coders working outside engineering and IT — including 34% of executives, 13% in product, 11% in operations, and 8% in sales. 69% vibe coded in the past month and another 21% were interested.[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]
- Barriers were led by lack of time to learn, followed by a skills gap (18%), policy and approval barriers (17%), tool access (10%), and not knowing which use cases to deploy (8%). At AI-encouraging organizations, 47% spent 10+ hours weekly versus only 29% at restrictive ones.[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]
- The average respondent used 3.5 models and only 5% used a single model.[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]

# Takeaways

Whittemore argues the time-savings era is giving way to higher-order benefits, which changes how organizations should measure AI ROI — measuring only time saved is fundamentally limiting. He says the newly widespread ability to write software to solve problems in one's own work is redrawing job roles outright: "the org chart is up for grabs," with implications for hiring, organizational design, procurement, and training. He is confident agentic adoption will accelerate, particularly because leadership is ahead of the rest of the organization, meaning permission structures will follow — making agent strategy, infrastructure, governance, and data access priorities now. On the portfolio approach to models he is less sure it generalizes, since average users may not spend the money or the time to map models to use cases. He predicts these vanguard patterns will reach the broader market within six to twelve months.[^the-time-savings-era-of-ai-is-over-HucDlw0Hr9A]
