# Overview

In this main-only episode, Nathaniel Whittemore argues that faint but real signals suggest the beginning of an AI vibe shift — a new strand of discourse that doesn't assume society is "hurtling off a cliff." What makes it a trend worth watching, he says, is that it's coming from two sides at once: the chattering class around jobs and the markets. He contrasts Jasmine Sun's New York Times essay about Silicon Valley bracing for a "permanent underclass" — which he thinks over-aggrandizes AI builders' views of societal impact — with Ezra Klein's NYT opinion piece "Why the AI job apocalypse probably won't happen," significant precisely because it comes from a prominent left commentator rather than the usual accelerationist voices.[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]

On the market side, Whittemore chronicles how the bubble narrative of six months prior has flipped as the industry moved "from seats to tokens": Anthropic's revenue is exploding on the back of Claude Code, hyperscaler CapEx forecasts keep rising while demand backlogs rise faster, and even the SaaS-apocalypse story wobbled after Atlassian's blowout earnings. He closes with Sam Altman's own messaging pivot — "jobs doomerism is likely long-term wrong" — while cautioning that the shift could easily be overstated given persistently negative public polling.[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]

# Key points

- Whittemore argues AI builders may wildly overestimate AI's impact — cynically because IPOs loom, and structurally because AI excels at their own work (coding), and Silicon Valley "is full of builders," not economists or people who've worked outside startups.[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]
- Ezra Klein notes AI labs have incentives to tell the mass-unemployment story (exciting investors, unwinding a post-COVID hiring binge) and finds economists "quite skeptical that mass joblessness is on the horizon"; his key line: "Every enthusiastic AI adopter I know is working harder than ever because there is more they can do."[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]
- Klein's most striking claim: a world where AI displaces 8 million workers might be harder to handle than one displacing 80 million, because limited displacement doesn't force economic restructuring — the ~2 million jobs lost to China competition devastated particular communities and "we did very, very little to help them."[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]
- Macro data isn't matching anecdote: unemployment was 4.3% in March 2026 versus 4.4% in March 2020; Citadel Securities data shows software job postings up 18% from the May inflection point, and the Federal Reserve shows software engineering jobs at their highest since November 2023.[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]
- Anthony Pompliano publicly changed his mind on AI job losses: new-college-grad hiring up 5.6% over 12 months, unemployment for degree-holding 20-24-year-olds down from nearly 9% to about 5%; LinkedIn analysis cited by the Wall Street Journal found AI created 640,000 US jobs between 2023 and 2025.[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]
- The Jevons-paradox framing (from economist Alex Immis's "what will be scarce" essay) distinguishes elastic work that expands as costs fall (software, sales outreach, legal discovery) from capped inelastic work (payroll, month-end close, compliance filings).[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]
- Entrepreneurship data: Stripe Atlas hit 100,000 all-time incorporations with Q1 up 130% year over year; Derek Thompson's read is that "AI agents are better at creating firms than destroying jobs."[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]
- The Atlantic's "So, about that AI bubble" credits Claude Code with revenues catching up to hype; SemiAnalysis reported Anthropic's ARR "exploded from 9 billion to over 44 billion" this year — doubling roughly every 6 weeks, about $96 million of new ARR per day per analyst Ming Li, with inference margins reportedly up from 38% to 70%.[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]
- Morgan Stanley raised hyperscaler CapEx forecasts to ~$805 billion this year and $1.1 trillion next year; Mag Seven Q1 CapEx topped $400 billion while reported and projected backlog is around $1.3 trillion; David Sacks says AI CapEx adds 2.5% to GDP growth this year and over 3% next.[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]
- Atlassian's stock rose almost 30% on earnings: revenue grew 32%, and customers using its Rovo AI search grew their own ARR at twice the pace of non-users — token-efficient graph lookups in Jira/Confluence beating token-hungry RAG, challenging the SaaS-apocalypse narrative.[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]
- Sam Altman tweeted May 1 that OpenAI wants "tools to augment and elevate people, not entities to replace them" and that "jobs doomerism is likely long-term wrong"; Noah Smith called it "a huge messaging pivot" from a company whose explicit goal had been replacing human labor.[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]

# Takeaways

Whittemore is careful not to overclaim: he's turning early market signals plus one prominent essay into a theme, while the New York Times simultaneously reports AI worry as the one issue uniting Democrats and Republicans and polls remain extremely negative. His own view matches Klein's — confident in the long-term trajectory but expecting an extraordinarily painful transition in which some categories of work do get wiped out. Still, he finds it "extremely encouraging to feel the collective foot being taken off the gas of the AI doomerism," because it opens space for a conversation that is neither doom nor utopia but about adapting to and maximizing the opportunity of the change that's coming — and the more time spent there rather than in the extremes, the better.[^is-ai-doom-going-out-of-style-Bn43KmcMMB8]
